KIRK COUSINS Bombshell Decision Shocks The NFL! After CRAZY PACKERS GAME!

Vikings fans, Kirk Cousins just won at Lambeau Field. And then he stepped to the podium and said some things that tell you exactly why this reunion could work.

He talked about fighting back tears before the game. About not even knowing the door code to get into the building on Wednesday. About seeing Justin Jefferson make a play and thinking, “I remember that guy.”

And he revealed a decision about how he’s going to approach this second stint in Minnesota, one that’s different from 2022, and that could change the dynamic between him and Kevin O’Connell.

Thirty to ten. Eight sacks. Four turnovers. A season sweep of Green Bay.

And now, the man at the center of it all has spoken.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s break down everything Kirk Cousins said after one of the most emotional wins of his career.

Let’s start with how Cousins opened his press conference, because it set the tone for everything.

He didn’t make it about himself.

He called it a total team win.

And he explained why. When your defense is creating turnovers, and your special teams is creating turnovers, it puts the wind at your back as an offense. It gives you short fields.

That’s exactly what happened. Minnesota forced four turnovers. The Vikings jumped out to an early 3-0 lead after a turnover. And with short fields, Cousins didn’t have to drive 80 yards every time.

He said he felt an ability to really lean on the people around him: coaches and teammates.

And he gave the staff credit right away. He said they did a great job with the plan, and carrying out the plan as the game evolved.

That’s not the typical “I did it” press conference. That’s a veteran who knows he walked into a good situation and wants everyone to know it.

Then he went straight to the playmakers.

He called Myles Price’s touchdown a big-time play.

Remember the moment. Third-and-23. The Vikings up 3-0. The kind of situation where this offense had been hopeless all season.

Cousins hit Price on a short throw, and Price turned it into a 38-yard touchdown.

And Cousins pointed to exactly what made it special. Price didn’t just come down with the catch. He kept his body control and his balance, and turned it into six points.

Then he did the same for Gavin Bartholomew.

On the fourth-quarter drive that sealed the game, Cousins found Bartholomew over the middle from 20 yards out. Bartholomew broke a tackle and scored.

And Cousins said something that shows how he thinks about offense.

He said it makes a big difference when players do that. And he compared it to Justin Jefferson.

He said that was always the deal with Justin. Justin would find his way into the end zone, instead of making the offense trot out its goal-line or red-zone package.

Think about what that means. Minnesota’s red zone has been a problem all season. The Vikings rank dead last in the NFL in rushing touchdowns. They’ve been settling for Will Reichard field goals.

But when your receivers break tackles and score from 20 and 38 yards out, you never have to get into the red zone at all. That’s how you beat a red-zone problem.

Cousins also praised the running backs. He said they ran hard and played smart. Really smart.

Aaron Jones scored a touchdown at the stadium where he started his career. Jordan Mason ran out the clock at the end with a series of hard runs.

And he gave one more shoutout: to Kevin O’Connell and the staff, for getting the plays in quickly and communicating what needed to happen.

At Lambeau Field, one of the loudest places to play in football, getting the play in fast matters. It gives the quarterback time to get to the line, read the defense, and adjust.

Then he was asked about the hardest part of the week. And this answer was incredible.

A reporter asked how difficult it was to come in on a Wednesday, especially on a double-pump play during the game.

Cousins said that specific play wasn’t too bad, because the receiver was open.

But the process? That was difficult.

He said they got in late Tuesday night. And he didn’t even know what his door code was to get into the building on Wednesday.

Think about that.

The starting quarterback for Sunday’s division game, at Lambeau, didn’t know the code to open the door of the facility on Wednesday morning.

That’s how fast this happened.

He said there were a lot of pieces to work through and a lot of questions that needed answers.

And he gave credit to everyone who helped. Starting with Kevin O’Connell. Then offensive coordinator Wes Phillips. Then Josh and Chris O’Hara, and the rest of the staff. And the quarterback room.

He said the guys came to his aid and answered so many questions for him. In his words, it took a village.

That’s a big detail. Remember, Kyler Murray was demoted to backup this week. Carson Wentz is likely QB3. And yet the quarterback room helped Cousins get ready. That says something about the culture in that building.

Then a reporter asked how much he knew about the two players who caught his touchdowns, Myles Price and Gavin Bartholomew.

And Cousins was honest. Very little.

He said he’s still learning names. He joked that he probably knows their nicknames better than their real names.

But here’s the part that matters. He said he was told early in the week that he might not know much about Myles Price, because Price isn’t getting the Justin Jefferson-level attention publicly.

But the feedback was clear. This guy is the real deal. Trust him.

And Cousins said it helps when you get that kind of feedback from teammates.

So on third-and-23, he trusted Myles Price. And Price delivered.

That’s the kind of trust that usually takes months to build. Cousins built it in four days, because his teammates vouched for each other.

Now let’s talk about the moment the game could have turned.

A reporter pointed out that even with a 10-point lead, it felt like the momentum was starting to shift. And asked how important that fourth-quarter touchdown drive was.

Cousins agreed. He said that early in the second half, the momentum started to change.

The Vikings had a three-and-out. He thought they might have had another one near the end of the first half too.

And remember what happened. Green Bay scored a third-quarter touchdown to cut the lead to 20-7. Then a field goal made it 20-10.

That’s the moment where this team had been collapsing all season. The lead shrinking. The road crowd waking up. The offense stalling.

Cousins said they needed to put something together. And he credited O’Connell’s play-calling for putting together a good mix of run and pass to get them down the field.

And what happened? Cousins went 8 for 8 on an 11-play, 75-yard touchdown drive.

Eight for eight.

Then came one of my favorite moments of the press conference.

A reporter asked about one specific play on that drive. About 11 minutes left. Jefferson lined up on the right twice. Then third-and-2, and Cousins went into a hurry-up, walking up and down the line.

What was he saying?

Cousins explained that it was a great design by the coaches. But Lambeau is a tough place to play. And from a communication standpoint, he didn’t feel he could get the call across unless he walked up and down the line himself, because there was so much noise.

Then Jefferson did a great job, and the guys blocking for him did a great job, springing him for 11 yards and a first down.

And then Cousins said the line every Vikings fan has been waiting to hear.

He said it was fun. It felt like old times.

And watching Justin do what he did on that play, he said, it’s like, “I remember that guy.” He’s pretty special.

I remember that guy.

Think about everything Jefferson has been through. One 100-yard game in more than a year. Five catches for 33 yards in Detroit. Three catches on 11 targets against Buffalo. A bubble screen thrown so high he couldn’t reach it. Analysts suggesting Minnesota should trade him. Stephen A. Smith calling the team’s handling of him an utter disgrace.

And then, in his first game back with Kirk Cousins, on a third-and-2 at Lambeau, Jefferson made a play that reminded his old quarterback exactly who he is.

That’s the connection Harry Douglas said would click right away. It clicked.

Then the conversation got personal.

A reporter asked whether, three years after leaving, part of him wanted to come back and make things right. Course-correct. Finish things the right way.

Cousins said there’s so much football ahead. But he admitted it’s gratifying to come back to Lambeau with the purple on.

He said coming back to Lambeau with another team isn’t quite the same. But to come back with the purple on, at a similar time of year, three years later, is a blessing. To get a win like this one.

Remember, he tore his Achilles at Lambeau in October 2023. In a win. And he walked off that field not knowing if he’d ever wear purple again.

Now he walked off a winner, in purple, at the same stadium.

But then he said something that tells you he isn’t satisfied.

He said there’s so much football ahead, and this story is going to be written much later than now.

Asked where this ranks among his regular-season wins, he said it’s tough to rank. He’s been through some things. But it’s certainly up there.

Then a reporter mentioned that several teammates had described him as a calming influence. And asked whether he was calm coming in, with only four days to prepare.

Cousins said he’d call himself steady. He joked that his usual self probably isn’t super calm.

But he turned the compliment around. He said his teammates were a calming influence for him. Putting his trust in them, and in the staff.

And then he said the defense, the way it’s playing and played today, is always going to be a calming influence.

That’s the key. When you know your defense is going to sack the quarterback eight times and force four turnovers, you don’t have to be perfect. You can just play.

He also admitted it was a little emotional coming in, and that he was grateful for how it turned out.

And speaking of that defense, Cousins was asked about watching it take the ball away again and again.

He called it unbelievable.

And he offered a unique perspective. He’s played against this defense. He reminded reporters that when he was with Atlanta, the Falcons came to Minnesota and played the Vikings.

And he practiced against it back in 2023, when Brian Flores was first installing it.

Cousins said it’s different watching it turn the ball over like that. He called it a different brand of football when you have a defense doing that.

A different brand of football. That’s exactly what this Vikings team is when the offense does its part.

Then the most emotional question of all.

Did any of the emotions surprise him, walking in or walking off the field?

Cousins said football is an emotional game. His football journey goes way back. There have been highs and lows. And there’s more of the story to write.

And then he said this: the last three years since leaving Minnesota have been a tough three years.

Think about that journey. The Achilles tear. Leaving Minnesota. Signing a massive deal in Atlanta. Getting benched for Michael Penix. Getting released. Signing in Las Vegas as a bridge to a rookie. And then being traded at the deadline.

Three tough years.

And now he said he has a lot of gratitude for being with this group.

He was asked whether he fought those emotions coming in.

And his answer was perfect. He said he doesn’t think it’s good to be crying in front of your teammates pregame. So he fought it back. But he said you can channel it.

He channeled it into 21 of 31, 207 yards, two touchdowns, and a win at Lambeau.

And now we get to the bombshell. The decision Cousins revealed about how he plans to approach this second stint.

A reporter mentioned that teammates had described him as vocal in meetings, and asked what the intent was behind that.

Cousins explained one of the biggest challenges of joining a new team.

He said he’s changed offensive coordinators 11 times. Play-callers 11 times.

Eleven different play-callers over his career.

So he said there’s always a dance. When does he speak up and say, “I’m not comfortable with that, we need to do it this way”? And when does that become tone-deaf, being uncoachable, not listening to the coach?

He said that’s a balance he’s always tried to find with coaches, and he was trying to find it this week. There were a couple of times when he said, “You know what, I’d be more comfortable doing this.” So he picked his spots.

And then he said it.

Maybe even more than in 2022, he’s going to really trust Kevin. He’s going to say, “What do you want to do, coach?” And then, in his words, he’ll shut up and just go play.

That’s the decision.

Kirk Cousins, a 38-year-old veteran who has seen 11 play-callers, who has every right to want things his way, is choosing to trust Kevin O’Connell more than ever.

Think about why that matters.

All season long, there have been questions about O’Connell. Dominique Foxworth said he should be fired if the season ends in disappointment. Jason McIntyre put his chances of being fired at 20 to 25 percent. People questioned whether his offense could adapt.

And now his quarterback is saying publicly: I trust this coach, I’m going to run his offense, and I’m going to execute.

That’s a quarterback putting his faith in his head coach, and a head coach getting back the one quarterback who knows exactly how to run his system.

And it showed on the sideline. A reporter asked what it meant to have O’Connell grabbing his helmet after touchdowns, after everything they’ve been through together.

Cousins said they’re just building. There’s so much more football left, and so much more to make happen.

But he said O’Connell is in his corner. And so are the guys. And he’s in their corner.

That’s a team.

So let’s put it all together.

Kirk Cousins called it a total team win, crediting the defense and special teams for creating turnovers and short fields.

He praised Myles Price for turning third-and-23 into a touchdown, and Gavin Bartholomew for breaking a tackle and scoring.

He revealed he didn’t even know the door code to the building on Wednesday, and that it took a village to get him ready.

He said he barely knew Price and Bartholomew, but teammates told him to trust Price because he’s the real deal.

He went up and down the line at Lambeau to communicate over the noise, then watched Jefferson make a play and thought, “I remember that guy.”

He fought back tears before the game, called the last three years tough, and said there’s more of the story to write.

And he made a decision: more than in 2022, he’s going to trust Kevin O’Connell, ask what the coach wants, and just go play.

The Vikings are 7-2. They’ve swept the Packers. And next up is Sunday Night Football against San Francisco.
