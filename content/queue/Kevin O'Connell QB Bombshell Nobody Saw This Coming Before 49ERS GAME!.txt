Kevin O’Connell’s QB Update Before the 49ers Game! Plus Huge Injury News!

Vikings fans, Kevin O’Connell just gave us his final quarterback update before Sunday night. And it tells us a lot about where this team stands heading into the biggest game of the season.

But that’s not all.

Two key starters are coming back. One on offense who could be a nightmare for San Francisco. One on defense who’s the brain of the No. 1 defense in football.

Andrew Van Ginkel had everyone holding their breath this week, and we finally have an answer.

The only problem? The Vikings might be without one of their starting cornerbacks against Brock Purdy.

And then there’s the matchup itself. Kyle Shanahan’s offense against Brian Flores’s defense. No. 1 against No. 1. And there’s a stat about Flores against Shanahan that you won’t believe.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s get into it.

The Vikings are heading down to Mexico City today, Saturday, ahead of Sunday night’s showdown with the San Francisco 49ers.

They wrapped up their final practice of the week on Friday. And overall, the injury news was pretty positive.

Only two players have been ruled out. Jordan Addison and Kyler Murray.

Neither one is a surprise. But let’s start with the quarterback situation, because that’s where O’Connell’s comments stood out.

Kyler Murray still hasn’t practiced as he works through his hamstring strain.

O’Connell said Murray is getting close to returning to practice. But the team is being cautious. They don’t want to make anything worse.

And then O’Connell said something that’s worth paying attention to.

He said the team feels good about its quarterback room behind Kirk Cousins, with Carson Wentz and Max Brosmer.

Think about that for a second.

Remember back at the trade deadline, when reports said Max Brosmer might be waived once Cousins arrived? When it looked like Minnesota would have to make a tough roster decision with four quarterbacks?

Well, Brosmer is still here. And O’Connell is publicly saying he feels good about him.

And remember, after the Cousins trade, O’Connell said Murray would be the No. 2 quarterback when healthy. So the quarterback picture is now very clear.

Kirk Cousins is the starter. No debate.

Murray is still recovering, and the team isn’t rushing him.

And Wentz and Brosmer are the backups for Sunday night.

For Vikings fans who lived through the Wentz start against Buffalo, that’s a reminder of just how important Kirk Cousins’s health is right now. This offense finally found its rhythm at Lambeau. Keeping Cousins upright against the 49ers’ pass rush is priority No. 1.

Now let’s talk about Jordan Addison.

He’s likely to miss two to three games with the thumb injury. But he avoided injured reserve, which is a great sign. It means the Vikings believe he can come back before the four-game IR minimum.

Still, he’s going to miss time. And that means more work for the young guys, Myles Price and Tai Felton.

And after what Price did at Lambeau, with four catches, 49 yards, and that 38-yard touchdown on third-and-23, and with O’Connell saying the team wants to keep finding ways to get him involved, this could be a big opportunity.

And then there’s good news. Jauan Jennings is back.

He practiced in full all three days this week after missing the last two games with a calf injury.

And O’Connell said he expects Jennings to make an impact on Sunday, against his former team. Especially with Addison sitting out.

That could mean physicality in the run game on early downs. Or it could finally mean Jennings getting targeted and catching some passes.

And remember the storyline here. Jennings refused a trade back to San Francisco at the deadline. Now he gets to face them in primetime. You know he wants this one.

Hopefully he and Kirk can start building some rapport.

But the player who might make the biggest difference in the passing game this week?

T.J. Hockenson.

Hockenson was dealing with chest and wrist injuries last week. He didn’t play against the Packers.

But he returned to practice this week and ramped up to being a full participant on Friday.

That means he has no game designation. He’s good to go.

And this is a big deal, for one simple reason. Think back to the 2022 and 2023 seasons, when Hockenson played with Kirk Cousins, before Hockenson tore his ACL.

Those two had a really strong connection.

In the 18 games they played together, Hockenson averaged more than six catches and 55 yards per game.

And here’s the fun part. Fifty-five yards is exactly what the 49ers have been allowing to tight ends per game this season.

So if Hockenson and Cousins pick up where they left off, he could be a huge factor on Sunday night.

Think about it. At Lambeau, Cousins won without Hockenson. He found Jefferson nine times. He found Price for a touchdown. He found Gavin Bartholomew for a touchdown.

Now he gets back his favorite tight end, the one he already has years of chemistry with. This offense keeps getting more weapons back.

Now let’s go to the defense. Because there’s some really big news here too.

Andrew Van Ginkel is completely off the injury report.

He left late in the Packers game with a quad injury, and he was limited on Wednesday and Thursday. That had Vikings fans worried.

But on Friday, he was a full participant. He’s good to go.

And that’s massive for this defense. Because if you’ve been following the Vikings this season, you know Van Ginkel is playing like one of the best edge rushers in football.

Through nine games, he has eight and a half sacks. Eleven tackles for loss. Two forced fumbles. Two fumble recoveries.

And what makes him so special isn’t just the pass rush. It’s his versatility.

He can get after the quarterback. He has 32 pressures to go with those sacks.

But he’s also solid against the run. Twenty-one run stops in nine games, the third-most among edge rushers.

But here’s the stat that tells you everything.

Van Ginkel is tied for the second-most sacks in the NFL, while also having the most coverage snaps of any edge rusher in the league.

Read that again. He’s one of the best sack artists in football, and he also drops into coverage more than any edge rusher in the league.

That’s absurd. And it’s a huge part of what makes this Vikings defense so unpredictable and hard to crack. You never know if Van Ginkel is rushing or dropping.

And remember, just this week, Alex Boone questioned whether anyone should be intimidated by Van Ginkel. Well, tied for second in sacks, sixth in get-off, and the most coverage snaps of any edge rusher. Those numbers answer that question.

Blake Cashman is another one of those versatile players.

And after missing last week in concussion protocol, he’s officially cleared. He’s returning to the lineup on Sunday night.

He’ll resume his duties as the starting middle linebacker, with the green-dot responsibilities. He’s the one calling the plays on the field.

And just this week, Brad Gagnon of Bleacher Report named Cashman the Vikings’ midseason MVP.

Remember, the Vikings dominated Green Bay without him, with Ivan Pace Jr. filling in. Now they get their defensive MVP back.

And his return matters even more because, as we’ll get to in a minute, the 49ers have a vulnerable spot along their offensive line, one that both inside linebackers, Cashman and Eric Wilson, can help attack.

And on top of Cashman, Tyrion Ingram-Dawkins is back this week too, after missing the Packers game with an ankle injury.

Now here’s an interesting strategy note from Brian Flores.

Flores said earlier in the week that because this game is being played at such high altitude in Mexico City, the plan is to rotate as many players in and out as possible.

Fresh legs. That’s the strategy.

And with Ingram-Dawkins coming back, it wouldn’t be surprising to see the Vikings activate all six defensive tackles on the 53-man roster.

That’s Jalen Redmond, Levi Drake Rodriguez, Tyrion Ingram-Dawkins, Caleb Banks, Dominique Orange, and Elijah Williams.

The goal is to keep the big guys up front as fresh as possible in the thin air.

Redmond, Rodriguez, and Ingram-Dawkins have been the three starters all season, with the rookies mixing in.

But over the past two weeks, Elijah Williams, a former undrafted free agent, has been getting more and more snaps.

He played 32 snaps in Week 9 and 21 snaps in Week 10.

And with stopping the run being so crucial against the 49ers, he could play a significant role on Sunday.

He may even have passed Caleb Banks and Dominique Orange on the depth chart for now. Banks was a healthy scratch against the Bills and only played 17 snaps against the Packers. Orange played just seven snaps last week.

So keep an eye on that rotation on Sunday night. With the altitude, depth up front could decide the fourth quarter.

Now, the one concern.

Only two players are heading into Sunday night as questionable, and both are in the secondary.

Safety Jay Ward has been limited all week with a knee issue.

And cornerback Isaiah Rodgers has been limited the past two days with a quad injury.

O’Connell said Rodgers was feeling good on Friday, but the team will monitor him over the next couple of days and see where he is on Sunday.

And Rodgers has been extremely steady for the Vikings this season.

He has the ninth-lowest passer rating allowed when targeted among all cornerbacks. And his 6.7 yards per catch allowed are the fourth-fewest among corners.

Now, it definitely helps having the best pass rush in the NFL in front of you. But Rodgers, Byron Murphy Jr., and James Pierre have all been really solid at corner this season.

If Rodgers can’t go, a rookie or Zemaiah Vaughn could mix in for some snaps.

Or the Vikings could just use more safety-heavy looks, which they do a lot anyway. Harrison Smith, Josh Metellus, Theo Jackson, and rookie Jakobe Thomas.

And speaking of Jakobe Thomas, he made his NFL debut last week and forced a fumble on special teams. Maybe at some point we see him on defense. But throwing him into his first defensive snaps on Sunday night, against the 49ers? That would be a big ask.

Either way, the Vikings secondary is going to have its hands full.

Because the 49ers’ receiving corps is a bit of a veteran crew, but they find a way to be productive every single week.

George Kittle, coming off an Achilles tear, leads them in catches, receiving yards, and receiving touchdowns.

Mike Evans probably would be leading them in all those categories, but he’s missed the last three games with an injury. And he could be returning this week. He was a limited participant in practice every day.

Kyle Shanahan said Friday that Evans was feeling a little sore, but overall he’s trending in the right direction. That would be a big boost for San Francisco.

Then there’s Deebo Samuel, who’s made a nice impact this season. And the 49ers traded for Keenan Allen before the deadline.

Their second-year receiver Jordan Watkins has put together three solid games in a row with Evans out.

And they’ve done all this even with Ricky Pearsall out for the year, their first-round pick on injured reserve, and free-agent signing Christian Kirk on IR as well.

But the key is Brock Purdy. He’s playing at an elite level, and he looks extremely comfortable and confident in Shanahan’s system.

And Shanahan has been one of the best play-callers in the NFL for a long time.

This could be the Vikings defense’s biggest challenge of the season.

So let’s break down the matchup everyone wants to see. Kyle Shanahan’s 49ers offense against Brian Flores’s Vikings defense.

It’s No. 1 against No. 1.

The 49ers rank first in the NFL in success rate, EPA per play, and yards per game.

The Vikings rank first in success rate allowed and points per game allowed, and second in EPA per play allowed.

You can’t ask for a better matchup than that.

San Francisco is coming off a win over the Cowboys where they put up 46 points and nearly 600 yards of offense.

Now, to be fair, the Cowboys’ defense isn’t great. But the 49ers have scored 30 or more points five times this season.

And Brock Purdy has been playing like an MVP candidate. More than 2,300 passing yards. Twenty passing touchdowns. And the second-best QBR in the NFL.

Meanwhile, the Vikings are coming off a win over the Packers where the defense held them to 10 points and forced four turnovers.

And that could be the key to this game.

The Vikings have the third-most takeaways in the league. The 49ers have turned it over 11 times, slightly above league average.

And here’s the interesting part. In the 49ers’ only loss this season, against the Falcons, they turned it over four times. That was the biggest reason they lost.

So the formula for beating San Francisco is clear. Force turnovers.

For that to happen, the pass rush has to get home. And it has all season.

We all know Flores blitzes more than any team in the NFL. The Vikings generate more pressure than anyone, and they lead the league in sacks.

Andrew Van Ginkel and Dallas Turner are both in the top five in sacks.

But they’ll have their work cut out for them in this one.

Trent Williams is one of the best left tackles of all time. He’s only given up one sack this entire season.

And on the right side, Colton McKivitz has been excellent too. He hasn’t allowed a single sack.

As a team, the 49ers are allowing the sixth-fewest pressures on dropbacks.

But there’s one area where they’re vulnerable. The interior. Specifically, left guard.

Connor Colby, a seventh-round pick from last season, has given up 28 pressures and eight quarterback hits. Both are the most among all guards in the NFL.

The most among all guards.

That could mean a big game for Jalen Redmond. And it’s something Flores will attack, especially with his blitzing inside linebackers. That’s Blake Cashman and Eric Wilson.

This is exactly why Cashman’s return matters so much. Flores loves sending linebackers through the A-gaps. And if Colby is the weak spot, expect Cashman and Wilson to come screaming through the middle.

So how will the 49ers fight the pass rush? By running the ball. A lot.

That’s what they always do. They have the fourth-highest run play rate in the NFL, and the third-highest yards per carry.

And that’s the one area where the Vikings have been vulnerable at times. They’re 19th in yards per carry allowed.

Christian McCaffrey is one of the most dynamic backs in the league. More than 900 total yards and 13 combined touchdowns in nine games.

And their rookie running back has been a solid partner for him.

This team can beat you in so many different ways.

But here’s the thing. So can the Vikings defense.

And here’s the stat that should give every Vikings fan confidence.

Brian Flores has had a lot of success against Kyle Shanahan.

As play-callers, they’ve faced each other three times. Two of those games came with Flores in Minnesota.

And in all three games, Flores is 3-0 against Shanahan.

His defense forced at least two turnovers in every one of those games. And it held the 49ers to exactly 17 points in all three.

Three games. Three wins. Exactly 17 points each time.

That’s not a coincidence. That’s a coordinator who knows how to slow down Kyle Shanahan.

Now, nobody is guaranteeing that happens again. But it wouldn’t be shocking.

Look at what this defense has done the last three weeks.

The Lions and the Bills are two of the top-scoring offenses in the league. The Vikings held them to 19 and 23 points, while the Vikings offense gave the defense almost no help in either game, especially against Buffalo.

And last week, they held the Packers to 10 points, with eight sacks and four takeaways.

So we’ve seen this defense shut down some of the best offenses in football.

And now? They get Cashman back. They get Ingram-Dawkins back. Van Ginkel is healthy. And the offense finally has a quarterback who can put points on the board.

So let’s put it all together.

Kevin O’Connell confirmed Kyler Murray is out but getting close, and said the team feels good about Carson Wentz and Max Brosmer behind Kirk Cousins.

Jordan Addison is out, but he avoided IR. Jauan Jennings is back against his former team.

T.J. Hockenson is back with no designation, and he averaged 55 yards a game with Cousins, exactly what the 49ers allow to tight ends.

Andrew Van Ginkel is off the injury report. Blake Cashman cleared protocol. Tyrion Ingram-Dawkins is back.

Isaiah Rodgers and Jay Ward are questionable.

Flores plans to rotate heavily because of the altitude.

The 49ers’ weak spot is left guard Connor Colby, who has allowed the most pressures among guards in the NFL.

And Brian Flores is 3-0 against Kyle Shanahan, forcing at least two turnovers and holding the 49ers to exactly 17 points every time.

The Vikings are three-point underdogs. But this team has proven people wrong all season.
