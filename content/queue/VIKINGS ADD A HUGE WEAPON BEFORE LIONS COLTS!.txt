Vikings fans, the roster changes have already started.

Two tight ends have joined Minnesota. A trade proposal puts a three-time Pro Bowl guard on Minnesota’s radar to give this offense some badly needed help. With the trade deadline getting closer, three positions stand out as clear priorities.

And while the front office looks outside the building, one of Minnesota’s own just earned a major honor, and his next contract is becoming an even bigger talking point.

The Detroit Lions are next. The November 10th deadline is less than two weeks away. And there’s a lot to get through.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s break it all down.

Let’s start with the moves Minnesota has already made, because the Vikings’ latest additions address a position that has taken a serious injury hit.

According to multiple media reports, the Vikings signed tight ends Devin Culp and Bryson Nesbit to their practice squad on Wednesday, bringing in two developmental options as they keep looking for roster reinforcements.

Let’s be clear about what this is. These are practice squad signings, so neither player should be confused with an immediate addition to the active roster.

But there’s a clear reason to build depth here.

Josh Oliver tore his biceps in the fourth quarter against Tampa Bay. He had surgery and landed on injured reserve on September 29th. He’s expected to miss the rest of the season.

That’s a big loss. Oliver was the run-blocking specialist in that tight end room, the guy who did the dirty work at the line of scrimmage. Without him, Minnesota is missing an established contributor at a position that’s become more and more involved in this offense.

So who are the new guys?

Devin Culp entered the NFL as Tampa Bay’s seventh-round pick in 2024, after playing college football at Washington, where he was part of the Huskies’ offense. He’s spent time with both the Buccaneers and the Browns, and Minnesota is now giving him another chance to develop.

Bryson Nesbit already knows the organization. He originally signed with the Vikings as an undrafted free agent in April 2025. He spent most of that time on the practice squad, then moved through other practice squads and reserve lists. Now he’s back, and his return gives the coaching staff a familiar player to evaluate alongside Culp.

Neither signing changes who leads the group.

T.J. Hockenson is the clear top receiving threat at tight end, with 27 catches for 237 yards and two touchdowns on 32 targets. And remember what we talked about earlier this week: the growing trust between Hockenson and Kyler Murray, the way they’re reading zone coverage together and finding the soft spots. That connection is one of the most encouraging things about this offense right now.

Behind him, Gavin Bartholomew has seven catches for 54 yards, and Ben Yurosek is another option in the room.

As a unit, the tight ends have accounted for 342 receiving yards and two touchdowns.

But here’s the bigger picture. An undefeated team facing a tough remaining schedule can’t assume its current personnel will stay healthy through a playoff push. Injuries happen. Depth matters. And Culp and Nesbit now have a chance to show whether they can be useful insurance as the season goes on.

But the bigger offensive question sits right in front of Kyler Murray, and it’s the offensive line.

Here’s a stat that sums up this season perfectly. Minnesota is the NFL’s only undefeated team. But it has scored just 10 touchdowns.

Ten touchdowns in six games. That’s the lowest total for a team starting 6-0 since the 1934 Detroit Lions.

Nineteen thirty-four. And the Vikings’ next opponent? The Detroit Lions. You can’t make this stuff up.

Will Reichard has supplied a big share of the scoring, setting a franchise record along the way. But you can’t win playoff games on field goals alone.

And the 16-10 win over Indianapolis showed the same problems again. Protection breakdowns. Stalled drives. Too few explosive plays. And not enough success finishing in the red zone.

That puts general manager Nolan Teasley’s approach to the offensive line under the microscope.

Look at the interior.

Donovan Jackson, Minnesota’s first-round pick in 2025, is out for the season with a torn meniscus. That’s a major loss for a young player the team was counting on.

Will Fries hasn’t consistently delivered the performance you’d expect from his big free-agent contract, after dealing with a leg fracture in 2024.

Blake Brandel and Joe Huber have helped in stretches. But the interior of this line is still unsettled for a team with real postseason ambitions.

So what’s the answer?

One proposed solution is a big one: Philadelphia Eagles left guard Landon Dickerson.

Dickerson is 28, an Alabama product, and a three-time Pro Bowler. He recently returned from injured reserve after a bone bruise in his knee, and he played every offensive snap against Dallas on Monday night.

So he’s healthy, he’s playing, and he’s one of the best guards in football.

And Philadelphia’s changing situation has fueled a lot of discussion about his future.

Look at what’s happened with that offensive line. The Eagles traded center Cam Jurgens to Baltimore. Lane Johnson retired. And Dickerson’s contract was reworked into a three-year structure worth $58.5 million, with fewer short-term guarantees.

That’s a lot of change for one of the best offensive lines of recent years.

Dickerson’s own comments have also created some uncertainty about his role.

ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler said something feels off in Philadelphia, and he put Dickerson’s chances of being traded at 25 percent. Fowler named Green Bay and the Chargers as possible fits.

Minnesota is another logical destination because of its need. But to be clear, that doesn’t mean any negotiations are underway.

Then there’s the money, and this part is interesting.

The financial argument is that Philadelphia has already paid significant option bonuses, which would leave an acquiring team mainly responsible for the remaining base salary. The analysis estimates that at roughly $7 million to $9 million for this season.

Now picture it. Christian Darrisaw at left tackle. Landon Dickerson right next to him at left guard.

That’s an elite left side. Dickerson could strengthen both pass protection and run blocking, and give Kyler Murray a much more stable pocket.

Remember all the talk about the offense stalling? About Murray maybe being too cautious? Part of that could come from not trusting the protection. Give him a cleaner pocket, and you might see a very different quarterback.

But his recent injury, his age, and the acquisition cost would all still need careful thought, even if Minnesota saw him as a short-term solution.

And there are less prominent alternatives too.

Tampa Bay’s Ben Bredeson has been linked to Minnesota in multiple reports.

He’s also 28. He came into the league as Baltimore’s fourth-round pick in 2020 and has mostly played left guard, with some flexibility elsewhere on the interior.

His contract is affordable and runs through 2027. The suggested cost is a possible day-three draft pick swap.

He could provide starting help or dependable depth.

And there’s a family angle here. Bredeson would be joining his younger brother, Vikings fullback Max Bredeson. Brothers in purple.

Tampa Bay’s early struggles could make a veteran trade possible, though that’s still uncertain.

New Orleans guard Cesar Ruiz is another candidate.

ESPN has given him a 25 percent chance of being traded. The 27-year-old has posted good pass block win rates and solid Pro Football Focus pass-blocking grades in recent seasons.

The Saints, at around 2-5, are evaluating their veterans. But Ruiz’s multi-year contract creates possible dead-money complications. And his usual position is right guard, where Minnesota already has Fries.

Now here’s an important update. Remember Quenton Nelson, the Colts guard we talked about so much earlier this month? He’s no longer available.

Indianapolis traded Nelson to Seattle for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-round pick.

That’s one option off the board, and one NFC contender just got stronger.

Arizona’s Hjalte Froholdt has also come up in discussion, though his recent extension complicates that.

Philadelphia’s depth players are other possibilities. And around the league, the competition is moving. San Francisco and Seattle are adding pieces. Green Bay and Chicago keep showing up in deadline speculation.

So Teasley and Kevin O’Connell face a practical choice.

Go after a proven Pro Bowler like Dickerson. Or find a more affordable veteran who improves protection, supports the running game, and gives Justin Jefferson and T.J. Hockenson more time to get open.

But guard is only one part of Minnesota’s deadline discussion.

A separate ranking of the positions the Vikings could address before November 10th puts running back first, guard second, and the defensive line third.

Let’s go through each one, starting with running back.

The case for running back isn’t about dismissing Aaron Jones or Jordan Mason. It’s about creating a different kind of threat.

Here’s the issue. Both Jones and Mason are averaging 4.2 yards per carry. Solid, but neither has consistently delivered the explosive runs this offense needs.

And here’s the stat that tells the story. Kyler Murray owns Minnesota’s longest rush of the season, at 35 yards.

The quarterback. Not a running back. That’s how few explosive runs this team has gotten from the backfield.

And Jones is still dealing with that nagging knee problem, with the MRI we talked about earlier this week.

Then there’s what defenses are doing. Opponents are using two-high safety looks against the Vikings more than half the time. And the analysis argues that Minnesota’s running game hasn’t done enough to force defenses out of those structures.

Here’s why that matters. When defenses keep two safeties deep, they’re taking away the big passing plays to Jefferson and Addison. The way to punish that is to run the ball effectively and force a safety to come down into the box. Once he does, the passing game opens up.

But if your running game can’t threaten them, defenses can just keep sitting back.

A back who can turn a small opening into a long gain could change that math, and create better opportunities for Murray and his receivers.

Jonathan Taylor and Saquon Barkley are the obvious blockbuster options. Remember, Taylor ran for 95 yards and a touchdown against this same Vikings defense on Sunday.

But a complementary runner with breakaway ability could be a more affordable way to add that dimension.

Next, guard.

The ranking notes Minnesota’s reported interest in Quenton Nelson before he was moved to Seattle.

With Jackson’s season-ending injury, undrafted rookie Joe Huber has been pushed into the left guard conversation, while Fries’s uneven play leaves questions on the other side.

That opens up several possible approaches. A short-term replacement. More competition. Or a player who could eventually take over at right guard if Minnesota decided to move on from Fries.

The price Seattle paid for Nelson leads the analysis to question how aggressively the Vikings are willing to spend at the position, though it doesn’t reveal what Minnesota actually offered.

Raiders interior lineman Jackson Powers-Johnson is named as another player worth watching.

And then, the defensive line.

On defense, the concern is experience behind the starters.

Jalen Redmond has carried over his strong 2025 play and ranks among Pro Football Focus’s top 10 defensive tackles through seven weeks. That’s elite company.

Levi Drake Rodriguez has also played well in his first season as a starter.

But behind them, rookies Caleb Banks and Dominique Orange have shown flashes and remain inconsistent.

And their struggles on Jonathan Taylor’s 38-yard touchdown run against Indianapolis showed the risk of relying on developing players in big moments.

Remember, Banks also left Sunday’s game with a limp, and his history of foot problems makes that a concern.

The ranking doesn’t call defensive tackle an emergency need, unless injuries change things. Instead, it suggests adding a veteran who could provide reliable rotational snaps while the rookies develop.

DeForest Buckner has already been traded, so another name we discussed is off the board. But Colts defensive tackle Grover Stewart is presented as a possible target for interior experience and extra pass-rush help.

These are possible paths, not confirmed deals.

So which would change this team the most? A runner defenses have to respect? A guard who stabilizes the pocket? Or a veteran who strengthens the defensive rotation?

Now, while the front office considers additions from outside, Blake Cashman is making a very strong case for Minnesota to take care of someone already in the building.

Cashman was named NFC Defensive Player of the Week after his performance in the 16-10 win over Indianapolis.

And his final numbers were outstanding.

Twelve tackles, including eight solo stops. Two tackles for loss. Two sacks. Three quarterback hits. And four passes defensed.

The sacks and the pass breakups were both career highs.

Think about how complete that is. He was a tackler against the run. A pass rusher getting after the quarterback. And a coverage linebacker breaking up passes. All in the same game.

He helped Minnesota take back control after Indianapolis scored an early touchdown, contributing in coverage, against the run, and as a pass rusher.

And the history behind this performance is wild.

Cashman became the first player since Chicago’s Alex Brown in 2005 to record at least two sacks and four passes defensed in the same game.

And he became the first player since sacks became an official stat in 1982 to finish a game with 12 tackles, two sacks, and four passes defensed.

Read that again. Since 1982. More than 40 years of NFL football, and nobody had ever put up that exact stat line until Blake Cashman did it on Sunday.

That performance was his 20th career game with double-digit tackles, and his 16th as a Viking.

And his consistency goes well beyond one afternoon.

Cashman has recorded at least seven tackles in 17 straight games. That’s the longest active streak in the NFL, and the longest by a Viking since at least 1987.

Seventeen straight games. Every single week, he shows up.

And for Minnesota fans, his background makes this especially meaningful.

He grew up in Eden Prairie. He joined the University of Minnesota as a walk-on. He played for the Golden Gophers. And now he’s leading the defense of his home state’s NFL team.

A walk-on from Eden Prairie, now the NFC Defensive Player of the Week, anchoring the best defense in football. That’s a Minnesota story.

And his role in Flores’s system goes beyond the stat sheet.

Cashman wears the green dot. He’s the one communicating the defensive calls, getting everyone lined up, and making sure all eleven players are on the same page.

Think about what that means in this defense. We talked earlier this week about how complex Flores’s disguises are, the rotations, the simulated pressures, the blitzes from every angle. Someone has to communicate all of that, quickly and clearly, before every snap. That’s Blake Cashman.

He combines that organizational responsibility with steady production, on a unit ranked first in multiple league categories.

This is his second career Defensive Player of the Week award. He also won the AFC honor with Houston in 2023.

But there’s a business question coming.

Cashman is in a contract year. And Minnesota didn’t finish an extension with him before the season.

Performances like this strengthen his negotiating position and could make keeping him a lot more expensive.

Every week he plays like this, the price goes up. And now he’s the NFC Defensive Player of the Week, with a historic stat line and the longest active tackle streak in the league.

So here’s the question for Nolan Teasley. While he’s looking at trades for guards, running backs, and defensive tackles, does he also need to lock up his green-dot linebacker before the price gets even higher?

Because the best move this front office makes might not be a trade at all. It might be keeping the heart of this defense in purple for years to come.

And Cashman’s award continues a remarkable run of recognition for Vikings players this season, a sign of just how many people on this roster are playing at a high level.

So let’s put it all together.

The Vikings added two tight ends to the practice squad in Devin Culp and Bryson Nesbit, building depth after Josh Oliver’s season-ending injury.

The offensive line is under scrutiny, with Landon Dickerson, Ben Bredeson, and Cesar Ruiz all part of the trade conversation, and Quenton Nelson now off the board after going to Seattle.

The deadline priorities are clear: running back, guard, and defensive line, in that order.

And Blake Cashman just made history, while his contract situation gets more urgent by the week.
