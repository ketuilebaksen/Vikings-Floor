VIKINGS ANNOUNCES SHOCKING DECISION AFTER CRAZY PACKERS GAME!

Vikings fans, he’s back. And he’s exactly who we hoped he’d be.

Kirk Cousins returned to Lambeau Field, the same stadium where his last chapter in Minnesota ended with a torn Achilles three years ago, and walked out with a 30-10 win over the Green Bay Packers.

Thirty to ten. At Lambeau. Five days after the trade.

The Vikings sacked Jordan Love eight times. They forced four turnovers. They led 17-0 at halftime while the Lambeau crowd booed its own team. And they’re now 7-2, with a season sweep of their biggest rival.

Before the game, the experts said Cousins didn’t need to be a superhero. He just needed to hit the layups.

He hit the layups.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. What a day. Let’s break it all down.

Let’s start with what everyone said before kickoff, because it makes what happened even sweeter.

After weeks of disastrous quarterback play, former NFL receiver Harry Douglas went on ESPN and said the Vikings were about to explode, and fast.

His reasoning? History.

Cousins and Justin Jefferson have four years together. Jefferson’s best season in the NFL came in 2022, which was also Kevin O’Connell’s first year as head coach. The Vikings went 13-4 and made the playoffs.

Douglas said that normally, when a new quarterback joins a team, it takes time to build chemistry with the receivers. But because these two have a history and understand each other completely, they’d click right away.

And he made a point that turned out to be the key to the whole game.

Cousins doesn’t need to throw for 350 yards. He needs 250 smart yards. Get the offense into the right play. Get the ball out consistently and on time. And when you get a chance, take a shot over the top.

The phrase everyone kept using: hit the layups.

Because when you go back and watch Minnesota’s games this season, the throws the quarterbacks missed weren’t hard throws. They were layups.

Mike Greenberg went even further. He pointed to the Monday night disaster against Buffalo, played in front of Roger Goodell, Randy Moss, and Adrian Peterson, and said, without meaning any disrespect, that it was as bad a game as you’ll ever see an NFL quarterback play.

Remember that night. Carson Wentz threw a simple bubble screen over Justin Jefferson’s head, and it turned into a fumble. Eight of 25 for 81 yards.

Greenberg’s point was that the improvement could be substantial just because the starting bar was so low.

And there was this from inside the building. Texting with sources in Minnesota, the word was that Cousins’s arrival was like the ultimate deodorant. It had been so bad in that building the last couple of weeks. And three days after that Monday night debacle, people were smiling around the facility again.

O’Connell, who was Cousins’s quarterbacks coach in Washington back in 2017 and coached him to 13 wins in 2022, reportedly looked 20 years younger.

That alone, the thinking went, might justify the third-round pick just for the change in atmosphere.

But not everyone was sold.

On First Take, Stephen A. Smith and Marcus Spears scoffed at the idea of trusting Kirk Cousins and Justin Jefferson over Jordan Love, who came into the game leading the NFL with more than 2,600 passing yards, with Christian Watson and Matthew Golden as weapons.

Cousins had thrown three interceptions against San Francisco in his last game with the Raiders. Green Bay had just torched New England for more than 300 passing yards. And the Packers were three-and-a-half-point favorites at home.

Some analysts were openly skeptical about how good Cousins would look right away, just days after the trade.

But Joe Fortenbaugh pointed out the trap waiting for Green Bay.

The Packers can’t run the football. And the way you beat Minnesota is by running the football. So if Green Bay became one-dimensional against this defense, how much confidence could they really have?

Meanwhile, he said, Minnesota would become multiple again. The Vikings would be able to run the ball and set up play-action. And their defense wouldn’t be stuck on the field for 79 plays and 43 minutes like it was against Buffalo.

Rich Eisen added one more edge. Cousins knows the NFC North cold. And he’ll face three opponents for the second time this season, after playing them earlier with the Raiders: San Francisco, New England, and the Jets.

And reportedly, Matt LaFleur wasn’t thrilled that Minnesota got Cousins for just a third-round pick.

After Sunday, you can understand why.

So let’s get to what actually happened.

Kirk Cousins finished 21 of 31 for 207 yards, two touchdowns, and an interception.

Remember Harry Douglas’s prediction? Two hundred fifty smart yards. Cousins threw for 207. Not flashy. Not 400 yards. Just smart, efficient football.

And that’s exactly what this team needed.

The Vikings scored 30 points, their most since the 39-22 win over these same Packers in Week 1.

Think about what that means. Before Sunday, Minnesota’s offense had scored 16 points or fewer in each of its last three games. Six against Buffalo. Fifteen against Detroit. Sixteen against Indianapolis.

Five days with Kirk Cousins, and the Vikings put up 30.

And the start was everything.

Minnesota jumped out to a 3-0 lead after a turnover.

Then came the play that set the tone for the entire afternoon.

Third-and-23.

Third and 23. The kind of situation where this offense had been hopeless all season. The kind of down where Wentz and Murray would check it down and punt.

Cousins hit Myles Price on a catch-and-run for a 38-yard touchdown.

Third-and-23, touchdown. Vikings up 10-0.

Remember Myles Price? The returner whose kickoff return set up the Colts game, and who’s been getting better every week. Now he’s catching touchdown passes from Kirk Cousins on third-and-long.

Then Cousins did it again. On another third-and-long, he found rookie Tai Felton for 24 yards to keep a drive alive.

Two huge third-and-long conversions. Both either produced or led to a touchdown.

And then Aaron Jones punched it in.

Aaron Jones. Playing through a knee issue that might date back to training camp. Scoring a touchdown at Lambeau Field, where he spent the first part of his career with the Packers.

Vikings 17, Packers 0.

That’s how the first half ended. And the Lambeau crowd booed its own team off the field at halftime.

Booed. At home. By a crowd that was supposed to be watching a red-hot Jordan Love.

Now let’s talk about the defense, because it was dominant.

Remember all the talk about Jordan Love being unstoppable? Five wins in his last six games, about 1,700 yards, 28 completions of 20-plus yards, 15 total touchdowns?

Brian Flores’s defense sacked him eight times.

Eight sacks.

Levi Drake Rodriguez had two of them. Remember, he was described as exceeding expectations in his first season as a starter. Now he’s getting home twice at Lambeau.

Dallas Turner got his hands on Love for a sack in the first quarter. The young edge rusher who’s been tied for third in the NFL in sacks.

And the Vikings forced four turnovers.

Remember what Matt LaFleur said before the game? He said what stands out about Flores’s defense is how consistently it forces offenses to play from behind the sticks, and that he’d rarely seen so many third-and-long situations this far into a season. He said Green Bay needed a clean game.

It was anything but clean.

And Fortenbaugh’s prediction came true. Green Bay can’t run the ball. Without Josh Jacobs, the Packers became one-dimensional, and Minnesota’s pass rush took over. Local coverage described the Packers’ offensive line as crumbling under the Vikings’ blitz package.

That’s the formula Fortenbaugh described. Make them one-dimensional, and the pass rush feasts.

Now, Green Bay did show some life.

In the third quarter, Michael Sturdivant scored a touchdown to cut the Vikings’ lead to 20-7. Then a field goal made it 20-10 heading into the fourth quarter.

That’s the moment that would have broken this team a few weeks ago. The lead shrinking. The momentum swinging. A road crowd waking up.

And this is where Kirk Cousins showed exactly why Minnesota traded for him.

Cousins went 8 for 8 on an 11-play, 75-yard touchdown drive that essentially sealed the win.

Eight for eight. Every throw on target.

He rolled out and found Justin Jefferson for 11 yards and a first down. And then, on first down from the 20, Cousins found tight end Gavin Bartholomew over the middle. Bartholomew broke a tackle and went in for a 20-yard touchdown.

Vikings 27, Packers 10, with 8:29 left.

Remember who Bartholomew is? One of the young tight ends getting his first real NFL snaps this season. The guy fans worried about when Josh Oliver went down for the year.

And here’s something that makes it even more impressive. Cousins did all of this without T.J. Hockenson, Minnesota’s top tight end.

Then the Vikings added a Will Reichard field goal to make it 30-10. On third-and-goal, Cousins’s throw went off Jefferson’s hands, so they settled for three. But by then, the game was over.

And Jordan Mason closed it out with a few late runs to run out the clock.

That’s Minnesota football. Score early, rush the passer, force turnovers, and run out the clock at the end.

So what does this mean?

First, the Vikings snapped their two-game losing streak. They’re now 7-2.

Second, they beat the Packers for the fifth time in six meetings, and completed a season sweep of their biggest rival. Remember, they won the opener 39-22, scoring the final 29 points in that game. Now this.

Third, Green Bay drops to 6-4. And Minnesota’s grip on the NFC North just got a lot tighter.

Remember the playoff math before the game? A win would push Minnesota’s playoff chances to roughly 90 percent. The division odds would climb to 37 percent. The No. 1 seed chances would jump to 17 percent.

Well, the Vikings won. And they didn’t just win. They dominated.

And there’s the full-circle moment.

Kirk Cousins’s final snaps in his first stint with Minnesota came at this same stadium, when he tore his Achilles in a 24-10 win over the Packers in October 2023.

Now he begins his second stint the same way: with a win at Lambeau Field.

He walked off that field injured three years ago, not knowing if he’d ever wear purple again. This time, he walked off a winner.

Now let’s talk about what this changes going forward.

Remember the stat from Vikings Now? Minnesota had been 18-2 in its last 20 games when its quarterback posted a passer rating above about 91.7.

The point was that this team doesn’t need a superstar. It just needs competent quarterback play.

Sunday proved it. Cousins didn’t throw for 400 yards. He threw for 207. But he converted third-and-23. He converted third-and-long again. He went 8 for 8 when the lead was shrinking. He hit the layups.

And look at what happened to the defense. For two straight weeks, it was on the field for roughly 80 combined minutes, against Detroit and Buffalo. When the offense can sustain drives and build a lead, the defense gets to play fast, play aggressive, and pin its ears back. That’s when you get eight sacks and four turnovers.

That’s the hidden value of Kirk Cousins. He made the defense better without ever stepping on the field with it.

And now, the attention turns to the next big test.

At 7-2, Minnesota sets up a Sunday Night Football showdown with the San Francisco 49ers.

The same 49ers who added DeForest Buckner and Keenan Allen at the deadline. And remember Rich Eisen’s point. Cousins faced San Francisco earlier this season as a Raider. He knows exactly what that defense looks like.

That’s a heavyweight matchup on the biggest stage. And for the first time in weeks, Minnesota walks into it with real confidence on both sides of the ball.

So let’s put it all together.

Kirk Cousins, five days after being traded back to Minnesota, went 21 of 31 for 207 yards and two touchdowns at Lambeau Field.

He converted third-and-23 for a 38-yard touchdown to Myles Price, and went 8 for 8 on the game-sealing drive.

Brian Flores’s defense sacked Jordan Love eight times and forced four turnovers.

The Vikings led 17-0 at halftime, and the Lambeau crowd booed its own team.

Minnesota won 30-10, scored its most points since Week 1, snapped a two-game losing streak, swept the Packers, and improved to 7-2.

Harry Douglas said Cousins just needed 250 smart yards and to hit the layups. Joe Fortenbaugh said Green Bay would become one-dimensional and the pass rush would feast. Both were right.

And Stephen A. Smith, Marcus Spears, and everyone who picked Jordan Love? Lambeau gave its answer.
