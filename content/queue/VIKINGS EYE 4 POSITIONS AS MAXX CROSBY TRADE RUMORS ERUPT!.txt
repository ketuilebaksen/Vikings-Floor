VIKINGS EYE 4 POSITIONS AS MAXX CROSBY TRADE RUMORS ERUPT!

Vikings fans, the trade deadline chaos has officially arrived in Minnesota.

A blockbuster trade proposal involving Maxx Crosby could make this defense even more dangerous. Another veteran has come up as a possible addition. And now the reports are in: Nolan Teasley has been described as a “madman on the phones,” exploring upgrades at four different positions.

The pressure is building as NFC rivals load up. So how far should the Vikings go?

And on top of all that, the team just got an encouraging injury update ahead of Sunday’s showdown in Detroit.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s break it all down.

Most of Minnesota’s trade speculation has focused on finding another running back, especially Jonathan Taylor.

But a different proposal asks a fascinating question. Should the Vikings double down on the unit that’s already carrying this team?

What would Brian Flores do with Maxx Crosby?

That’s the possibility raised in a trade discussion involving the Las Vegas Raiders’ defensive star.

And there’s an interesting backstory here.

Crosby was part of an attempted trade to Baltimore earlier this year. But that deal fell apart after he failed his physical.

Think about that. A trade for one of the most feared pass rushers in football, agreed to, and then it collapsed at the very last step.

Now fresh speculation has put his name back in the conversation, and this time Minnesota is part of it.

During an appearance on the SKOL Network, Tyler Forness said he was confident, based on information he’d received, that there could be something real behind the rumors.

Now, let’s be clear about what that means. It doesn’t establish that Minnesota and Las Vegas are negotiating. But it explains why this possibility is getting so much attention.

So what would it cost?

The acquisition price would be substantial. The analysis suggests a move this big could approach the value of two first-round picks, similar to the proposed Baltimore deal.

But the specific hypothetical package is different.

Minnesota would send a 2027 first-round pick, a 2028 second-round pick, and a 2028 fifth-round pick to Las Vegas.

The Raiders would send Maxx Crosby and a 2028 sixth-round pick back.

A first, a second, and a fifth, for Maxx Crosby and a sixth.

A to Z Sports’ trade simulator rated that package as fair. But again, that’s an evaluation of the proposal, not evidence that either front office would accept it.

So what’s the argument for Minnesota?

It’s simple. Another disruptive defender could make an already dominant defense even harder to handle, while the offense keeps searching for consistency.

Picture it. Brian Flores, blitzing 70 percent of the time. Dallas Turner, tied for third in the league in sacks. Andrew Van Ginkel, Jalen Redmond, and Blake Cashman wearing the green dot.

And now add Maxx Crosby on the edge.

Opposing quarterbacks are already trapped in the Matrix. With Crosby, they might never find their way out.

But Crosby isn’t the only Raider connected to the Vikings.

ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler also looked at Kirk Cousins’ trade value. And he noted that Cousins has played well enough through seven games to interest another team.

Fowler pointed to a reworked contract with a base salary of $1.3 million, which could make him financially appealing.

One point three million dollars. For a quarterback who, as we covered earlier this week, was leading the league in touchdown passes. That fits even a tight cap situation like Minnesota’s.

And here’s what Las Vegas might be thinking. If the Raiders decide to start rookie Fernando Mendoza, third-string quarterback Aidan O’Connell could move into the main backup role. So Cousins could become expendable.

But Fowler added some important context. The Raiders hadn’t planned to be especially active at the deadline, and there aren’t any glaring quarterback needs around the league right now, though injuries could change that.

Then ESPN’s Matt Bowen broke down what kind of quarterback Cousins is.

Bowen described Cousins as a pocket passer who wins through timing, ball placement, post-snap reads, and distributing the football.

He also noted the limits in Cousins’ ability to create outside the structure of a play. He’s not going to scramble for 35 yards the way Kyler Murray did against Indianapolis.

But Bowen identified bootlegs and play-action concepts as useful parts of his fit. And those are exactly the kinds of concepts Kevin O’Connell built his offense around when Cousins was here in 2022.

Cousins has 16 touchdown passes and five interceptions this season. And Bowen listed Carolina, the Giants, and Minnesota as possible destinations.

To be clear, neither the Crosby proposal nor the Cousins idea is a completed deal. These are possibilities.

But now there’s real reporting behind the broader expectation that Minnesota is actively looking for upgrades.

Sports Illustrated’s Albert Breer reported Friday morning that the Vikings have asked about interior offensive linemen, interior defensive linemen, and running backs ahead of the November 10th deadline.

That matters, because it moves the conversation beyond fans and analysts listing players who might make sense. This is reporting on what the Vikings are actually doing.

And NFL analyst James Palmer offered a similar picture.

Palmer described first-year general manager Nolan Teasley as someone who’s looking at everything. And he called him “a madman on the phones.”

A madman on the phones. That’s what Vikings fans wanted to hear.

Palmer specifically identified running back and tight end as positions being examined.

So put those two reports together, and here are the four positions Minnesota is exploring: interior offensive line, interior defensive line, running back, and tight end.

That’s a front office looking for help on both sides of the ball, with offensive needs that go beyond just the backfield.

And every one of those positions makes sense.

Interior offensive line? Donovan Jackson is out for the season, undrafted rookie Joe Huber is starting in his place, and Will Fries has been inconsistent. Remember Landon Dickerson, the three-time Pro Bowler we talked about yesterday.

Interior defensive line? Rookie Caleb Banks left the Colts game with a limp, and the rookies behind Jalen Redmond and Levi Drake Rodriguez have been inconsistent.

Running back? Aaron Jones is managing a knee injury, and the run game ranks 22nd in yards per carry.

Tight end? Josh Oliver is out for the season with a torn biceps, and the team just added two players to the practice squad.

So Teasley isn’t just making noise. He’s working on the actual weak spots.

Minnesota is still unbeaten and leading the NFC North. But the defense has been playing at a much higher level than the offense.

And the running back situation adds urgency.

Aaron Jones has been managing a left knee sprain. Jordan Mason could see a bigger workload depending on Jones’s availability and effectiveness. Another runner could provide insurance and another option for an offense that has struggled to sustain production.

But here’s the reality check. The biggest names in the conversation might not actually be available.

Tom Pelissero said Jonathan Taylor was not on the trading block at that point. And he said he wouldn’t hold his breath on either Taylor or Philadelphia’s Saquon Barkley being moved.

That doesn’t mean things can’t change. But it limits what we can reasonably assume from Minnesota’s interest alone.

And here’s the key point. Nolan Teasley can make all the calls he wants. That doesn’t mean he can convince another team to give up a player it doesn’t want to trade.

Being a madman on the phones only matters if someone on the other end is willing to deal.

And the division’s activity only adds to the pressure around those conversations.

Chicago acquired Jeffery Simmons from Tennessee, strengthening a defensive front Minnesota will have to deal with twice a year.

Other contenders are building toward the postseason too, while Vikings fans wait for a response that improves their own roster.

So the unresolved questions are clear.

Which players can actually be acquired? What would they cost? And is the available upgrade big enough to justify the investment?

Being busy on the phone is the start of that process. Turning those conversations into the right addition is the part that will ultimately be judged.

And that distinction is at the center of the growing debate about Teasley’s first trade deadline in charge.

Let’s look at the timeline.

The deadline arrives at 4 p.m. Eastern on Tuesday, November 10th. And several potential playoff opponents have already acted since Minnesota’s 16-10 win over Indianapolis.

On Tuesday, the Colts traded left guard Quenton Nelson to Seattle. That addressed an important need for the Seahawks as they compete with San Francisco in the NFC West.

The 49ers answered that same afternoon by acquiring defensive tackle DeForest Buckner from Indianapolis.

Then on Thursday, Chicago sent Tennessee a package including two first-round picks and a fourth-rounder for Jeffery Simmons.

Three days. Three NFC contenders. Three major additions.

Minnesota’s undefeated record puts it in a strong position. But those moves show that other teams are spending real resources to get better.

So let’s look at both sides of the argument.

The argument for Teasley responding aggressively is simple.

The offense has struggled. The upcoming schedule is tough. And a defense playing at this level is an opportunity worth supporting.

You don’t waste a defense like this. Defenses this good don’t come around often, and they don’t stay this good forever. Players get expensive. Coordinators get head coaching jobs. Brian Flores is already in the Assistant Coach of the Year conversation, and he’s going to be on head coaching lists again this offseason.

Breer’s reporting on the interior lines and running back, along with Palmer’s comments during Amazon Prime’s Thursday Night Football postgame coverage, suggests Teasley recognizes there are several possible routes.

But what the reporting doesn’t settle is whether he’s chasing a major acquisition or comparing cheaper options.

And that’s the central choice.

Jason Harmon outlined it in an October 29th post. Inject life into the offense, or build an even more imposing pass rush.

His point was that the defensive route shouldn’t be dismissed just because the offensive problems are more obvious.

And that’s where the Crosby proposal becomes relevant to the bigger discussion.

A general manager could decide that strengthening an exceptional defense gives Minnesota its best chance, even if it doesn’t directly fix the passing game or the blocking.

There’s logic to that. If your defense is so dominant that opponents can barely score, your offense only needs 17 or 20 points to win. Crosby could make the defense so suffocating that the offensive struggles matter less.

But the long-term argument deserves attention too.

Minnesota is expected to have needs next offseason at quarterback, running back, tight end, outside linebacker, middle linebacker, cornerback, and along the defensive line.

That’s a long list. Seven positions.

And spending premium draft capital now could make those future needs a lot harder to address.

If you trade a 2027 first-round pick for Crosby, that’s one fewer premium pick to fill all those holes next spring. And remember what Kevin O’Connell said: he wants Teasley to have the resources to put his own stamp on this roster.

So this decision is about more than showing a willingness to make headlines.

Teasley has to weigh the quality of the available player, the immediate improvement, the cost, and the roster challenges that come after.

The case for a bold move is that Minnesota has the league’s last unbeaten record and a defense that can drive a serious postseason run.

The case for restraint is that an expensive acquisition still has to improve the team enough to justify what leaves the building.

So where would you draw that line? Pay heavily to chase this opportunity? Or protect enough resources to keep the roster competitive beyond this season?

While those conversations continue, Minnesota got some encouraging news about someone who could help right away.

NFL insider Ian Rapoport gave a positive update on Aaron Jones ahead of Sunday’s game against Detroit.

According to Rapoport, Jones has been dealing on and off with a knee injury believed to be a sprained MCL, and Rapoport believes the problem started during training camp.

Earlier reporting had said Jones was playing through the issue by Week 2. So this latest update gives a possible earlier starting point. It isn’t describing a brand-new injury.

Think about that. If Rapoport is right, Jones has been dealing with this knee since before the season even started. And he’s still been one of the most productive pieces of this offense.

Here’s the timeline.

Jones aggravated the knee against Indianapolis after taking a hit from a Colts safety at the end of a 17-yard run. That was the play where he limped off, slammed his hand on the bench, and went to the blue medical tent.

Kevin O’Connell described him as day-to-day, and his status stayed in question during the week.

Then came the good news.

Rapoport noted that Jones missed Wednesday’s practice but returned in a limited role on Thursday. And Rapoport called that progress a good sign for his chances of playing Sunday.

The veteran has dealt with MCL injuries before, which makes the team’s evaluation of his recovery especially relevant. As we covered last week, Jones has a long history with this kind of injury: Green Bay in 2017, 2018, 2021, and 2023.

And Jones himself? He described the latest issue as “a little bruise,” and said he plans to be ready for Detroit.

A little bruise. That’s Aaron Jones. You can’t measure heart.

That optimism is encouraging. But his expected availability and how effectively he can run are two separate questions.

Let’s look at what he’s given this offense.

Jones has 105 carries for 436 yards and two touchdowns. He’s added 13 catches for 72 yards, giving Minnesota a receiving option out of the backfield.

His best games include 105 rushing yards on 23 carries against Chicago in Week 2, the same game where he first hurt the knee, plus another performance with 25 carries for 113 yards and a touchdown.

Think about that Chicago game. He ran for 105 yards on a newly injured knee. That’s toughness.

And those games show exactly why having him available matters.

Against a physical division opponent like Detroit, Minnesota would benefit from being able to split the work instead of putting all of it on Jordan Mason.

Still, getting back on the field doesn’t automatically mean Jones will handle his usual workload or play without discomfort.

The practical questions are how the knee responds, whether it affects his movement, and how the coaching staff divides the carries.

His progress could improve the Vikings’ immediate options against Detroit, and it could shape how they view the backfield before the trade deadline.

If Jones looks healthy and productive on Sunday, maybe running back drops down the priority list. If he struggles, the search for another back gets more urgent.

So let’s put it all together.

Minnesota’s deadline discussion now goes far beyond one running back.

The Crosby proposal raises the possibility of making the defense even stronger, with a hypothetical package of a first, a second, and a fifth.

Kirk Cousins has been identified as a possible fit, on a $1.3 million base salary.

Reports from Albert Breer and James Palmer say Teasley is exploring four positions: interior offensive line, interior defensive line, running back, and tight end. And he’s being called a madman on the phones.

Jonathan Taylor and Saquon Barkley might not be available at all, according to Tom Pelissero.

And Aaron Jones’s progress offers encouragement for Sunday. But the bigger roster decisions are still ahead.
