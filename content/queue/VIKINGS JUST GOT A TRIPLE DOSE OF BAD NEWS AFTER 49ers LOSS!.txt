VIKINGS JUST GOT A TRIPLE DOSE OF BAD NEWS AFTER 49ERS LOSS!

Vikings fans, the bad news didn't stop when the clock hit zero in Mexico City.

Bad news No. 1: Christian Darrisaw, the man who protects Kirk Cousins's blind side, injured his shoulder and couldn't finish the game. Minnesota already made a move to add offensive line depth.

Bad news No. 2: a national writer just gave the Vikings a D-minus and said missing the playoffs has become a real possibility.

Bad news No. 3: the team that started 6-0 has slipped to third place in the NFC North.

And on top of all that, a Nick Bosa hit on Kirk Cousins could still draw punishment from the NFL.

But Cousins, Justin Jefferson, and a former Vikings fan favorite all have their own explanations for what happened, and one of them has a very clear message for this team.

There's a lot to sort out before Atlanta comes to town. Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let's get into it.

Bad news No. 1. The offensive line.

Christian Darrisaw injured his shoulder against the 49ers and couldn't finish the game. Ryan Van Demark replaced him.

And that's a big concern. Darrisaw is the starting left tackle. He protects Kirk Cousins's blind side. And this offense just struggled to protect the ball and sustain drives with him on the field for most of the game.

Now, the reports we have don't give a diagnosis or a timeline for his return. So we don't know yet how serious this is.

But Minnesota is already adding another offensive lineman.

CBS Sports reporter Matt Zenitz reported on November 23rd that the Vikings are bringing Tristan Leigh back to the practice squad.

Leigh has been with Minnesota earlier this season, and the team has released and brought him back several times.

He's a rookie, 6-foot-4 and 312 pounds. And he has a background worth developing.

He was a five-star recruit coming out of high school. Then he became Clemson's starting left tackle from 2023 through 2025.

That's three seasons as a starter at left tackle in a major college program.

Now, to be clear, adding Leigh doesn't mean Minnesota has named a new starter. It's a depth move. It gives the team another developmental option while it figures out the Darrisaw situation.

And there's a roster decision attached to it.

The practice squad is already at its 16-player limit. So Minnesota has to either release someone, or promote a player to the 53-man roster. And a promotion would require a corresponding move on the active roster too.

The other offensive linemen currently on the practice squad are Henry Byrd, Jack Nelson, and Josh Priebe.

So what does Leigh bring?

The scouting reports show both the appeal and the work still ahead.

NFL.com's Lance Zierlein described him as a talented but inconsistent prospect. He raised concerns about Leigh's ability to handle power, especially in the running game.

A pre-draft evaluation from Pro Football Network was more optimistic about his ceiling.

Its argument was that Leigh offered unusual explosiveness, raw power, physicality, and a combination of length and width, at a price expected to be outside the first round.

It highlighted his energetic movement and quick twitch, along with flashes of forceful hands and core strength in pass protection.

But it also pointed to specific areas he needs to improve. His anchor. His footwork. The precision and timing of his hands. And the coordination between his upper and lower body when he's protecting the quarterback.

The same report called him a people mover in the run game, and saw upside in both phases. It even suggested his explosive power in tight spaces could translate to guard, if tackle doesn't turn out to be his best position.

So he's a project. A talented one. But the real question for Sunday is Darrisaw's shoulder.

Now, let's talk about that hit on Kirk Cousins that could still draw attention from the league.

Nick Bosa finished with two quarterback hits in San Francisco's 35-10 win. One of them drew a roughing-the-passer penalty.

It happened in the first quarter. The Vikings were facing second-and-7 at the San Francisco 22-yard line.

Cousins threw incomplete toward T.J. Hockenson. But the flag gave Minnesota 15 yards and a first down.

Bosa appeared to try to brace himself as he fell. But he still landed with his body weight on Cousins.

And that brought back a familiar argument. What exactly is the rule supposed to prevent? And how is a defender supposed to follow it while finishing a tackle?

A fine is possible, but nothing has been announced.

The listed amount for a first offense is $17,911. A second offense carries a fine of $23,882.

And that distinction matters. A flag on the field and a fine from the league are two separate decisions.

This was the only penalty called in the game for a finable offense. The NFL can also review plays that weren't flagged.

No Vikings player was flagged for a finable offense. But that doesn't guarantee the league won't find something in its review.

And the body-weight rule has been controversial for years.

Back in 2018, increased enforcement created so much backlash that the league released videos explaining what it expected from defenders.

After that change, roughing-the-passer calls climbed above half a call per game.

In 2021, when the schedule expanded, the rate was 0.54 per game.

This season, it's above 0.44, the highest rate since 2021.

Through Week 10, the league had issued 21 fines classified as hits on quarterbacks or roughing the passer, including four involving body weight.

And the amounts show that the same classification doesn't always produce the same punishment.

Baltimore's Trey Hendrickson and a Washington defender each received $17,911 fines.

New Orleans linebacker Danny Stutsman received a much smaller fine under the same classification.

And Bosa's teammate Keion White was fined $13,770 for a roughing and body-weight violation after Week 2.

But here's the important part. The debate over this rule shouldn't hide what actually decided Sunday's game.

Minnesota came in with the fewest penalties in the league. And the Vikings committed just three penalties for 23 yards against San Francisco.

The 49ers had six for 49.

So by that measure, the Vikings played a clean game. And they still couldn't keep up.

The 49ers attacked the defense early. Minnesota had to chase the game through the air. And the pick-six made a comeback almost impossible.

Which brings us to bad news No. 2. The playoff warning.

CBS Sports writer John Breech gave Minnesota a D-minus for the loss. And he suggested that missing the postseason has become a real possibility.

His assessment was that Cousins, who was brought back to improve the offense, got rattled early and never looked comfortable.

That's Breech's evaluation of what he watched. And it led him to a much bigger concern.

And bad news No. 3 comes from the standings.

A team that started 6-0 is now 7-3, after losing three of its last four games.

Minnesota has slipped to third place in the NFC North. It enters Week 12 as the conference's sixth seed, holding the second wild-card spot.

Think about how fast that happened. A month ago, the Vikings were the last undefeated team in the NFC. Now they're holding onto a wild-card spot.

The San Francisco game definitely gave people a reason to question whether the Vikings belong with the conference's best teams.

The 49ers have the NFC's best record. And Minnesota wasn't very competitive at a neutral site, where San Francisco also seemed to have more fans in the stands.

The defense had its first real humbling of the 2026 season. And the offense didn't score after halftime.

Cousins completed 26 of 38 passes. But his 6.2 yards per attempt was his lowest of the season since Week 1. He threw no touchdowns, and he threw an interception that was returned for a score.

So yes, the concern is real.

But here's the good news. The same article gave reasons not to jump from one ugly loss to predicting the Vikings will miss the playoffs.

First, the Vikings are still in a playoff position.

Second, they own the tiebreaker over Green Bay.

Third, five of their remaining seven games are at home.

Now, the margin has gotten thinner. The Packers, Seahawks, and Rams are each one game behind Minnesota in the wild-card race.

And in the division, Chicago and Detroit are both a game ahead. And they play each other on Thanksgiving, with first place on the line.

That's a huge game for Vikings fans to watch. One of those teams is going to lose.

And Minnesota already has a win over the Bears. And still gets both Chicago and Detroit at U.S. Bank Stadium.

So the Vikings can still decide the division themselves.

The rest of the remaining schedule includes Atlanta, Carolina, New England, Washington, and the New York Jets.

And none of those five teams enters Week 12 with a winning record.

Minnesota also won't have to face San Francisco again during the regular season.

And Kirk Cousins gets more time to settle back into this offense.

None of that guarantees a turnaround. But it explains why the playoff picture is a lot more favorable than Sunday night felt.

And the next chance comes against Atlanta, at home, on November 29th, with kickoff at noon Central.

Now, let's hear from the people involved. Because their explanations of what went wrong on offense are a lot more specific than "nothing worked."

Minnesota did move the ball against San Francisco. But two costly turnovers and a one-for-three performance in the red zone kept that movement from turning into points.

Cousins finished with 235 passing yards and a 73.9 passer rating.

Afterward, he said the offense had put in a lot of work in practice, both last week and this week, and was able to run the game plan.

What it couldn't consistently create was explosive plays. The big gains that put points on the board and keep drives alive.

And remember, this was only his second game since coming back from Las Vegas in the deadline-day trade.

Cousins said he's feeling more comfortable every day and every week. And he specifically said he felt comfortable during the game.

That's his account. Even though Breech saw a quarterback who looked unsettled.

And Cousins made an interesting point. He noted that Minnesota converted 50 percent of its third downs. And he said keeping that rate over a full season would probably rank first in the league.

His point was that third-down efficiency wasn't the main problem. Minnesota could move the ball. It just stalled before getting the points it needed.

And Justin Jefferson's fumble was the most painful example.

Jefferson caught seven of his nine targets for 86 yards.

But early in the third quarter, with Minnesota driving coming out of halftime, he lost the ball. And with it, a chance to start the second half with a touchdown.

And Jefferson didn't blame anyone else.

Speaking after the game on November 22nd, he called it a play he'd take back if he could. And he called Cousins's throw phenomenal, delivered through the second window.

He took full responsibility for his ball security. He explained that he doesn't normally carry the ball that way, and that he takes pride in protecting it. When he goes to the ground, he expects to come back up with the ball.

That's accountability. O'Connell took the blame for the pick-six play call. Cousins took the blame for not seeing the safety. Jefferson took the blame for the fumble.

Nobody is pointing fingers in that locker room.

And then a former Vikings fan favorite weighed in.

Adam Thielen shared his perspective during a November 23rd appearance on KFAN.

He pointed to how quickly the game changed. Minnesota put together a promising touchdown drive in the first quarter. Then San Francisco scored 21 points in the second quarter, while the Vikings defense struggled to find answers.

One week after scoring 30 against Green Bay, Minnesota finished with 10 points, and nothing after halftime.

Thielen's advice? Flush it.

He acknowledged the team would watch the film. But his bigger point was that this was the kind of loss players need to put behind them, instead of carrying into the next week.

In his view, the way this game unfolded didn't offer many useful lessons.

That's his opinion, and he recognized that fans might not want to hear it after a performance like this.

His focus was on recovering, accepting the loss, and preparing for the next opponent with Super Bowl urgency.

And he specifically warned against underestimating Atlanta.

The Falcons are 3-7. But they've been competitive in a lot of games.

Thielen's message was simple: this is still the NFL. Minnesota can't just roll the ball out there and assume it's going to win.

He wants a fast start. One that gets the home crowd involved early. And keeps the boos we've heard at some recent home games from becoming part of the afternoon.

And that's a real point. The Vikings have lost at home this season. U.S. Bank Stadium needs to become a fortress again.

With five of the final seven games at home, including the rematches with Detroit and Chicago, the Vikings still have a path back into the division race.

The article also mentioned winning out as a path to at least a wild-card spot. But the immediate assignment is Atlanta.
