VIKINGS JUST GOT A TRIPLE DOSE OF GREAT NEWS!

Vikings fans, there’s a lot of good news coming out of Minnesota this week. And one twist that nobody saw coming.

Kevin O’Connell made a revealing admission about Jauan Jennings ahead of the 49ers game. The Vikings added a Super Bowl champion to the secondary. And Kirk Cousins is suddenly being mentioned in a conversation that seemed impossible a month ago: the MVP race.

But the biggest twist comes out of New York, where there’s talk about a possible trade involving Kevin O’Connell himself.

There’s a reason that conversation is happening. And there’s also a problem in the running game that has to be fixed for this offense to reach its full potential.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. The 49ers are on Sunday night in Mexico City. Let’s get into it.

Great news number one: Jauan Jennings is ready for his moment.

Let’s start with Jennings, because this matchup has both a personal angle and a real football reason for Minnesota to get him involved.

The Vikings face the 49ers on November 22nd in Mexico City. And Jennings will be lining up against the organization he used to play for.

And remember what happened at the trade deadline. The Vikings had a deal in place to send Jennings back to San Francisco. It fell through because Jennings decided he didn’t want to go back to the Bay Area.

He turned down a return to the 49ers. And now he gets to face them.

That makes this a lot more interesting than a routine game against a former team.

But motivation alone won’t give Minnesota the production it needs.

Here’s the football reason it matters. Jordan Addison is out. And his absence opens up opportunities in the passing game.

Kirk Cousins showed against Green Bay that he’s willing to spread the ball around to whoever is available. Remember, he threw touchdowns to Myles Price and Gavin Bartholomew, players he admitted he barely knew.

On the November 17th edition of X’s and O’s with Kevin O’Connell, the coach confirmed that Jennings is excited for this one.

And O’Connell made a point of recognizing work that doesn’t always show up in the receiving numbers.

He said Jennings brings it every week. And he said Jennings has handled important early-down assignments, including jobs the coach believes very few players can do the same way.

Think about what that means. Jennings is known as one of the best blocking receivers in football. He does the dirty work. On a team where the run game has struggled, a receiver who can block on the edge is a big deal.

But with Addison out, O’Connell said the Vikings need plays from Jennings in the passing game too.

And we just saw how fast a receiver’s role can change.

O’Connell explained that most of the Green Bay game plan was already installed when Addison’s freak injury happened during a Friday throwing session.

Suddenly, a player the coaches had been planning to use was unavailable for a huge division game.

And who stepped up? Tai Felton and Myles Price.

Felton caught a 24-yard pass on third-and-long. Price caught the 38-yard touchdown on third-and-23.

O’Connell praised both players. But he also praised the response across the whole organization. Players and coaches adjusted, trusted the receivers who were available, and let them play.

So for Jennings, this opportunity comes with competition from teammates who just helped Minnesota win at Lambeau.

The Vikings can give him a bigger role without forgetting what Price and Felton showed.

Now look at what’s waiting on the other side.

San Francisco is coming off a 46-point performance against Dallas in Week 10.

Forty-six points.

Speaking to reporters on November 16th, O’Connell called the 49ers a very difficult opponent, and said they may be the best team in football.

He pointed to the record, the film, all three phases, and the coaching.

He said Brock Purdy is playing as well as anybody in the league.

And he named the players San Francisco has gotten the most out of. Trent Williams. Christian McCaffrey. George Kittle. Deebo Samuel.

That’s a loaded roster. And that’s before you add DeForest Buckner and Keenan Allen, who the 49ers picked up at the deadline.

That’s why Jennings’s opportunity matters beyond the revenge storyline.

Minnesota needs productive possessions against an opponent with that many ways to hurt you. If Jennings gets a chance on a big down, he has to give Cousins a reason to come back to him.

Great news number two: a Super Bowl champion joins the secondary.

While the offense works through all of that, Minnesota has made a move to rebuild its defensive depth.

KPRC2’s Aaron Wilson reported on November 17th that the Vikings are signing safety DeAnthony Bell to the practice squad, citing a league source.

Bell is 30 years old, 6-foot-1, and 211 pounds.

He came into the league as an undrafted free agent and spent his first three NFL seasons in Cleveland, before also spending time in Seattle and Carolina.

And here’s the part that makes this interesting. Bell earned a Super Bowl ring for his contributions during Seattle’s championship season, even though he moved to the Panthers between Weeks 17 and 18.

So Minnesota is bringing in a player who has been around very different defensive environments, and who has real experience beyond a training camp résumé.

And there’s a fun connection here. Remember, Nolan Teasley was part of that Seattle championship front office before coming to Minnesota. He knows exactly who DeAnthony Bell is.

Let’s look at his numbers.

In 64 career games, including nine starts, Bell has 76 total tackles, two interceptions, a forced fumble, and half a sack.

His most recent regular-season or postseason action came during Carolina’s 2025 postseason.

He also appeared in all three of Seattle’s preseason games this year, recording nine combined tackles, according to Pro Football Focus.

So why now?

The opening in Minnesota came after the Giants signed a defensive back off the Vikings’ practice squad. That move left room for another veteran defensive back.

And Minnesota had no safeties on its practice squad. Cornerback Devon Armstead was the only other defensive back in that group.

On the active roster, the Vikings have safeties like Josh Metellus, Harrison Smith, and Jay Ward, among others.

So Bell arrives as extra insurance, not as someone being presented as an immediate starter. The report makes clear that several setbacks would likely have to happen before he sees the field.

But the point of a practice squad move like this is to get a player into the building before things become urgent.

Bell can learn the calls. Work with the coaches. And give Brian Flores another option if the secondary loses somebody.

And in a Flores defense, that learning curve matters. Remember how complex this scheme is: disguised looks, late rotations, safeties blitzing off the edge, covering the slot, rotating at the snap. You can’t just drop a player into it on a Sunday morning. Getting Bell in now means he’ll be ready if he’s needed in December.

His versatility could matter if Flores has to shuffle personnel later in the season.

And there’s a bigger benefit if the offense keeps doing what it did at Lambeau.

This defense has carried Minnesota through most of the schedule. Sustained drives and points from Cousins’s offense give those defenders more rest and better game situations. A fresher defense is a more dangerous defense.

Great news number three: Kirk Cousins enters the MVP conversation.

A few weeks ago, nobody in Minnesota was talking about a big individual season for any Vikings quarterback.

Now there’s an MVP case being made for Kirk Cousins.

And it starts with what he’d already done in Las Vegas before coming back.

Across the season so far, Cousins has 2,547 passing yards, fifth in the league. And 22 touchdown passes, second in the league.

He’s completing 64.5 percent of his throws, which ranks 18th. And his Pro Football Focus passing grade of 75.2 is tied for 14th.

Those numbers show both the strength and the limits of the argument.

The article acknowledges his production doesn’t match Joe Burrow’s. But it argues that the circumstances could make Cousins more interesting to voters.

He played his first nine games with the Raiders, an offense the author sees as short on supporting talent.

Now he’s joined a team the author projects to win at least 11 games, with a chance to turn around one of the league’s least productive offenses.

And look at that turnaround already. Minnesota scored only six points against Buffalo in Week 9. In Cousins’s first game back, it scored 30 against Green Bay.

If that improvement lasts, his impact on the Vikings’ season becomes a big part of the case.

And there’s an unusual schedule detail that makes this even more interesting.

Las Vegas hadn’t reached its bye when Cousins was traded. And Minnesota had already taken its bye back in Week 6.

So Cousins is on track to play 18 games this regular season, if he stays healthy. And the report presents that as a potential NFL first.

Eighteen regular-season games in one season, because he switched teams before having a bye.

That gives him more opportunity for production. But the MVP scenario still depends on what he does from here, and how well Minnesota finishes.

The argument is that a complete offensive turnaround could put him in the conversation with Burrow at NFL Honors.

To be clear, that’s a projection at this point. There isn’t any developed voting consensus.

But a month ago, Kirk Cousins was a backup plan in Las Vegas. Now people are writing about his MVP case. That’s how fast things change in this league.

Now here’s the twist nobody saw coming: Kevin O’Connell’s name in a trade rumor.

This is where we have to keep the reporting separate from any possible outcome.

First, the context.

Minnesota is 7-2 and firmly in the NFC playoff race. But the gap between the two sides of the ball is still striking.

The defense is allowing 273.3 yards per game, the second-fewest in the league.

The offense is producing 271.8 yards per game, second from the bottom.

Second-best defense. Second-worst offense. By almost the exact same number.

Cousins helped the Vikings put up 30 points at Green Bay, an encouraging response after weeks of frustration. But the argument in this story is that one performance doesn’t erase the questions raised by the earlier games.

If Minnesota ends up missing the playoffs, O’Connell’s handling of the offense and the quarterback position would face even closer scrutiny.

Remember what we heard last week. Dominique Foxworth said the Vikings should fire O’Connell and promote Brian Flores if the season ends in disappointment. Jason McIntyre put O’Connell’s chances of being fired at 20 to 25 percent.

That’s the backdrop for what ESPN Jets reporter Rich Cimini said.

Cimini said people around the league could see Jets general manager Darren Mougey giving up a high draft pick to acquire O’Connell.

The two went to college together and have stayed close.

Cimini also said he was told Mougey has spoken highly of O’Connell in meetings. And he wouldn’t completely dismiss the possibility.

Now, let’s be very clear about what this is.

It does not mean Minnesota wants to trade its head coach. It does not mean the Jets have made an offer. And it does not mean negotiations are happening.

It’s a reporter’s account of why the idea could appeal to New York, if O’Connell ever became available.

And his résumé explains that interest.

O’Connell was the 2024 NFL Coach of the Year. He has a career record of 49-26.

The criticism centers on his quarterback decisions.

O’Connell found stretches of success with Joshua Dobbs and Nick Mullens in 2023. He helped revive Sam Darnold’s career in 2024.

Minnesota also had successful offensive seasons in 2022 and 2024.

But the article argues that the other three seasons, including this one, have been much harder to defend, especially the quarterback play over the last two years.

That’s the evaluation being offered. And it’s why the Cousins reunion has consequences for the coach as well as the team.

If the offense improves and stays improved, O’Connell gets the chance to show what his system can do with a quarterback who runs it consistently.

As for a coaching trade, the article notes there have only been three of them over the past two decades, and highlights two examples.

In January 2023, Denver sent New Orleans a 2023 first-round pick and a 2024 second-round pick for Sean Payton.

In 2019, Tampa Bay acquired Bruce Arians from Arizona through a late-round pick swap.

And the suggestion is that O’Connell’s record and previous success could put his value closer to the Payton example than the Arians one.

A first-round pick for a head coach. That’s the kind of value we’re talking about.

But again, that’s an estimate, not an asking price.

For now, O’Connell is coaching a 7-2 team into one of the biggest games of the season. And what happens with this offense over the next eight weeks gives Minnesota something concrete to judge before any offseason scenario.

And remember what Kirk Cousins said after the Lambeau win. More than in 2022, he’s going to trust O’Connell, ask what the coach wants, and just go play. He said O’Connell is in his corner.

The coach and the quarterback are tied together now. If Cousins succeeds, O’Connell succeeds.

But there’s one problem that could complicate all of this: the running game.

The same article raises a concern that could limit any turnaround. The running backs.

It argues that O’Connell had considerable influence over offseason personnel decisions, describing him as a de facto general manager while Rob Brzezinski held the interim title.

That’s the author’s assessment of responsibility.

The decisions it highlights include bringing Aaron Jones back on a revised contract, and trading up for Demond Claiborne in the sixth round of the 2026 draft.

Jones handled a big workload during the month Jordan Mason was out. But the room as a whole hasn’t produced enough.

And Claiborne barely got on the field before going on injured reserve last week.

Here are the numbers through nine games.

Minnesota is averaging 108.6 rushing yards per game, 19th in the league.

Four yards per carry, 20th.

Expected points added per rush of minus 0.15, 27th.

And four rushing touchdowns, tied for last in the NFL.

But here’s the interesting contrast.

Only 13.2 percent of Minnesota’s rushing attempts are getting stuffed. That’s the fifth-best rate in football.

So the Vikings’ running backs are avoiding getting stopped at or behind the line of scrimmage. They’re just not turning that into big plays or touchdowns.

That suggests the problem isn’t the blocking at the point of attack. It’s what happens after that. The backs aren’t breaking the long runs, and they aren’t finishing in the end zone.

The article sees it as a talent issue that O’Connell and Frank Smith need to keep from limiting a playoff run.

Cousins can help the passing game. But Minnesota still needs the ground game to give him something reliable to lean on.

And it’s worth remembering: the Vikings tried to address this at the deadline. They were linked to Jonathan Taylor, Tony Pollard, and Derrick Henry. None of those moves happened. Taylor stayed in Indianapolis after the Colts turned down offers of second-round picks.

So this is the backfield Minnesota has for the rest of the season. Jones and Mason have to make it work.

So let’s put it all together.

Great news number one: Jauan Jennings is excited to face the team he refused to go back to, and with Jordan Addison out, O’Connell says the Vikings need plays from him in the passing game.

Great news number two: the Vikings signed DeAnthony Bell, a Super Bowl champion with 64 career games, to the practice squad as insurance for a secondary that had no safeties on the practice squad.

Great news number three: Kirk Cousins, with 22 touchdown passes, second in the NFL, and a chance to play a record 18 games, is entering the MVP conversation.

The twist: ESPN’s Rich Cimini says people around the league could see the Jets giving up a high draft pick for Kevin O’Connell, though there’s no indication Minnesota wants to move him.

And the problem: the running game ranks 27th in EPA per rush and tied for last in rushing touchdowns, even with one of the best stuff rates in the league.

This week gives us several things to watch in the same game.

Can Jennings turn his excitement into catches against his old team? Can Price and Felton build on what they did at Lambeau? Can the run game make the next down easier? And can the offense keep answering when San Francisco puts pressure on the scoreboard?

For O’Connell, the most useful response to all the outside noise would be another productive offensive performance, against a team he calls one of the best in football.
