VIKINGS JUST MADE A SHOCKING QUARTERBACK DECISION!

Vikings fans, one trade just fell through, and another door may have slammed shut.

A deal to send a receiver back to San Francisco is off, and now Kansas City is being floated as a new landing spot. The latest quarterback update could narrow Minnesota’s options even further. A new report on Jonathan Taylor changes the outlook. And an Eagles tight end has surfaced as a possible trade target.

Buffalo is Monday night. The trade deadline is the next afternoon.

So what move, if any, can Minnesota actually pull off?

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. This is the final weekend before the deadline, and there’s a lot to go through. Let’s break it all down.

Let’s start with the deal that almost happened.

According to the report, the Vikings had a deal in place to send Jauan Jennings back to the San Francisco 49ers.

A deal in place. Done, or close to it.

But it fell through, because Jennings decided he didn’t want to return to the Bay Area.

That’s a twist nobody saw coming. Players don’t usually get to block trades. But when a deal depends on a player’s willingness to go somewhere, things can fall apart at the last second.

Remember, earlier this week Adam Schefter reported that other teams had contacted Minnesota about Jennings, even though the Vikings weren’t actively shopping him. Now we know at least one of those conversations got very close to the finish line.

So now, with the November 10th deadline approaching, Minnesota has to decide. Keep Jennings, or see if another team makes a move.

And another team has entered the conversation.

Jordan Foote of Chiefs On SI included Jennings on his November 6th list of realistic receiver targets for Kansas City.

Foote noted that there was interest in Jennings around Kansas City last offseason, before he signed a one-year deal worth $8 million with Minnesota.

So the Chiefs liked him before. Could they come back now?

So far, his production in Minnesota has been modest. Six catches for 54 yards in six games, according to StatMuse.

Six catches in six games. One per game.

And remember, earlier in the season he was sitting at just two catches for nine yards. So it’s picked up a little, but not much.

But Foote raised a question that matters to both teams: how much of that comes down to where Jennings is lining up?

He’s spent 59.5 percent of his snaps in the slot. That’s his highest rate since 2023.

That alignment has given him chances to do damage after the catch. But Foote thinks his best role might be closer to the one he played in San Francisco.

And that’s where the Kansas City idea gets complicated.

Foote doesn’t see that role as a natural fit for what the Chiefs usually look for, even with only $3.3 million owed if a trade happens.

So Kansas City makes sense on paper, but maybe not in practice.

And then there was a much more blunt opinion.

Former San Francisco safety Donte Whitner weighed in on the November 5th episode of the Grit Code podcast.

He said he was fine with Jennings staying in Minnesota. But he argued that past production won’t protect a player if he’s averaging one catch a game.

Whitner compared Jennings’s current numbers to what you’d expect from a backup tight end. And he warned that Minnesota could eventually move on if that continues.

That’s harsh. But it reflects how quickly patience runs out in this league.

To be clear, that was Whitner’s opinion, not a report that the Vikings are ready to cut him.

And unlike the reported San Francisco deal, Kansas City is still just an analyst’s suggestion.

Now let’s get to the quarterback situation, because Saturday morning brought a much clearer answer, and it’s the decision that could narrow Minnesota’s options more than any other.

It’s about Daniel Jones.

NFL insider Tom Pelissero reported that the Colts received calls about their veteran quarterback, turned down the offers, and told interested teams they aren’t trading him before Tuesday’s deadline.

That’s a door slamming shut.

Jones had come up again and again in speculation about Minnesota. Rob Gronkowski endorsed the idea on Up & Adams. Mike Silver listed him among the possibilities.

But to be fair, Pelissero didn’t identify the Vikings as one of the teams that called.

Still, you can see why the idea got traction in the first place.

Look at where this offense ranks entering Week 9.

Twenty-seventh in scoring. Twenty-ninth in total yards. Thirty-first in passing yards.

Those numbers are why every quarterback name in the league has been connected to Minnesota.

Here’s where things stand at the position.

Kyler Murray started six of the first seven games. But a hamstring injury is the stated reason he’ll miss the Buffalo game.

Carson Wentz gets the start Monday night, one day before the deadline at 4 p.m. Eastern.

And Mike Florio sees the change as a possible soft benching.

Florio’s prediction is simple, and it might be the most important thing to watch on Monday night.

If Wentz plays well, Florio thinks that takes Minnesota out of the quarterback market.

But if the offense still can’t get going, he thinks the Vikings should try to add someone before time runs out.

And Daniel Jones was his preferred option in that scenario.

That’s why the Colts’ stance is such a real obstacle. Florio’s backup plan just got taken off the table.

Florio also discussed Kirk Cousins, who’s been playing well in Las Vegas while first overall pick Fernando Mendoza waits for his turn.

If the Raiders are ready to hand the job to Mendoza, a Cousins trade could bring back draft capital.

But remember what Tom Pelissero said earlier this week: Cousins has been playing like a top-10 quarterback by every metric, his teammates are rallying around him, and owner Mark Davis would be reluctant to break that up.

And there’s no indication that Las Vegas has made that decision, or that both sides would welcome a reunion.

Justin Herbert and Geno Smith have also come up in pundit discussions.

But Herbert looks especially unlikely, given the Chargers’ public reluctance to move players even while winless.

So the quarterback market keeps shrinking. Jones is off the table. Cousins is playing too well to move. Herbert isn’t available.

That puts a lot of pressure on Carson Wentz.

And all of this is happening with Minnesota at 6-1, first in the NFC North, largely because of its defense.

But look at how tight that division is.

Green Bay is in last place, at 5-3.

Five and three, and that’s last place.

That tells you everything about the NFC North right now. The Vikings have something worth protecting. And they’ll have very little time after Monday’s game to act if Wentz’s performance changes the plan.

Now here’s the twist out of Indianapolis.

The Colts may be telling teams to forget about their quarterback. But the outlook around Jonathan Taylor sounds very different.

In a Saturday morning report for The Athletic, Jonathan Jones said he’d be surprised if Indianapolis kept Taylor, regardless of how the Colts’ weekend game goes.

That’s a big shift.

Earlier this week, Adam Schefter said the Colts’ owner didn’t want to trade Taylor. Ian Rapoport said Indianapolis wanted a Christian McCaffrey-type package and hadn’t received one.

Now Jonathan Jones is saying he’d be surprised if Taylor stays.

And according to his sources, the Vikings and Giants remain the favorites.

The Seahawks, Packers, and Broncos are also in the mix. But Jones said he hadn’t gotten the impression that Green Bay or Denver would be willing to pay a premium.

So it’s down to two front-runners. Minnesota. And New York.

And think about that New York connection. The Giants just traded for J.J. McCarthy. They lead their division. And now they’re competing with Minnesota for Jonathan Taylor too.

The biggest question is what the Vikings would have to give up.

The article points to a first-round pick as the likely asking price.

To be clear, that’s an assessment of what Indianapolis may be holding out for, not a confirmed demand, and not an offer from Minnesota.

So why would Minnesota even consider it?

Because the running game is in worse shape than the raw yardage suggests.

Minnesota is around league average in rushing yards per game. That sounds fine.

But the Vikings rank 22nd at four yards per carry, and 27th in expected points added per rush.

Here’s what that means. Minnesota runs the ball a lot, so the total yards look decent. But each individual run isn’t doing much. The volume is hiding the inefficiency.

And remember, Minnesota ranks dead last in rushing touchdowns.

The case for Taylor is that a more efficient run game could help Minnesota sustain drives and take advantage of a defense good enough to carry this team deep into the playoffs.

The case for keeping the pick is that a first-rounder could help address the roster beyond this season.

And remember all those offseason needs: quarterback, running back, tight end, outside linebacker, middle linebacker, cornerback, and defensive line.

ESPN’s Adam Schefter also explained on his podcast why he’s watching Minnesota’s quarterback situation so closely.

Murray is hurt and hasn’t produced enough. Wentz is getting an opportunity. And the team has already moved on from J.J. McCarthy.

Schefter sees a defense good enough to win a Super Bowl, if the offense can do its part.

The article says quarterback discussions have taken place inside the front office. But it considers a move unlikely, partly because a new quarterback would have to learn the system on the fly.

That’s the same warning we’ve heard all week. Midseason quarterback trades rarely work.

And it named Daniel Jones and Kirk Cousins specifically, because both have spent time with Kevin O’Connell within the last three years. That familiarity was supposed to make a midseason transition easier.

But Pelissero’s update is why that familiarity with Jones no longer looks like a realistic path to a trade.

So the quarterback route keeps narrowing. And that makes the Taylor question even more interesting.

If Minnesota believes its best chance this season is to run the ball and lean on the defense, is that enough to put a first-round pick on the table?

Now there’s another way to help this offense, and it comes from the tight end market.

And here’s the timeline that makes everything so tense.

Once the Buffalo game ends Monday night, Minnesota will have roughly 16 hours to make any trades.

Sixteen hours.

If the offense struggles again, the Vikings could look for one significant move, or more than one addition if one of them comes at a manageable cost.

On Thursday, Trevor Ripley of Vikings On SI included Eagles tight end Dallas Goedert on his deadline board.

Goedert was among the remaining possibilities rather than one of the deals Ripley seemed to expect. But the name makes sense to talk about, with Josh Oliver out for the season and the offense looking for more ways to finish drives.

And Goedert’s red-zone numbers are impressive.

He has 37 receiving touchdowns in nine NFL seasons.

He set a career high with 11 last season.

And through six games this year, he has 26 catches for 258 yards and two scores.

Put those together, and that’s 13 touchdowns in his last 21 games.

Thirteen touchdowns in 21 games. From a tight end.

That’s the appeal. If Minnesota is looking for another receiving threat near the goal line, Goedert is a real weapon. And remember, the red zone has been one of this offense’s biggest weaknesses all season.

Plus, the Eagles are 2-6, with Jalen Hurts out for the season. That’s a team that could be selling.

But the Vikings already have T.J. Hockenson, and his role matters when you ask what kind of tight end they actually need.

At 29, Hockenson has 31 catches for 295 yards and three touchdowns through seven games.

Those numbers may not jump off the page. But he’s second on the team in receiving, behind only Justin Jefferson. And he was Minnesota’s leading receiver in the Detroit loss.

So adding Goedert would mean two receiving tight ends competing for targets in an offense that already can’t feed its stars consistently.

That’s why there’s another option worth considering.

If the priority is replacing what Josh Oliver brought as a run blocker, the Colts’ Mo Alie-Cox is another possibility mentioned in the article, and he fits that job more directly.

And remember what Dan Graziano reported earlier this week. If Minnesota makes a move to improve the running game, it could just as easily be an interior offensive lineman or a blocking tight end, not a high-profile running back.

A blocking tight end like Alie-Cox fits that description exactly. And he’d likely come at a much lower cost than Taylor.

To be clear, neither Goedert nor Alie-Cox is presented as a confirmed negotiation.

So the question for Minnesota is whether the offense would get more from another receiving option, or from a blocker who helps the ground game it already has.

That choice tells you a lot about what the front office thinks is really holding this offense back.

So here’s what I’m watching over the next 48 hours.

First, Jauan Jennings. He’s still in Minnesota after the San Francisco deal fell through. Kansas City is an idea, not a reported destination. Does another team make an offer before Tuesday?

Second, Carson Wentz. As Mike Florio said, a strong game could take Minnesota out of the quarterback market entirely. A weak one could send the front office scrambling, and with Daniel Jones off the table, the options are thin.

Third, Jonathan Taylor. Jonathan Jones says he’d be surprised if the Colts keep him, and the Vikings and Giants are the favorites. But the likely price is a first-round pick. Does Minnesota pay it?

And fourth, the tight end decision. If the Vikings go after a tight end, will it be a receiver like Dallas Goedert or a blocker like Mo Alie-Cox?

Buffalo comes first. Then the 16-hour window. Then the deadline.
