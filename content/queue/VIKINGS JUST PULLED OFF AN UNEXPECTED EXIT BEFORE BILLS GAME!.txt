VIKINGS JUST PULLED OFF AN UNEXPECTED EXIT BEFORE BILLS GAME!

Vikings fans, before the front office makes its final call at the trade deadline, you need to hear just how far these proposals go.

One analyst thinks the Vikings could take a massive swing at quarterback, for a former Pro Bowler. Another thinks Minnesota should go the opposite direction and trade away one of its stars. One writer is even floating a former MVP. And if the big deal doesn’t come together, there’s a Plan B whose last two games deserve a much closer look.

Plus, one name from the Vikings sideline is starting to come up for a head coaching job.

The Bills come to town on Monday Night Football. The deadline hits Tuesday. And the ideas on the table have never been bigger.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s break it all down.

Let’s start with the biggest swing on the board: Justin Herbert.

ESPN’s Aaron Schatz made the case for Minnesota going after the Los Angeles Chargers quarterback. And the numbers explain why the idea gets your attention.

The Vikings are 6-1. But they rank 29th in total offense, at 279.4 yards per game.

They’re second from the bottom in passing, at 165.6 yards per game.

Six and one, with the 29th-ranked offense. That’s the contradiction that keeps coming up.

The fit between Kyler Murray and Kevin O’Connell’s offense is already under the microscope, especially now that Carson Wentz is starting against Buffalo. And Schatz is asking a fascinating question.

Could O’Connell bring out the version of Justin Herbert that looked so dangerous early in his career?

Because that’s something Chargers offensive coordinator Mike McDaniel hasn’t been able to do this season.

Look at Herbert’s numbers. Five touchdowns, and a league-high 10 interceptions.

And the Chargers are the only winless team in the NFL, 0-7 through eight weeks.

That’s why there’s speculation that Los Angeles could start moving players with an eye toward 2028.

But here’s the thing. Justin Herbert is 28 years old. He’s been to two Pro Bowls. He’s spent much of his career in the top-10 quarterback conversation. Some evaluators have put his talent closer to the top five.

Teams rarely trade quarterbacks like that, even in a down year.

So what would it take?

The article puts the hypothetical starting point at three first-round picks.

Three first-round picks.

And it mentions Jordan Addison and Dallas Turner as examples of young players on rookie deals who could be part of the discussion.

Think about that. Dallas Turner is tied for third in the NFL in sacks. He’s becoming a star in Brian Flores’s defense. And Addison is one of the few reliable weapons this passing game has.

To be clear, that’s an estimate of what a deal might take, not an asking price from the Chargers.

And there are real obstacles.

Jim Harbaugh hasn’t entertained the speculation publicly. And a coach trying to win games with that locker room has very little reason to advertise a sell-off.

If the Chargers were willing to listen, general manager Joe Hortiz would be the one handling it. And the article argues that talks this big would likely stay private until there was an actual deal.

Minnesota’s case is simple. The defense is good enough to win now. And quarterback is the position where an upgrade could change the most.

The question is how much of the young talent around that quarterback you’d be willing to give up to get him.

Remember what we heard from Alec Lewis of The Athletic: the front office wants to supplement this team without compromising its ability to build through the draft. Three first-round picks, plus young players like Turner or Addison, would be the exact opposite of that.

Now flip that argument completely around.

Instead of bringing in a quarterback to help Justin Jefferson, what if Minnesota traded Justin Jefferson?

That’s what John Middlekauff of The Volume floated: sending Jefferson to the Kansas City Chiefs.

The proposal came after the Detroit game, where Jefferson’s frustration was visible.

He scored a touchdown. But five catches on nine targets for 33 yards is a quiet day by his standards. His longest catch went for just nine yards. And he didn’t speak to reporters afterward.

In his November 4th video, Middlekauff floated two first-round picks, a second, and a third as an example of what Minnesota might get back.

Two firsts, a second, and a third. That’s a franchise-altering haul.

His argument was that the 6-1 record, backed by a strong defense, was hiding how little the offense was getting out of Jefferson with Murray at quarterback.

And he isn’t the only one talking about Jefferson’s future.

Shannon Sharpe came at it from Jefferson’s side, on the November 1st edition of Nightcap.

He said he wouldn’t be surprised if Jefferson eventually asked for a trade. And he explained why.

A receiver who had been on a historic pace is now dealing with a second straight season of inconsistent quarterback play.

Sharpe used a 1,000-yard season to make his point. Spread over 17 games, that’s roughly 58 yards per game.

As a season total, a thousand yards still sounds good. But it’s a completely different standard when you’re talking about a player who was putting up historic numbers.

Remember, in O’Connell’s first season, Jefferson caught 128 passes for 1,809 yards and won Offensive Player of the Year. A thousand yards would be a big step down from that.

Sharpe also called Jefferson a team player who’s doing the right things. He’s not complaining publicly. He’s not making demands.

To be clear, Sharpe’s prediction isn’t a report that Jefferson has asked out. And Middlekauff’s proposal isn’t evidence of talks with Kansas City.

But when people like Shannon Sharpe are publicly wondering whether Justin Jefferson might want out, that tells you how much pressure is building on this offense.

And there’s also a scenario that starts with keeping Jefferson, and going even bigger.

In a November 3rd piece for FanSided, Zachary Rotman ranked Minnesota third among possible landing spots for Lamar Jackson, if Jackson and Baltimore can’t agree on an extension.

Lamar Jackson. A former MVP.

Rotman argued the Vikings should be the most aggressive team on his list, because they’ve settled for too many stopgap answers at quarterback.

Think about that history. One-year bridges. Short-term veterans. Patchwork solutions. Rotman’s point is that Minnesota keeps putting a temporary answer at the most important position on the field.

But he also acknowledged the trade-off. The draft picks involved, plus the size of Jackson’s next contract, could make it harder to build around him.

So that’s the choice these proposals put in front of Minnesota.

Do you move the star whose production has suffered? Or do you make an even bigger commitment to getting him the quarterback he needs?

Trade Jefferson for picks. Or trade picks for a quarterback who could unlock Jefferson.

Those are opposite philosophies. And both are being argued publicly right now.

But there’s another way to help this offense, and it starts in the backfield.

Indianapolis has already started moving players. Quenton Nelson to Seattle. DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. That’s why the Jonathan Taylor speculation keeps picking up.

But a Taylor trade isn’t guaranteed. Multiple teams are linked to him.

Remember, Ian Rapoport reported that the Colts want a Christian McCaffrey-type package, and haven’t received one.

And Mike Florio said the Colts’ position hadn’t reached a point where a deal could get done.

That’s why Florio brought up Tony Pollard as a potentially more affordable Plan B.

And Pollard’s last two games deserve a much closer look.

Over his last two games, Tony Pollard has rushed for 246 yards and five touchdowns, averaging 5.34 yards per carry.

Two hundred forty-six yards. Five touchdowns. In two games.

That’s a running back who is red-hot right now.

Through eight games, Pollard has 595 rushing yards, 14 catches for 70 yards, and six rushing touchdowns.

Six rushing touchdowns. That’s already more than he had all of last season.

And he’s on pace for a fifth straight 1,000-yard rushing season, after two with Dallas and two with Tennessee.

Five straight 1,000-yard seasons. That’s consistency.

And here’s the part that makes him especially interesting for Minnesota. He’s producing in an offense that has struggled a lot like Minnesota’s.

The Vikings are 27th in scoring and 29th in total yards. Tennessee has only one win. Pollard is putting up big numbers on a team where nothing else is working. That ability to produce in tough conditions is a big part of his appeal.

Now, to be fair, Taylor is still the bigger prize.

He won the rushing title with 1,811 yards in 2021. Last season, he led the league with 323 carries and 18 rushing touchdowns. And he’s nearly two years younger than Pollard.

But Pollard has a résumé of his own.

He made the Pro Bowl in his first 1,000-yard season with Dallas in 2022, when he scored a career-high nine rushing touchdowns. He ran for a career-best 1,082 yards last season. And he’s averaging 11 more rushing yards per game this year.

That makes him a credible alternative if the Taylor deal never comes together.

And think about the price difference.

Taylor would require a McCaffrey-type package. Pollard has been suggested at around a mid-round pick.

For a team that wants to protect its draft, a team that reportedly may not be able to absorb significant salary according to Dan Graziano, Pollard checks a lot of boxes.

And remember the number that matters most. Minnesota ranks dead last in rushing touchdowns. Pollard just scored five in two games.

And here’s why the timing matters so much.

The Vikings lead the NFC North at 6-1. But every team in the division is at least 5-3.

Read that again. Every team in the North is at least 5-3.

So a loss to Buffalo on Monday could leave Minnesota only half a game ahead of last place in the division.

From first place and 6-1 to a half-game cushion over last place. That’s how tight this division is.

The Bills come to Minneapolis on Monday night. And the deadline is the very next day, Tuesday, November 10th.

So if the price for Taylor stays out of reach, would you be happy to see Minnesota pivot to Tony Pollard?

Put your pick in the comments.

Now, while the offense is the focus of the trade talk, the man running Minnesota’s defense is getting attention of his own.

And this might be the most underrated stat from the Detroit loss.

The Lions ran for 230 yards against Minnesota. Two hundred thirty rushing yards.

And the Vikings still gave up only 19 points.

Think about how rare that is. When a team runs for 230 yards, it usually scores 30 or more. It controls the clock, wears down the defense, and dominates the game. Remember, Detroit had the ball for 35 minutes and 43 seconds.

And yet, Brian Flores’s defense held the Lions to 19 points. Bending, but not breaking. Getting stops in the red zone. Keeping Minnesota in a game the offense couldn’t win.

That ability to keep the Vikings in games, no matter what, is exactly why Flores’s name is now being floated for a head coaching job.

And it makes sense. Look at what he’s built. A defense allowing the fewest points in the league. Blitzing around 70 percent of the time. Pressuring Daniel Jones on 85 percent of his dropbacks. Players like Blake Cashman and Dallas Turner having career years.

Flores has been a head coach before, in Miami. And what he’s done in Minnesota is putting him right back in that conversation.

For Vikings fans, that’s a double-edged sword.

On one hand, it’s a sign of how special this defense is.

On the other hand, it’s a reminder that this window won’t stay open forever. If Flores becomes a head coach somewhere else next season, this defense might never look exactly like this again.

And that brings us right back to the deadline debate. If this defense is a once-in-a-while opportunity, with Flores, Cashman in a contract year, Van Ginkel’s deal coming up, and Harrison Smith at 37, how aggressive should Minnesota be right now?

So let’s put it all together.

The proposals have never been bigger.

Aaron Schatz floated Justin Herbert, at a hypothetical starting price of three first-round picks, possibly with Jordan Addison or Dallas Turner involved.

John Middlekauff floated trading Justin Jefferson to Kansas City for two firsts, a second, and a third.

Shannon Sharpe said he wouldn’t be surprised if Jefferson eventually asks for a trade.

Zachary Rotman ranked Minnesota third among landing spots for Lamar Jackson.

Jonathan Taylor would cost a McCaffrey-type package, and the Colts aren’t ready to deal.

Tony Pollard is the red-hot Plan B: 246 yards and five touchdowns in his last two games.

And Brian Flores is drawing head coaching buzz after holding a Lions team that ran for 230 yards to just 19 points.

But all of it comes down to this. Monday night against Buffalo, with Carson Wentz at quarterback. Then the deadline on Tuesday afternoon.
