VIKINGS' NEXT QB MOVE AFTER 49ERS BLOWOUT? Daniel Jones Is Back on the Market!

Vikings fans, that one hurt.

Thirty-five to ten. On national television. In Mexico City. Against the best team in the NFC.

The defense that hadn't allowed more than 23 points in a single game all season gave up 28 in the first half alone.

And now the questions are coming fast.

What happened to the Flores defense? What does this mean for Kirk Cousins's future? And why is the name Daniel Jones suddenly back in the conversation for Minnesota, at a price that's almost too cheap to believe?

Because a quarterback who's earned nearly $173 million in his career could be available to the Vikings for about $55,000.

Plus, a new analysis names three veterans Minnesota needs to start replacing in 2027. And George Kittle's pregame comments about this defense look very different this morning.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let's get into it.

Let's start with what happened Sunday night, because we have to face it.

The San Francisco 49ers beat the Vikings 35-10 at Estadio Banorte, in the first Sunday Night Football game ever played outside the United States.

San Francisco improved to 9-1. Minnesota fell to 7-3, with its third loss in four games.

This was supposed to be the ultimate test. The No. 1 offense against the No. 1 defense.

And the 49ers won it decisively.

It started on the very first drive. San Francisco went 72 yards in seven plays. Brock Purdy completed all four of his passes, and finished it with a 17-yard touchdown to George Kittle.

The Vikings did answer. They tied the game.

But the second quarter is where it fell apart.

San Francisco scored 21 points in that quarter alone. Purdy found Kittle for his second touchdown. He hit Mike Evans for another. And then, right before halftime, came the play that broke the game open.

Safety Ji'Ayir Brown intercepted Kirk Cousins and took it 18 yards the other way for a pick-six.

Twenty-eight to ten at halftime.

And think about that number. Twenty-eight first-half points. That's more than Minnesota had allowed in any full game all season.

After the game, Cousins took responsibility for the interception. He said the backside safety made a great play, and that he needed to sense him and move on through his progression instead of trying to force the ball in there.

That's Kirk. He owns it.

The third quarter brought another mistake. Justin Jefferson fumbled, forced by cornerback Deommodore Lenoir. San Francisco couldn't turn that one into points, but it killed any hope of a comeback.

And then early in the fourth quarter, the 49ers put the exclamation point on it. A 98-yard touchdown drive, with the crowd chanting "MVP" for Purdy, capped by a 6-yard Christian McCaffrey run.

Thirty-five to ten.

The numbers tell the story.

Purdy went 21 of 26 for 255 yards and three touchdowns. No interceptions.

Cousins went 26 of 38 for 235 yards. No touchdowns. One interception.

And here's the stat that stings the most. The Vikings came into this game allowing the lowest passer rating in the entire NFL, 69.5. Purdy finished with a rating more than double that.

So what happened to the defense?

Remember what we talked about all week. Flores's defense is built on chaos. Disguise, pressure, and forcing quarterbacks into fast, bad decisions.

But John Lynch told us the 49ers' plan before the game. Accept that the Vikings will create some negative plays, and just limit them. Let Purdy read the defense and get into the right play.

And that's exactly what happened. Purdy was surgical. He got the ball out fast, he found his tight end, and he made the Vikings pay for every blitz that didn't get home.

Remember also that the 49ers trained in Colorado Springs to prepare for the altitude. The Vikings stayed home and used their altitude chamber. Did that make a difference? We may never know for sure. But San Francisco looked fresh from the first snap to the last.

And Flores's perfect record against Kyle Shanahan? Three wins, exactly 17 points allowed every time?

That streak is over.

Now, let's keep perspective. This is one loss. The Vikings are still 7-3. This defense held the Lions to 19, the Bills to 23, and the Packers to 10 over the previous three weeks.

But losing three of four games is a trend. And the most important question this morning is about the quarterback position. Not just this season, but beyond it.

So let's talk about Kirk Cousins's future.

Cousins is expected to start for the rest of 2026. That's not changing after one bad night.

But what happens after this season still depends on what he does with the rest of this opportunity.

A recent article laid out the case clearly. If Cousins keeps playing well, he should become a priority for 2027 and possibly beyond. If he struggles, Minnesota would have to find another answer.

And before the game, the writer was firmly in Cousins's corner. He argued that Cousins is still a good quarterback, and might even be better now than when he first arrived in Minnesota in 2018.

The qualities he pointed to were Cousins's calmness and focus. Not an expectation that he'll suddenly stop making mistakes.

And that's an important distinction after Sunday night. Cousins made a mistake. The pick-six. But mistakes have always been part of the Kirk Cousins package. The question is whether the calm, efficient play around them is good enough.

At 38, he still has to prove he can sustain that level. But the article's prediction was that he'll be Minnesota's starter next year.

And remember the report from Mike Florio last week. The Vikings already expressed interest in keeping Cousins beyond this season, before he even played at Lambeau. And there are two big contract deadlines early in the 2027 league year: a $10 million guaranteed bonus, and nearly $80 million in 2028 guarantees that would vest a couple of days later.

So the stakes for the rest of Cousins's season just got bigger.

And interestingly, one possible alternative for 2027 was on the field Sunday night, on the other sideline.

Mac Jones.

Jones is Brock Purdy's backup in San Francisco. And after what Purdy did to the Vikings, with the crowd chanting "MVP," it's very clear Purdy is the 49ers' long-term starter.

That's why the writer considers it extremely unlikely that Jones would choose to stay in Kyle Shanahan's system beyond this season. The expectation is that Jones could attract significant interest in March of 2027.

And Jones offers a very different profile than Cousins.

In 2025, while J.J. McCarthy was struggling to find consistency in Minnesota, Jones filled in for an injured Purdy and used his time with Shanahan to rebuild his reputation.

He completed 69.6 percent of his passes for 2,151 yards, 13 touchdowns, and six interceptions. San Francisco went 5-3 with him as the starter.

At 28, Jones also has a lot more football ahead of him, assuming he stays healthy. The writer suggests another decade in the league is possible.

And his background adds to the appeal. New England selected him 15th overall in the 2021 draft. That first-round pedigree is something teams often value when they consider giving a quarterback a second chance.

To be clear, Mac Jones is an offseason possibility in this discussion. He's not an announced target, and the Vikings haven't made any decision.

But there's a more immediate quarterback possibility. And the financial details are the reason it's getting so much attention.

Daniel Jones.

Jones has been released by the Indianapolis Colts. And on Saturday, November 21st, Judd Zulgad of SKOR North predicted that Jones would clear waivers and become free to choose his next team.

And Minnesota has been that choice before.

When the Giants released Jones in the second half of the 2024 season, he joined the Vikings and Kevin O'Connell.

According to Zulgad, Minnesota planned to bring him back for 2025, to compete with first-round pick J.J. McCarthy for the starting job.

Jones ended up choosing Indianapolis instead.

So now the question is whether the Vikings would revisit that relationship.

And whether he clears waivers is a huge part of the math.

Here's why.

A team that claims Jones off waivers would take on his contract. That means $2,135,000 for the rest of this season. It would also inherit $38 million in non-guaranteed salary and bonuses for 2027, along with $10,510,000 in salary and bonus money that's guaranteed for injury and would become fully guaranteed next March.

That's a lot of money.

But Zulgad explains that if Jones clears waivers, all those obligations stay with Indianapolis.

And a team that signs him as a free agent would owe him only $55,556 for the rest of this season.

Fifty-five thousand dollars.

For context, before this season, Jones signed a two-year, $88 million extension with the Colts. He's earned nearly $173 million over his eight-year career.

But the number Minnesota would be looking at is that tiny remaining-season cost.

And the Vikings had just under $5.3 million in available salary cap space as of Saturday night. So the money works.

The article argues that Jones could be an inexpensive alternative to Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz behind Cousins.

And it questions whether Murray has shown enough to be trusted if Minnesota had to move away from its starter for any length of time.

Now, Murray was a bargain himself. Minnesota signed him for the league minimum, $1.3 million, while Arizona stayed responsible for about $35.5 million of his 2026 salary.

The Vikings have no obligations to Murray beyond this season. And the article considers a return highly unlikely, especially after Minnesota traded a third-round pick for Cousins earlier this month.

Injuries have also limited Murray's chance to change that assessment.

He missed his third straight game Sunday night with a hamstring strain. Earlier in the season, a concussion kept him out for more than three quarters of Week 1 and all of Week 2.

And when he has been available, he hasn't shown a strong fit in O'Connell's system, according to the report.

And Wentz? He had more success in O'Connell's offense last season, and in relief during Week 1 this year. But he led Minnesota to only nine points against Chicago in Week 2. And against Buffalo, he completed 8 of 25 passes with two interceptions.

So think about the situation Minnesota is in.

The starter is 38 and just threw a pick-six in the biggest game of the season. The No. 2 quarterback has missed three straight games with a hamstring injury. And the No. 3 quarterback went 8 for 25 the last time he started.

If anything happens to Cousins, the Vikings could be in real trouble.

And there's one more detail. Minnesota contacted Indianapolis about trading for Jones before acquiring Cousins. That was less than two weeks ago.

So the interest has been there.

But be clear: there's no signing to announce. This is a possibility, and a prediction from one writer.

So here's my question for you. Would that price make you want to bring Daniel Jones back? Tell me in the comments.

Now let's look beyond the quarterback position. Because another analysis asks where Minnesota needs to start developing rookie replacements in 2027.

The Vikings are contenders now. But the article argues that general manager Nolan Teasley still has to plan for aging players leaving before their departures become a problem.

It points to safety as an example of planning that's already underway.

Harrison Smith is 37 and likely playing his final NFL season. And Minnesota selected Jakobe Thomas in the third round of the 2026 draft to help prepare for life after the Hitman.

Thomas made his NFL debut recently and forced a fumble on special teams. The plan is already in motion.

But the writer names three other veterans whose roles need similar planning.

First: long snapper Andrew DePaola.

At 39, he's the oldest player on the roster. He's in his seventh season with Minnesota and his 12th in the NFL.

His résumé is outstanding. Three Pro Bowls. Two first-team All-Pro selections. Two second-team All-Pro selections. He's been a reliable part of the special teams since the day he arrived.

The argument for finding a younger replacement comes from the ages of the specialists around him. Minnesota has a 25-year-old kicker and a 26-year-old punter.

Developing a young long snapper alongside them could keep that trio together for years.

Second: edge rusher Kyle Van Noy.

Van Noy is 35, in his first season with the Vikings, and he's the NFL's second-oldest edge rusher, behind only Khalil Mack of the Chargers.

And this isn't a case of a veteran who has stopped contributing.

Van Noy has two and a half sacks while playing 35.5 percent of Minnesota's defensive snaps in a rotational role. His Pro Football Focus grade is 71.5, and his run defense grade is 78.9.

The question is who eventually takes over that contribution.

If Minnesota isn't confident in Chaz Chambliss, Bo Richter, Tyler Batty, or an edge rusher on the practice squad, the writer suggests addressing the position early in the 2027 draft.

And after Sunday night, when Brock Purdy had all day to throw at times, pass-rush depth might be an even bigger priority.

Third: Carson Wentz.

Wentz is 33. The article acknowledges that isn't especially old for a quarterback.

But the writer interprets his rough performance against Buffalo as a possible sign that physical wear has caught up with him.

The suggestion that Week 9 might have been his final professional appearance is the writer's opinion. It's not a retirement announcement.

The bigger recommendation is to draft a quarterback to develop, regardless of whether Minnesota brings back a veteran like Cousins.

And that keeps two ideas separate. Signing Daniel Jones would address a possible short-term backup need. Drafting a quarterback would be an investment in the future.

None of these rookie replacement ideas are announced roster decisions. But after a loss like Sunday's, these conversations get louder.

And finally, let's go back to George Kittle. Because what he said before the game looks very different now.

Speaking on Thursday, November 19th, Kittle described the confidence he saw throughout Minnesota's defense.

He acknowledged that the Vikings offense had struggled until Cousins came back, but said the defense kept performing the whole time.

What impressed him was that the defense's reputation wasn't built on a few early games, followed by an opponent figuring out the scheme. In Kittle's view, that hadn't happened.

He pointed to the pressure, the third-down effectiveness, and the chaos Minnesota creates.

He even compared this group's confidence to the Vikings team from two years ago, when Sam Darnold helped Minnesota win 14 games. That team finished one win short of both the NFC North title and the conference's top seed.

Kittle said he saw the same belief in this defense. In the scheme. In the ability to hit. In the ability to make plays that force opponents into mistakes.

He said Minnesota plays without hesitation, and called it impressive to watch.

And then he added a little uncertainty about his own experience. He said, hopefully it's fun to play against. To be determined.

Well, it was determined.

Kittle scored twice. Including the very first touchdown of the game.

And it turns out the 49ers found the answer to the defense Kittle said nobody had exposed.

That's the hard part to accept this morning. Before Sunday, Minnesota was 7-2, tied with Chicago and Detroit for first place in the NFC North. San Francisco was 8-1 and leading the conference. The 49ers were three-and-a-half-point favorites.

And they won by 25.

So where does this leave the Vikings?

There are two timelines running through this team.

The present: Kirk Cousins has to bounce back, and fast. This defense has to show that Sunday night was one bad game, not the moment the league figured it out. And the offense has to stop turning the ball over in big moments.

The future: Cousins's contract deadlines in 2027. Mac Jones possibly hitting the market in March. Daniel Jones potentially available right now for almost nothing. And a roster that needs younger replacements at long snapper, edge rusher, and quarterback.

And the rookie replacement analysis is a reminder that even a contender has to keep planning ahead.
