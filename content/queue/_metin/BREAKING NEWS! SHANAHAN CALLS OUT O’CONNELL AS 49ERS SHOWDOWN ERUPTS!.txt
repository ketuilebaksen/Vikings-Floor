BREAKING NEWS! SHANAHAN SOUNDS THE ALARM ON BRIAN FLORES AS 49ERS SHOWDOWN ERUPTS!

Vikings fans, the 49ers are worried. And they’re saying it out loud.

Kyle Shanahan just called Brian Flores more unique than every other defensive play-caller in the NFL. John Lynch called the Vikings a huge challenge, and explained exactly why this defense is so hard to deal with.

Meanwhile, Kevin O’Connell wants to give an explosive playmaker a much bigger role. Justin Jefferson revealed a secret weapon the Vikings have been using for weeks to prepare for Mexico City. Blake Cashman just got named the team’s midseason MVP. And a new report brings fresh details on Kirk Cousins’s future, with two contract deadlines that complicate everything.

This matchup in Mexico City has a lot more at stake than just two good teams meeting in primetime.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with what the 49ers are saying, because it tells you everything about how they see this game.

First, Kyle Shanahan.

Shanahan is one of the best offensive minds in football. And when he talked about Brian Flores this week, he didn’t hold back with the praise.

He called Flores more unique than the other 31 defensive play-callers in the league.

More unique than everyone else. That’s not something Shanahan says lightly.

And he emphasized that this isn’t a temporary approach Flores built for the last few games. Flores has committed to it throughout his time in Minnesota, just as he did in Miami.

And then Shanahan said Flores is as good as it gets at that style of defense.

As good as it gets.

That respect comes from a coach who has spent all week trying to find answers against it. When Kyle Shanahan says your defense is unique, he’s telling you he’s having a hard time figuring it out.

Then there’s 49ers general manager John Lynch.

During a November 19th appearance on KNBR, Lynch called the Vikings a huge challenge.

And his explanation went way beyond how often Flores sends pressure.

Lynch said Minnesota makes its defense look chaotic, while staying remarkably sound in its assignments.

Chaotic, but sound. That’s the hardest combination to beat.

Here’s why. Lynch explained that heavy pressure usually comes with a feast-or-famine element. A blitzing defense can create a big negative play. But committing extra rushers can also leave openings somewhere else.

That’s normally the trade-off. You blitz, you might get a sack, but you might also give up a big play.

What impressed Lynch about Minnesota is that it pressures the quarterback without routinely giving up the structural discipline behind it.

The chaos is on the surface. The discipline is underneath.

And Lynch gave credit to both sides of that. He credited Flores for how long and how consistently the Vikings have practiced this approach. And he credited the players for fitting the system and executing their jobs.

And that’s a direct answer to what Alex Boone said earlier this week. Remember, Boone dismissed most of Minnesota’s defenders as system players. Lynch, the man running the 49ers, said the opposite. He credited the players for executing.

So what’s San Francisco’s plan?

From the 49ers’ perspective, Lynch said the challenge is limiting negative plays, while accepting that Minnesota is going to create some.

Think about that. The 49ers’ general manager is basically admitting that the Vikings will get some sacks and pressures. The goal is just to survive them and stay on schedule.

And that puts a lot on Brock Purdy, before and after the snap.

Lynch explained that the 49ers often have a couple of plays called in the huddle, leaving Purdy responsible for reading what the defense is doing and getting the offense into the right one.

Against a defense that keeps changing the picture, the challenge is deciding which look to trust, and adjusting fast enough for everyone to execute.

Remember what Jordan Love said before Lambeau. More film on Flores just means more looks to prepare for, and no guarantee which ones he’ll use. Purdy is about to face the same problem.

Lynch praised Purdy’s ability to handle that responsibility smoothly. And he pointed to Purdy’s range. He can operate on time and in rhythm. But he can also create when the original play breaks down.

Lynch said both of those were on display in the win over Dallas, and described Purdy as playing at an extremely high level.

So the Vikings have to do more than just get into the backfield. They have to keep Purdy from escaping and turning a broken play into an improvised big gain.

That’s the matchup. Flores’s chaos against Purdy’s composure.

Now let’s turn to the Vikings’ side. Because Kevin O’Connell just made it clear he wants to use an explosive weapon a lot more.

Myles Price.

Price has spent most of his career making his impact on special teams. But against Green Bay, he showed what can happen when Minnesota gives him chances as a receiver.

Four targets. Four catches. Forty-nine yards. And that 38-yard touchdown on third-and-23.

That was a huge change from his production coming into the game. Before Lambeau, he had six catches for 32 yards across eight games.

O’Connell noticed. And during a November 19th interview with Paul Allen on KFAN, he made his intentions very clear.

He said the Vikings want to keep finding ways to get Myles involved in the offense.

And the important part is that this sounds like something Minnesota wants to develop long-term, not just an emergency response to injuries.

Let’s look at Price’s journey, because it’s a great story.

He came into the league in 2025 as an undrafted player out of Indiana.

As a rookie, he appeared in all 17 regular-season games. But all 139 of his snaps came on special teams.

Not a single offensive snap as a rookie.

This season, he’s played 123 offensive snaps, including 33 at Lambeau, along with 79 on special teams.

Minnesota’s receiver depth has made offensive snaps hard to come by. But while he waited, Price has been putting together one of the best return seasons in the NFL.

He leads the league with 262 punt return yards and an average of 17.5 yards per return.

His 86-yard punt return touchdown is the longest punt return in the NFL coming into Week 11.

And he’s returned 28 kickoffs for 818 yards, an average of 29.5, ranking third in the league in both kickoff return yards and average.

That’s elite. And the Green Bay game gave the coaches evidence that his explosiveness in the return game can carry over into the passing game with Kirk Cousins.

Remember, Cousins said he barely knew Price before that game. Teammates told him Price was the real deal and to trust him. Now O’Connell wants to feature him.

And the injury situation still leaves room for that.

Jordan Addison’s practice injury required thumb surgery. But Minnesota has chosen not to put him on short-term injured reserve.

That keeps open the possibility of him coming back sooner than the four-game minimum that injured reserve would require. The team is hoping he misses no more than the next three games, though that’s a hope, not a promised timeline.

Then there’s Jauan Jennings. He’s missed the last two games with a calf injury. But Minnesota is optimistic he can return Sunday night in Mexico City, against his former team, the 49ers. The team he refused to be traded back to at the deadline.

Even if Jennings plays, Addison’s absence still leaves opportunities for Price.

And look at the numbers side by side.

Jennings signed a one-year, $8 million deal. But injuries and inconsistent play have limited him to six catches for 54 yards on 10 targets.

Price now has 10 catches for 81 yards and a touchdown on 15 targets.

Those totals don’t tell you everything about their roles. Jennings does a lot of blocking that doesn’t show up in the box score, and O’Connell has praised him for it. But the numbers do show why Price has earned a closer look.

So here’s the question for you. Would you keep expanding Price’s role even after the receiver room gets healthier? Tell me in the comments, because this is becoming a real decision about how Minnesota uses its talent.

Now let’s hear from Justin Jefferson, because he revealed something this week that changes how you should think about the altitude.

Jefferson is looking at this matchup as the player Cousins targeted most at Green Bay. He caught nine of 13 targets for 74 yards. A season-high in catches.

And against San Francisco, that connection could decide whether the Vikings can sustain drives and keep pace when the 49ers make plays of their own.

Speaking to reporters on November 18th, Jefferson called it a big game. Sunday night. Two teams near the top of the league. And an opponent that’s been making impressive plays.

He said he expects San Francisco to be very difficult to beat.

But what stood out was how he wants Minnesota to approach it.

He talked about the defense. About the offense growing into what it’s expected to be. About teammates feeding off the all-around performance against Green Bay.

And then he brought the focus back to the next play.

Jefferson said he doesn’t want the Vikings getting caught up in the size of the matchup. Or the location. Or the final result. Or the urge to make one huge play that sets the tone.

He wants them executing one snap at a time, and playing freely.

That mindset matters, because the setting itself is a challenge.

And here’s the secret weapon Jefferson revealed.

Remember when we reported earlier this week that the Vikings weren’t leaving Minnesota early to acclimate to Mexico City’s altitude, while the 49ers went to train in Colorado?

Well, now we know why.

Minnesota installed an altitude chamber at its practice facility to help players get ready for Mexico City. And Jefferson said they’ve been using it for the past four or five weeks.

Four or five weeks.

So the Vikings didn’t skip altitude preparation. They brought the altitude to Minnesota.

Jefferson said the first few sessions made the difference very noticeable. He said he could feel it every time he went into the chamber, because the conditions were so different from what the players were used to.

He compared the feeling to Denver, where Minnesota got a taste of playing at altitude during the preseason.

And the chamber has let the Vikings get familiar with that feeling while continuing to prepare at home, with their normal routine, in their own building.

Jefferson said the team feels ready. And he credited the staff for preparing the players to play fast, without dwelling on everything surrounding the game.

His confidence was simple. They believe their lungs are ready, and they’ll be able to keep running.

That’s a big deal. Because if players are thinking about how strange the conditions feel, that’s one more distraction competing with their assignments.

For Jefferson and Cousins, the next step is carrying the timing they found at Lambeau into a completely different setting, against another strong opponent.

Now let’s talk about the defense, and the player one national writer just named Minnesota’s midseason MVP.

While the offense has gotten most of the attention since Cousins returned, Brad Gagnon of Bleacher Report put the spotlight back on Blake Cashman, naming him the Vikings’ midseason Most Valuable Player.

And the numbers explain why.

Cashman is one of only two players in the entire NFL with more than 75 tackles and at least four sacks. And he reached those marks in just eight games.

He also has seven passes defended, tied for the team lead.

Gagnon described the 30-year-old linebacker as the focal point of the league’s No. 1 defense.

Tackling. Pressure. Pass coverage. Cashman touches almost every part of this defense. And as the green-dot linebacker, he’s the one calling the plays on the field.

And here’s the part that makes Minnesota even more dangerous.

Cashman wasn’t on the field at Lambeau. He was in concussion protocol.

Ivan Pace Jr. filled in capably. And the Vikings still produced one of the most dominant defensive performances of the season. Eight sacks. Four takeaways. Ten points allowed.

That gives Flores proof that the system and the depth can hold up when a key player is out.

Think about that. The best defensive performance of the season came without the team’s defensive MVP.

But Cashman’s future is a big question. He’s scheduled to become a free agent after this season. And the article names re-signing him as a priority for Minnesota.

And he isn’t the only veteran whose next contract could shape what the Vikings do.

Here’s the new report on Kirk Cousins.

Mike Florio of Pro Football Talk reported that Minnesota has already expressed interest in keeping Cousins beyond this season.

According to Florio, that interest was communicated casually, in passing, before Cousins’s big return at Lambeau. Florio described it as a sign that discussions will take place.

That’s meaningful interest. But it stops short of a new agreement or settled terms.

And this is where it gets complicated, because of two contract deadlines.

Deadline number one: Cousins is due a $10 million guaranteed bonus on the third day of the 2027 league year.

Deadline number two: two days later, on the fifth day of that same league year, nearly $80 million in guarantees for 2028 are scheduled to vest.

Florio sees the first figure as manageable. But the second is something Minnesota can’t realistically leave untouched if it wants to keep Cousins.

Those two dates create a very narrow window, where the structure of a new deal matters just as much as the desire to keep the reunion going.

But Florio said the mutual appreciation between Cousins and the Vikings gives both sides strong motivation to find a way to keep it going, at least for 2027.

A revised deal could give Minnesota continuity at quarterback while it looks for a younger long-term answer.

And the article frames that as a chance to handle the next young quarterback’s development better than the J.J. McCarthy situation was handled.

Picture it. Cousins as the starter in 2027, with a young quarterback learning behind him, instead of being thrown into the fire. Remember, Cousins played that exact role in Las Vegas with No. 1 overall pick Fernando Mendoza.

To be clear, that’s a possible direction, not a quarterback move Minnesota has announced.

So the Vikings have two big contract questions involving established contributors. How to keep the centerpiece of Flores’s defense. And whether they can build a workable next chapter with the quarterback who just revived the offense.

But those are questions for later. Sunday night is now.

So here’s what I’ll be watching in Mexico City.

First, Myles Price. When he gets an offensive opportunity, does Minnesota keep finding ways to use his explosiveness beyond the return game? And can his special teams ability flip the field against San Francisco?

Second, Justin Jefferson and Kirk Cousins. Can that connection keep drives moving, without the offense pressing for one huge play, exactly the way Jefferson described?

Third, the altitude. After four or five weeks in that altitude chamber, do the Vikings still look fresh in the fourth quarter?

And fourth, the chess match. When San Francisco changes its call at the line, does Brian Flores still get the result he wanted from his original look? Can his disguised pressure disrupt Brock Purdy’s decisions, without giving up the openings John Lynch knows a blitz can create?

Those are the details that will decide whether Minnesota can build on Lambeau.
