Vikings fans, this is the game.

Sunday night. Mexico City. More than 7,000 feet above sea level. The 7-2 Minnesota Vikings against the 8-1 San Francisco 49ers.

The NFL’s most dangerous offense against one of the most dominant defenses of this century.

Kevin O’Connell himself says the 49ers might be the best team in football. They just put up 46 points and 584 yards on Dallas.

And Minnesota is walking in without Jordan Addison, possibly without T.J. Hockenson, and with a running game that’s tied for last in the NFL in rushing touchdowns.

So the question isn’t whether the Vikings can win this game. It’s who has to step up for them to do it.

Today on Vikings Floor, we’re breaking down the five Vikings who absolutely must deliver on Sunday night. And at the end, I want you to tell me who you think matters most.

Let’s get into it.

First, let’s understand exactly what Minnesota is up against.

Speaking to reporters on November 16th, Kevin O’Connell called the 49ers a very difficult opponent. And he said they may be the best team in football.

He pointed to their record. The film. All three phases. And the coaching.

He said Brock Purdy is playing as well as anybody in the league.

And then he named the players San Francisco has been able to get the most out of. Trent Williams. Christian McCaffrey. George Kittle. Deebo Samuel.

That’s one of the best left tackles of his generation. One of the most versatile running backs in NFL history. An All-Pro tight end. And a receiver who can line up anywhere, including in the backfield.

And the 49ers made themselves even stronger at the trade deadline. They brought back DeForest Buckner from Indianapolis. And they added Keenan Allen.

Now look at what they just did to Dallas. Forty-six points. Five hundred eighty-four yards.

The 49ers lead the NFL in offense.

On the other side, Minnesota’s defense is allowing 273.3 yards per game, the second-fewest in the league. It’s holding opponents to 14 or fewer points per game and fewer than 175 passing yards per game. Since 2000, only the 2002 Buccaneers and 2008 Steelers have done that, and both won the Super Bowl.

Unstoppable force. Immovable object.

But here’s the problem. Minnesota’s offense is still producing just 271.8 yards per game, second from the bottom.

Second-best defense. Second-worst offense. By almost the exact same number.

Thirty points at Lambeau was a huge step forward. But one game doesn’t fix a season’s worth of offensive problems. And against an offense like San Francisco’s, the Vikings will almost certainly need to score more than they have most of this season.

So who has to step up?

Player number one: Jauan Jennings.

This one has a personal angle and a football reason.

Jennings will be lining up against the organization he used to play for. The 49ers.

And remember what happened at the deadline. The Vikings had a deal in place to send Jennings back to San Francisco. It fell through because Jennings decided he didn’t want to return to the Bay Area.

He turned down a return to the 49ers. Now he gets to face them on Sunday Night Football.

On the November 17th edition of X’s and O’s with Kevin O’Connell, the coach confirmed that Jennings is excited for this one.

And O’Connell made a point of recognizing work that doesn’t always show up in the stat sheet.

He said Jennings brings it every week. And he said Jennings has handled important early-down assignments, jobs the coach believes very few players can do the same way.

That’s a reference to Jennings’s blocking. He’s one of the best blocking wide receivers in the NFL. On early downs, he’s essentially an extra blocker in the run game, sealing the edge, taking on linebackers, opening lanes.

But with Addison out, O’Connell said the Vikings need plays from Jennings in the passing game too.

And here’s why that matters so much this week.

Jennings knows the 49ers. He knows their defensive terminology. He’s practiced against their defensive backs every day for years. He knows their tendencies, their checks, their habits.

That’s an edge Cousins can use.

But motivation alone won’t produce catches. Jennings has had a quiet season in Minnesota. If he gets a chance on a big third down, he has to make the play. He has to give Kirk Cousins a reason to keep coming back to him.

If Jennings has his best game as a Viking against the team he refused to go back to? That’s a storyline that could define this season.

Player number two: Myles Price and Tai Felton.

Yes, I’m counting them together, because they earned it together.

Remember what happened before the Green Bay game. O’Connell explained that most of the game plan was already installed when Addison got hurt in a freak injury during a Friday throwing session.

Suddenly, a player the coaches had built plans around was gone for a huge division game.

And Felton and Price stepped up.

Felton caught a 24-yard pass on third-and-long to keep a drive alive.

Price caught the 38-yard touchdown on third-and-23. The play that set the tone for the whole game.

O’Connell praised both of them. But he also praised the response across the organization. Players and coaches adjusted, trusted the available receivers, and let them play.

And remember what Kirk Cousins said. He barely knew Price before that game. He knew his nickname better than his real name. But teammates told him early in the week that Price was the real deal, and to trust him.

He trusted him. And Price delivered.

Now they have to do it again, against a much better defense.

Here’s why they matter so much on Sunday.

San Francisco’s defense will have one priority: stop Justin Jefferson. That’s what every defense does. Detroit doubled him. Buffalo took him away.

So the question becomes: when the 49ers roll coverage toward Jefferson, who wins on the other side?

That’s Price. That’s Felton. That’s Jennings.

If those receivers win their one-on-one matchups, Jefferson gets freed up, and the entire offense opens up. If they don’t, San Francisco can sit on Jefferson all night.

Player number three: the running game, Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason.

This might be the biggest question mark on the entire roster.

Let’s look at the numbers through nine games.

Minnesota is averaging 108.6 rushing yards per game, 19th in the league.

Four yards per carry, 20th.

Expected points added per rush of minus 0.15, which ranks 27th.

And four rushing touchdowns, tied for last in the NFL.

That’s the problem in a nutshell. Minnesota can’t punch it in when it matters.

But here’s the interesting contrast. Only 13.2 percent of Minnesota’s rushing attempts get stuffed at or behind the line of scrimmage. That’s the fifth-best rate in the league.

So what does that tell you?

The blocking at the point of attack is actually working. The backs are getting past the line. They’re just not turning it into big plays. They’re not breaking tackles in the second level. And they’re not finishing in the end zone.

One analysis called it a talent issue that O’Connell and his staff need to keep from limiting a playoff run.

And the depth is thin. Rookie Demond Claiborne barely got on the field before going on injured reserve last week. The Vikings tried to add a back at the deadline. They were linked to Jonathan Taylor, Tony Pollard, and Derrick Henry. None of it happened.

So it’s on Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason.

And this week, the run game matters even more, for two reasons.

The first reason is time of possession. The 49ers’ offense is a machine. The best way to slow it down is to keep it on the sideline. Remember when this Vikings team was 6-0, holding the ball for around 40 minutes in some games? That’s the formula. Long drives. Short-yardage conversions. Keep Purdy, McCaffrey, and Kittle sitting on the bench.

The second reason is the altitude. At 7,280 feet in Mexico City, every player gets tired faster. And a defense that’s been on the field for 10 straight plays at altitude is a defense that starts missing tackles. If Minnesota can sustain long drives, it wears down San Francisco’s defense, and keeps its own defense fresh.

Remember how Lambeau played out. Jones scored a touchdown in the second quarter. Mason closed the game out with a series of hard runs in the fourth. That’s the blueprint.

Player number four: Kirk Cousins.

Obviously, the quarterback has to step up. But this game is personal for him too.

Because Cousins already faced the 49ers this season, as a Raider.

And it didn’t go well. Las Vegas lost 38-7. Cousins completed 22 of 32 passes for 216 yards, with one touchdown and three interceptions. His passer rating was 58.9.

That game was the low point of his Las Vegas season.

But now he’s seen that defense. He’s studied what went wrong. And he faces it again with a completely different supporting cast, and a head coach who knows him better than almost anyone.

And remember, he’s in the middle of a remarkable run. With 22 touchdown passes, he’s second in the NFL. He’s being mentioned in an MVP conversation that seemed impossible a month ago. And because he switched teams before having a bye, he’s on track to play 18 regular-season games, which would be an NFL first.

But here’s the thing about Cousins in this game.

He doesn’t need to win a shootout. He doesn’t need 400 yards.

Remember the stat. Over the last three seasons, when the Vikings get even league-average quarterback play, they’re 19-2.

That’s the job. Protect the football. Convert third downs. Avoid the turnovers that killed him against San Francisco as a Raider. And give this defense a short field, not a long one.

Remember what he said after the Packers game. More than in 2022, he’s going to trust Kevin O’Connell. Ask what the coach wants to do, and just go play.

Against the 49ers, the game plan will be designed to keep Purdy off the field. Cousins’s job is to execute it, one completion at a time.

And a big part of that will be getting Justin Jefferson involved. Jefferson had a season-high nine catches for 74 yards at Lambeau. If he has another game like that, Minnesota’s chances go way up.

Player number five: Brian Flores’s defense, and specifically, the pass rush.

This is the unit that has carried Minnesota all season. And Sunday is its biggest test yet.

At Lambeau, Flores cranked his blitz rate from about 60 percent on the season to 67 percent. He sent extra rushers on 25 of Jordan Love’s 37 dropbacks.

Seven different Vikings recorded a sack. Levi Drake Rodriguez had two. Dallas Turner, Andrew Van Ginkel, Kyle Van Noy, Eric Wilson, Jalen Redmond, and Elijah Williams each got one.

Eight sacks. Four takeaways. Ten points allowed.

But the 49ers are a completely different challenge, for three reasons.

Reason one: Trent Williams. One of the best left tackles in football. Green Bay’s offensive line crumbled under Minnesota’s blitz package. San Francisco’s line, anchored by Williams, is a different animal.

Reason two: Christian McCaffrey. Here’s the danger of blitzing two-thirds of the time. When you send extra rushers, you leave fewer players in coverage and fewer players to stop the run. A back like McCaffrey can punish an aggressive front with one cutback. One missed gap, and he’s gone for 60 yards.

And he’s also one of the best receiving backs in the league. Blitz, and Purdy can dump it to McCaffrey in space, where your linebackers have to make open-field tackles.

Reason three: the altitude. Flores’s defense lives on relentless pressure. Full-speed rushers, every snap. That takes enormous energy. At 7,280 feet, that energy drains faster. Remember, the 49ers trained in Colorado to get ready. The Vikings stayed in Minnesota.

Can the Vikings’ pass rush stay as relentless in the fourth quarter in Mexico City as it was at Lambeau?

That might be the single biggest question of the game.

And there’s one more factor. Blake Cashman, the green-dot linebacker who calls the defense, entered concussion protocol earlier this month. His status is something to watch all week. Without him, someone else has to communicate all those disguises and rotations.

The Vikings did add some insurance in the secondary. They signed safety DeAnthony Bell to the practice squad, a Super Bowl champion from last year’s Seahawks team, with 64 career games and two interceptions. He isn’t expected to play right away. But it shows the team is preparing for the long haul.

Now let’s zoom out. Why does this game matter so much?

Because the stakes are enormous for Minnesota.

The Vikings are in a three-way tie for first place in the NFC North, with Detroit and Chicago. And Detroit holds a tiebreaker after beating Minnesota head-to-head.

But the Vikings also have the cleanest path in the division. A 3-1 division record. A tiebreaker over Chicago. Only two division games left. And a Week 14 game against a Patriots team without Drake Maye.

San Francisco, on the other hand, has a solid cushion in the NFC West.

So a loss hurts Minnesota much more than it hurts the 49ers.

But a win? A win in Mexico City, against the best offense in football, on national television?

That changes everything.

As Nick Wright put it, if the Vikings go to Mexico City and it looks anything like what we saw at Lambeau, the rest of the NFC, and the rest of the NFL, will be on notice.

A win could move Minnesota into the conversation for the No. 1 seed in the NFC. And a lot of people would start calling them the best team in the league.

And there’s one more reason this game matters. Kevin O’Connell’s name has been floating in rumors this week. ESPN’s Rich Cimini said people around the league could envision the Jets giving up a high draft pick for him, though there’s no indication Minnesota wants to move him.

O’Connell’s best answer to all of that noise? A big win, with a productive offensive performance, against a team he calls possibly the best in football.

So let’s put it all together.

To beat the 8-1 49ers in Mexico City, the Vikings need:

Jauan Jennings to turn his revenge game into real production in the passing game.

Myles Price and Tai Felton to win their matchups when San Francisco focuses on Justin Jefferson.

Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason to sustain drives, control the clock, and finally finish in the end zone.

Kirk Cousins to avoid the mistakes that sank him against San Francisco as a Raider, and just hit the layups.

And Brian Flores’s defense to keep the pressure on through four quarters at altitude, without letting Christian McCaffrey punish them for it.

If all five deliver, Minnesota doesn’t just win. It makes a statement to the entire league.
