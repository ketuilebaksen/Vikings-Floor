MCCARTHY SPEAKS OUT AS VIKINGS TRADE SHOCKWAVES SPREAD!

Vikings fans, Minnesota is making a major change, and the stakes couldn’t be higher.

Carson Wentz will start against Buffalo on Monday Night Football.

Talk of adding another veteran quarterback is heating up, and trade updates could come at any moment. A key detail about Kyler Murray’s injury is fueling the debate over his future. And in New York, J.J. McCarthy’s latest comments are putting Kevin O’Connell in the spotlight.

The trade deadline is Tuesday. And behind the scenes, things are moving fast.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. There’s a lot going on, so let’s break it all down.

Let’s start with the decision that’s already been made.

For Monday night, Kevin O’Connell has made his call. Carson Wentz will start against the Buffalo Bills because of Kyler Murray’s hamstring injury.

And the timing of the announcement caught people’s attention. It came on Thursday, before Minnesota had even released its injury report for the Monday matchup.

So why sit Murray?

Ian Rapoport reported that the injury would limit Murray’s mobility, and that playing could make it worse.

That matters a lot for this quarterback. Murray’s movement is one of the biggest parts of his game. His legs have given this offense some of its best moments, including that 35-yard scramble against Indianapolis, still the longest run by any Viking this season.

A quarterback like Murray with a limited hamstring isn’t really Kyler Murray. And risking a bigger injury before a brutal stretch of the schedule wouldn’t make sense.

So the medical reasoning is clear.

But here’s where the debate comes in.

The announcement has sparked talk about whether this amounts to a “soft benching.” And Wentz’s earlier production helps explain why that idea has gotten traction.

Here’s a detail a lot of fans didn’t know.

Earlier this season, in his limited playing time while Murray was in concussion protocol, Wentz posted a passer rating 20 points higher than Murray’s. He averaged 0.44 more yards per attempt. And he threw three touchdowns with no interceptions.

Compare that with Murray’s five touchdowns and three interceptions across six starts.

Three touchdowns, zero picks, in limited action. Versus five touchdowns and three picks over six starts.

Those are meaningful differences. Remember, Wentz helped Minnesota beat Green Bay in the season opener.

But to be fair, Wentz’s sample is much smaller, and that has to be part of the comparison. A few quarters of good football isn’t the same as six full starts against six different defenses.

Still, Buffalo now gives us a much more immediate test of what Wentz can deliver.

And look at what follows. Green Bay and San Francisco, over the next two weeks.

Buffalo. Green Bay at Lambeau. San Francisco.

That’s a demanding stretch for a team trying to stay in control of the NFC North and compete for position across the conference.

O’Connell needs an offense that can sustain drives and finish opportunities. Wentz’s earlier numbers give Minnesota something encouraging to work with.

Monday will show how that translates against a real opponent.

Now, the question beyond Buffalo starts with another important detail about Murray’s situation.

Injured reserve is not currently under consideration.

Alec Lewis of The Athletic reported O’Connell’s comments from Thursday, with the coach describing the hamstring strain as a week-to-week issue, not a month-to-month problem.

That’s an important distinction.

Putting Murray on injured reserve would mean a minimum four-game absence. Minnesota isn’t planning around that right now.

So Murray isn’t facing a long-term absence. He’s day to day, week to week.

That’s encouraging. But it doesn’t set an exact return date, and it doesn’t guarantee he’ll be ready right after Buffalo.

Keeping him on the active roster lets Minnesota evaluate his recovery without committing to a longer absence.

But this has become part of the soft benching theory too.

Some people are suggesting the Vikings could use Monday as a trial run for Wentz, while keeping the option of bringing Murray back quickly.

And here’s where you have to be careful.

The decision alone doesn’t prove the injury was exaggerated. It doesn’t prove the medical explanation is covering up a performance decision.

Earlier this week, O’Connell said Murray remains the starter when healthy. That’s still his public position.

And there’s another detail worth mentioning. Murray stayed in the Detroit game despite the hamstring issue. He came back from the blue medical tent and finished the game. That’s raised some questions about this week’s change, but it doesn’t tell us how ready he is for the next game.

So here’s where the real complication could come in.

What if Wentz helps Minnesota beat Buffalo, and the offense looks more effective?

In that scenario, Murray’s medical clearance and the coach’s evaluation of the offense would both become part of the conversation about who plays next.

That’s when this gets really interesting.

But until then, there’s no confirmed permanent change at quarterback. Murray’s recovery decides when he’s available. Wentz’s performance could decide how hard the decision is once that happens.

Now let’s look at what’s happening on the trade front, because the quarterback conversation goes well beyond Wentz and Murray.

Rob Gronkowski has an answer to Minnesota’s quarterback questions. And it’s Daniel Jones.

During his November 5th appearance on Up & Adams, the former tight end supported the idea of the Vikings trading for the Indianapolis quarterback.

Gronkowski said he wasn’t sold on Kyler Murray, pointing to inconsistent performances and throws that Minnesota needs its quarterback to make.

He believes another veteran could be the answer.

And that suggestion comes right as Wentz gets ready to start, and right before a short turnaround to the November 10th deadline. If the offense doesn’t take a step forward on Monday night, the quarterback speculation could get even louder Tuesday morning.

But Gronkowski’s endorsement deserves a close look, alongside Jones’s actual production.

Because the numbers tell a very different story.

Through 308 passing snaps for Indianapolis, Daniel Jones has a Pro Football Focus passing grade of 46.

Forty-six. That’s a low grade.

He’s thrown for 1,542 yards, with five touchdowns and nine interceptions.

Five touchdowns. Nine interceptions.

He has six big-time throws, and 20 turnover-worthy plays.

Twenty turnover-worthy plays, compared with six big-time throws.

Those numbers raise an obvious question. Would Minnesota be getting a real improvement? Or just another quarterback with his own concerns about consistency and mistakes?

Remember, the strength of this Vikings team is a defense that doesn’t give opponents short fields. A quarterback with 20 turnover-worthy plays would put that defense in exactly the situations it wants to avoid.

So to be clear, Gronkowski’s endorsement is a recommendation, not a report that the Vikings are pursuing Jones.

Then there’s Kirk Cousins, another veteran connected to this conversation.

But Tom Pelissero’s latest assessment explains why a reunion could be difficult.

In a November 5th video for The Ringer, Pelissero said he expects Minnesota to evaluate every possible option.

And he said a Raiders loss to the Jets might have created a real chance for Las Vegas to consider moving Cousins.

But that’s not what happened.

Instead, Cousins delivered a big performance. And Pelissero described him as a top-10 quarterback this season by every metric.

He also pointed to teammates rallying around Cousins, and the return of the “Kirko Chop” energy, as reasons owner Mark Davis would be reluctant to break up that setup and turn to the rookie.

So here’s the irony. Cousins’s strong play in his late 30s is exactly what makes him appealing to Minnesota, and exactly what gives Las Vegas a reason to keep him.

The better he plays, the less likely he’s available.

Then there’s the backfield market, which has a completely different obstacle.

Indianapolis’s price for Jonathan Taylor.

Ian Rapoport reported on Good Morning Football on November 5th that the Colts want a Christian McCaffrey-type package.

Think about what that means. When San Francisco traded for McCaffrey, it gave up a big haul of draft picks. That’s the kind of price Indianapolis is asking for.

According to Rapoport, the Colts haven’t received that kind of offer. And Taylor is still someone they’d like to build around.

Minnesota is among the teams reportedly interested. But Indianapolis could wait until the final hours before the deadline for a package strong enough to change its mind.

Let’s look at Taylor’s current numbers.

He’s played 412 offensive snaps, with an overall Pro Football Focus grade of 62.8.

He has 610 rushing yards and eight touchdowns on 148 carries, averaging 4.1 yards per carry and forcing 19 missed tackles.

He’s also caught 21 of 28 targets for 202 yards.

Eight rushing touchdowns. For context, Minnesota ranks dead last in the NFL in rushing touchdowns as a team.

But at 4.1 yards per carry, Taylor isn’t averaging more than Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason, who are both around 4.2. The value is in the touchdowns, the missed tackles forced, and the threat he creates, not raw efficiency.

And for a McCaffrey-type package, Minnesota would have to decide whether that’s worth it, especially after Alec Lewis reported that the front office wants to protect its ability to build through the draft.

So the Vikings have options to evaluate. But Cousins’s situation and Taylor’s price show why identifying help and actually getting it are two very different challenges.

Now let’s turn to New York, because while Minnesota sorts through these decisions, a familiar face is drawing fresh attention.

J.J. McCarthy is getting ready for his second start with the New York Giants.

And his early success is bringing new scrutiny to the Vikings’ decision to trade him. His praise for Giants offensive coordinator Matt Nagy has added another layer.

Let’s look at what McCarthy has done.

In his first start, he completed 20 of 28 passes for 252 yards with no turnovers, against Houston’s talented defense.

He’s 1-0 as New York’s starter. He’s put together five strong quarters of football.

Now, coming off a bye, he’s preparing to face a Philadelphia team that’s 2-6 and dealing with injuries.

And here’s the part that’ll sting for some Vikings fans. The Giants lead their division.

So the 23-year-old quarterback Minnesota traded away has a chance to contribute to a team with something real to play for.

Speaking to reporters this week, McCarthy praised Nagy’s play-calling and his ability to adapt as a game develops.

He described Nagy as coming into games with a clear vision, and said how much he’s enjoyed watching Nagy adjust when the original plan needs to change.

McCarthy also talked about what an honor it is to play for him, and to execute alongside the other 10 players in the huddle.

Now, some people have read that praise as indirect criticism of Kevin O’Connell, especially given how McCarthy’s time in Minnesota ended.

And think about why that interpretation is getting traction right now.

Remember what we’ve talked about all season. Questions about whether O’Connell’s offense can adapt. Trevor Ripley of Vikings On SI said the system hasn’t shown much adaptability. Fans upset about the lack of creativity. A run-heavy approach that hasn’t been producing.

And now McCarthy is praising his new coordinator for exactly that: adapting during games.

You can see why people connected the dots.

Here’s the backstory. O’Connell strongly supported McCarthy in 2025, before backing away from that level of support. Then general manager Nolan Teasley traded McCarthy for a fifth-round pick in late September.

But to be clear, McCarthy didn’t explicitly criticize O’Connell in those comments. Calling it a deliberate swipe is an interpretation.

Meanwhile, back in Minnesota, the scrutiny on the offense keeps building. Murray’s struggles. His injury. And visible frustration from Justin Jefferson and Jordan Addison after the team’s first loss.

And this is where it gets really interesting.

ESPN’s Dan Graziano reported that Vikings coaches had already talked to him about the potential fallout, back in Detroit.

They told him McCarthy had matured significantly since the start of the 2026 offseason, and said they were happy he’d landed somewhere he could succeed.

But they also acknowledged something honest. They could look bad if McCarthy played well for the Giants while Murray failed to get Minnesota’s offense going.

The coaches saw this coming.

According to Graziano, there was agreement that McCarthy needed a change of scenery.

That explains the thinking behind moving on. But it won’t stop the comparisons as both teams keep playing.

And let’s keep perspective.

McCarthy has had an encouraging start in New York, not a full season of evidence. Five strong quarters is a nice beginning, not a verdict.

And Minnesota still has games left to find its offensive answer.

Those performances, over the rest of the season, will be a much clearer judgment of the decision than anyone’s reading of his praise for Nagy.

So let’s put it all together.

The Vikings are 6-1. But the quarterback conversation now stretches from Monday’s starter, to the deadline, and beyond.

Carson Wentz gets the immediate opportunity against Buffalo. And his earlier numbers, a passer rating 20 points higher than Murray’s, three touchdowns and no interceptions in limited action, give Minnesota reason for optimism.

Kyler Murray’s recovery is a week-to-week evaluation. Injured reserve isn’t being considered. And O’Connell still says Murray is the starter when healthy.

The trade options all come with serious questions. Daniel Jones has a PFF passing grade of 46 and nine interceptions. Kirk Cousins is playing so well that the Raiders may not want to move him. And Jonathan Taylor would cost a McCaffrey-type package.

And J.J. McCarthy’s strong start in New York, along with his praise for Matt Nagy’s adaptability, adds even more scrutiny to O’Connell and this offense.

But before any of that matters, Minnesota needs a better offensive performance against Buffalo.
