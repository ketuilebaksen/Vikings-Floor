Minnesota Vikings Target A Blockbuster Jonathan Taylor Trade

“There’s no time for fairness.”

That’s the line that’s been echoing around the NFL this week. And it’s aimed right at Minnesota.

Kyler Murray’s hamstring injury has opened the door for Carson Wentz. An offense averaging around 19 points a game has Justin Jefferson starving for the ball. And with the trade deadline just hours away, the Vikings are right in the middle of the Jonathan Taylor sweepstakes.

Tonight, Minnesota hosts the Buffalo Bills on Monday Night Football. Tomorrow afternoon, the deadline hits.

And what happens over the next 24 hours could reshape this entire season.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s break it all down.

Let’s start with the conversation that has everyone talking.

On a national panel this week, the question was put bluntly. If Kyler Murray ends up losing his job because of a hamstring injury, is that fair?

The answer was just as blunt.

Absolutely not.

But when you’re on a journey to win a Super Bowl, there’s no time for fairness. You put the best player out there to win.

And then came the part that really stings for Murray.

The argument was that if Murray had been performing really well, if the team felt great about him leading the way to a Super Bowl, he’d be right back in the lineup as soon as he was healthy. No questions asked.

The truth, as the panel put it, is that everyone is watching, and everyone can see this offense needs to be better.

So the hope, if you’re Kevin O’Connell, is that Carson Wentz gets in there and gives this offense a spark.

But there was a note of caution too. Nobody wanted to jump on the Wentz hype train. Nobody was pretending he’s done something that makes you feel amazing about him taking over.

The point was simpler than that. If you’re the Vikings, you have to do something to see if you can get more out of this offense.

That’s where Minnesota is right now. Not looking for a savior. Just looking for a spark.

Then former NFL cornerback Jason McCourty was asked the big question: could this injury-related move become permanent?

His answer: yes.

McCourty said Kyler Murray hasn’t played great. But he also challenged the coaching staff, saying Kevin O’Connell hasn’t done a good job of figuring out the best ways to use Murray.

And he said something a lot of fans have been feeling. When you watch the games, at times it just doesn’t look like a good fit.

He described two kinds of plays that keep showing up.

First, there are plays where nobody’s open, and Murray is forced to scramble around and make something out of nothing.

Second, and this is the one that drives fans crazy, there are plays specifically designed to go to Justin Jefferson. Play-action. The window is there. Jefferson is open. And for whatever reason, Murray doesn’t throw the football.

Think about that. The play works. The design works. Jefferson gets open. And the ball doesn’t come out.

That’s exactly the frustration we’ve been talking about on Vikings Floor all season. Remember the stat: Jefferson has had only one 100-yard game in more than a year. Remember Detroit: nine targets, five catches, 33 yards, a long of nine.

McCourty’s view is that for Carson Wentz, this is a great opportunity. He’s stepping into a team with a Super Bowl-caliber defense.

But you have to be able to score. As McCourty put it, you can’t average 19 points a game and think you’re going to get through the gauntlet of the NFC and reach the Super Bowl.

So what does Wentz need to do?

At the very least, throw the ball to number 18. Figure out where Jefferson is on the field, and give him a chance.

And McCourty believes that if Wentz goes out and performs well against Buffalo, there’s a real opportunity for him to take over the job.

But not everyone sees it as a quarterback problem.

Another analyst on the same panel, someone who has been watching Vikings offensive film closely for two months, gave a more measured take.

His first reaction? Simple. They’re struggling on offense.

But he said you can’t pin it on any one thing.

The offensive line hasn’t been playing well. Remember, Donovan Jackson is out for the season, undrafted rookie Joe Huber has been starting in his place, and Will Fries has been inconsistent.

The Vikings haven’t been able to get any consistent run game going. They rank 22nd in yards per carry and dead last in rushing touchdowns.

It didn’t help that Murray got hurt in Week 1, missed games, and then came back trying to find a rhythm midseason.

And they haven’t been good on third down. But as the analyst pointed out, that’s really a reflection of poor performance on first and second down. When you’re constantly in third-and-long, third down becomes nearly impossible.

Remember the Detroit game: three of 10 on third down.

He also said there are things to talk about from a scheme standpoint, things the coaches could potentially be doing better.

So his conclusion? There’s enough blame to spread around. You can’t put this all on the quarterback.

And then he made a point that every Vikings fan should remember.

With this defense, the offense only needs to be okay.

This doesn’t have to be an offense that puts up 30 points a game. Not with a defense allowing the fewest points in the NFL.

It just has to be better situationally. Better in the red zone. Better on third down.

And he believes Kyler Murray actually has a lot to offer in exactly those situations.

That’s the hard part, he said. There’s been so much structure in Kevin O’Connell’s offense, so much effort to get Murray to play within the structure of the play.

But he’d love to see something different. Put Murray in some quarterback run situations. Let him use his legs. Spread the field out at times, and let him be dynamic.

That’s interesting, because it lines up with what O’Connell himself said after the Detroit loss. He talked about shotgun runs, shotgun play-action, movement plays, and getting Murray on the perimeter. The staff knows what Murray can do. The question is whether they’ll lean into it.

And the analyst finished with a reminder of the stakes. This might be the best division in football, certainly the most complete when you look at all four teams.

Remember, every team in the NFC North is at least 5-3. Green Bay is in last place at 5-3.

In a division like that, the Vikings offense has to get better. And as he said, we’ll see if they make any moves.

Now, what actually happens inside an NFL locker room when an injury costs a starter his job?

This is where things get personal, because Jason McCourty lived through it himself.

He called it tough, but also part of the business.

And he told a story from his own career.

In his second year in the league, McCourty competed for a starting cornerback spot with Alterraun Verner, opposite Cortland Finnegan.

He won the job.

Then around Week 3 or 4, he fractured his forearm.

Verner stepped in and played well.

Four weeks later, McCourty was healthy. He had a meeting with his defensive coordinator, and the message was clear: Verner is playing really well, so we’re keeping him in the starting lineup. You’ll slide into a nickel role.

McCourty admitted he was angry. It hurt enough that one of the team’s veterans actually came over to talk with him about it.

That’s the reality Kyler Murray could be facing. He didn’t lose his job because of a bad game. He might lose it because his hamstring gave the coaches a reason to try something else, and that something else might work.

Wally Pipp. Drew Bledsoe. Every football fan knows how these stories can go.

That’s the unwritten rule of every NFL locker room. When you’re chasing a ring, there’s no time for fairness.

And that brings us to the other half of this story: the trade deadline, and the biggest name on the board.

Jonathan Taylor.

When McCourty was asked where he’d like to see Taylor end up, he named two teams. The Cincinnati Bengals, and the Minnesota Vikings.

His reasoning? Both teams have Super Bowl potential right now. And a player like Taylor can change the dynamic of an offense.

For the Vikings, McCourty said, it’s all about finding any kind of spark.

If Taylor can start moving the chains and doing damage in the run game, it opens everything up for Justin Jefferson, and it makes life a lot easier on the quarterback, whether that’s Murray or Wentz.

And that’s the logic that ties everything together.

Remember what we’ve talked about all season. Defenses are using two-high safety looks against Minnesota more than half the time. They’re doubling Jefferson. And they can do it because the Vikings’ run game isn’t scary enough to force a safety down into the box.

A back like Jonathan Taylor changes that equation.

Eight rushing touchdowns this season. Eighteen last season, leading the NFL. A rushing title with 1,811 yards in 2021. Nineteen missed tackles forced this season.

Put him behind this offensive line, next to Jefferson and Addison, and suddenly defenses have to pick their poison.

And the reporting says Minnesota is right in the middle of it.

Remember what The Athletic’s Jonathan Jones reported over the weekend. He said he’d be surprised if Indianapolis kept Taylor, regardless of how the Colts’ game went. And according to his sources, the Vikings and Giants are the favorites.

The Seahawks, Packers, and Broncos are also in the mix. But Jones said he didn’t get the impression that Green Bay or Denver would pay a premium.

So it’s Minnesota and New York at the front of the line.

But the price is steep.

The likely asking price, according to the analysis, is a first-round pick. And Ian Rapoport reported earlier that the Colts wanted a Christian McCaffrey-type package.

That’s a big ask, especially for a front office that, according to Alec Lewis, wants to supplement this team without compromising its ability to build through the draft.

And remember Dan Graziano’s report that Minnesota may not be in a position to absorb significant additional salary.

So the Vikings have a real decision to make.

Is a first-round pick worth it for Jonathan Taylor?

Here’s the case for it.

This defense is a once-in-a-while opportunity. Brian Flores is drawing head coaching buzz. Blake Cashman and Andrew Van Ginkel are on expiring deals. Harrison Smith is 37. Justin Jefferson is 27 and in his prime, but frustrated.

And the offense ranks 27th in scoring, 29th in total yards, and 31st in passing yards. Something has to change.

Here’s the case against it.

A first-round pick is how you build the next version of this team. Minnesota has needs next offseason at quarterback, running back, tight end, linebacker, cornerback, and defensive line. And a running back alone doesn’t fix an offensive line that’s struggling or a passing game that can’t find its rhythm.

There’s also a middle path. Tony Pollard has run for 246 yards and five touchdowns over his last two games, and he’s been suggested at a much lower price. A blocking tight end like Mo Alie-Cox could help the run game for even less.

But none of those names has the star power, or the potential impact, of Jonathan Taylor.

And here’s where tonight’s game becomes so important.

Even if Carson Wentz plays well against Buffalo, the other analyst on that panel isn’t convinced he’s the guy who takes Minnesota to the promised land.

But if Wentz does play well, he said, it raises a big question. Do the Vikings try to make something happen right before the deadline?

Remember Mike Florio’s prediction from over the weekend. A strong Wentz game could take Minnesota out of the quarterback market. A weak one could send the front office scrambling.

But here’s the twist. With Daniel Jones off the table, the Colts told teams they aren’t trading him, the quarterback options are thin.

So if the offense struggles again tonight, the most realistic move might not be at quarterback at all. It might be Jonathan Taylor.

Instead of replacing the quarterback, you give whoever is playing quarterback a weapon that makes his job easier.

And that analyst made one more important point. Putting all of this on Kyler Murray would be crazy if you actually turn on the film.

There are plenty of times Murray is playing well, he said, but there’s just not much there within the structure. The offensive line has struggled. There’s no dynamic run game at all.

So blaming the quarterback alone wouldn’t be accurate. But the reality, as he admitted, is that if Wentz plays well tonight, the noise will get louder.

It’s a week-to-week league. And this week might be the most important one of the season.

Here’s the situation in a nutshell.

Minnesota has an elite defense, one capable of carrying this team deep into January. But on the other side of the ball, the offense is struggling to produce, scraping together around 19 points per game.

When a franchise has Justin Jefferson, the most dangerous receiver in football, averaging 19 points a game is a real problem.

And in an NFC where other contenders are scoring big and loading up at the deadline, Seattle with Quenton Nelson, San Francisco with DeForest Buckner, Chicago with Jeffery Simmons, an offense that can’t score isn’t going to win in January.

That’s why Carson Wentz doesn’t need to play like a superhero tonight to shake up the depth chart.

All he has to do is find number 18, trust his arm, and give Justin Jefferson and Jordan Addison a real chance to make plays.

And that’s why Jonathan Taylor might be the missing piece, whoever ends up at quarterback.

So here’s what I’m watching tonight.

First, Jefferson’s targets, and more importantly, where they come from. Is Wentz throwing into the windows the play-action concepts are creating? Is Jefferson getting the ball in space?

Second, third down and the red zone. Those are the two areas every analyst keeps pointing to. If Minnesota converts on third down and finishes drives with touchdowns, the offense might be turning a corner.

Third, the run game. If Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason can’t get anything going against Buffalo, the case for Jonathan Taylor gets a lot stronger before tomorrow’s deadline.

And fourth, the quarterback conversation. A great Wentz game turns this into a real quarterback controversy. A rough one puts even more pressure on the front office to do something by tomorrow afternoon.
