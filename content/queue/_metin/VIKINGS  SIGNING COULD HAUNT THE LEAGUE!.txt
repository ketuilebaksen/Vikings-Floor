TYREEK HILL TO MINNESOTA?! Vikings Could Add The FASTEST Weapon In Football For Kirk Cousins!

Vikings fans, close your eyes for a second and picture this.

Kirk Cousins drops back at Lambeau Field. Justin Jefferson runs a deep crosser over the middle. Jordan Addison clears out the sideline. And on the other side of the formation, the fastest player in the NFL is already 10 yards behind the safety.

Tyreek Hill.

In purple.

That might not be a fantasy for much longer.

Here’s where things stand. Tyreek Hill is a free agent. He was released by Miami in February after suffering a dislocated knee and a torn ACL early last season. He’s spent this entire season rehabbing.

And now he’s close.

On November 10th, Hill’s agent, Drew Rosenhaus, went on The Pat McAfee Show and said Hill hasn’t been medically cleared yet, but he’s expected to be cleared in a few weeks. And he made it clear that any team that didn’t land a receiver at the deadline could be interested in Hill as a free-agent option.

So which teams have been linked to him? The Chiefs, 49ers, Patriots, Ravens, and yes, the Minnesota Vikings.

And here’s the part that makes this story special. Tyreek Hill has said, more than once, that he grew up a Minnesota Vikings fan. He’s said that if he could choose any team to play for, it would be the Vikings.

His childhood team. His dream jersey. A contender with Super Bowl ambitions. And a front office that just showed it’s willing to make a move.

Now think about what he could do in this offense.

All season long, defenses have played two-high safeties against Minnesota more than half the time, and they’ve doubled Justin Jefferson. It’s suffocated this passing game.

Put Tyreek Hill on the field, and that plan falls apart. You can’t roll coverage to Jefferson when the fastest man in football is running past your other cornerback. You can’t sit two safeties deep and still contain a player with that kind of speed. Something has to give.

And Hill’s speed doesn’t just help the passing game. It forces safeties to stay back, which opens running lanes for Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason.

Hill has more than 11,000 career receiving yards. He’s one of the most explosive players this league has ever seen. Pair him with a quarterback like Kirk Cousins, who throws on time, reads defenses before the snap, and gets the ball out, and you could have one of the most dangerous receiver groups in the NFL overnight.

Jefferson. Addison. Hockenson. Tyreek Hill.

Let’s be realistic, though. Hill hasn’t signed anywhere. He isn’t medically cleared yet. He’s 32, coming off a serious knee injury, and Kansas City is also in the mix. Nothing is done.

But if Hill gets cleared and Minnesota makes the call? This could be the move that turns a struggling offense into a nightmare for every defense in the NFC.

Keep your eyes on this one.

Now let’s get to everything else happening in Minnesota, because there’s a lot.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor.

Kirk Cousins is back. But Kevin O’Connell still hasn’t confirmed whether he’ll start at Lambeau.

And that’s not the only big question heading into Sunday’s game against Green Bay.

One of the Vikings’ most productive defenders is in concussion protocol, and his numbers show exactly why that matters.

Then there’s the warning. If this team misses the playoffs, O’Connell might have more to answer for than just his quarterback decisions.

Let’s break it down.

Let’s start with the defense, because this injury could matter a lot.

Kevin O’Connell confirmed Wednesday afternoon that Blake Cashman is in concussion protocol.

Cashman entered the protocol Monday evening after the Buffalo game, which leaves Minnesota with a short week to find out whether he’ll be available Sunday against the Packers.

And losing him would be a big blow.

Cashman has 76 combined tackles this season, and at least seven in every single game.

Remember, he has the longest active streak in the NFL of games with seven or more tackles. Seventeen straight games heading into the Colts game, and it’s kept going since.

Look at his recent run.

Against Buffalo, he had 12 tackles, one tackle for loss, and two passes defensed.

Against Indianapolis on October 25th, 12 tackles. That was the NFC Defensive Player of the Week performance, with two sacks and four passes defensed.

Against Detroit on November 1st, 10 tackles.

That’s three straight games with at least 10 tackles.

And if you want to know what he can do against Green Bay, go back to the season opener. He finished with seven tackles and half a sack against the Packers.

He followed that with 10 tackles, a sack, and a run stuff against Chicago.

The tackle totals show how often he’s involved. The plays behind the line and the passes he’s broken up show how many different ways he helps.

And remember, he wears the green dot. He’s the one communicating the calls in Brian Flores’s defense. When we talk about all those disguises, rotations, and pressures, Cashman is the one getting everyone lined up.

And this level of consistency took time to build.

Cashman came into the league with the Jets in 2019. As a rookie, he had 40 combined tackles and half a sack in seven games.

Injuries limited him early in his career. In 2020, he played just four games and recorded six tackles.

He spent one more season with the Jets before moving on to Houston in 2022. In his first year with the Texans, he had 26 tackles across 16 games.

Then the next season, in 14 games, he had 106 tackles, two sacks, and an interception. That was the year his production really took off.

And now Minnesota is getting that same dependable play from him in the middle of its defense. The Eden Prairie kid. The Gophers walk-on. The heart of this unit.

Green Bay’s situation has changed too. Josh Jacobs’s injury changes the picture for the Packers. But they still have explosive weapons, and they can make you pay if you lose track of someone or miss a tackle.

So while the quarterback question gets most of the attention, Cashman’s availability is one to follow all the way to Sunday.

Now let’s go to the offense, because the trade is official.

Minnesota sent a 2027 third-round pick to Las Vegas for Kirk Cousins. General manager Nolan Teasley reunited him with Kevin O’Connell on Tuesday.

But when O’Connell spoke to reporters on Wednesday, November 11th, he stopped short of naming Cousins the starter.

As Ben Goessling of the Star Tribune reported on X, O’Connell said it was too early to commit to the quarterback plan.

He said he was excited to spend every moment between now and Sunday getting Cousins comfortable enough to play. That’s the goal they’re working toward.

O’Connell pointed out that Cousins spent two seasons in his offense, so he already knows a lot of the core concepts.

But Cousins told a story that shows exactly how he sees this challenge.

When Minnesota traded for T.J. Hockenson in 2022, Cousins remembers thinking he didn’t envy him. Hockenson had a lot to learn in a hurry, and the coaches had to put in extra work to get him caught up.

Now, Cousins is the one trying to get up to speed in the middle of the week.

And he said the key is that you can’t play quarterback if it isn’t instinctive.

That’s an important point. All the work in the meeting room and on the practice field is about getting comfortable enough to just react to what he sees. If you’re still thinking through the offense after the snap, knowing the right answer on paper only gets you so far.

Remember Hockenson’s own description of playing with Kyler Murray, how he was sometimes still deciding whether to block or work deeper when the quarterback had already reached him. That’s what happens when instincts aren’t in sync. Cousins knows he has to get there fast.

And his first test would be against a good Green Bay defense, at Lambeau Field, just five days after the trade.

So what’s the backup plan?

If Minnesota decides Cousins needs more time, Kyler Murray is the likely next option, as long as he’s healthy enough.

The Vikings held Murray out against Buffalo, citing hamstring tightness. But ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported Wednesday that the move was also a soft benching.

So the theory a lot of fans had was right. It wasn’t just the hamstring.

Carson Wentz got that start, and you know how it went. Eight of 25 with two interceptions.

Buffalo’s defense hadn’t looked especially intimidating coming into the game, but it had the upper hand against Minnesota all night.

That performance is why Murray, if healthy, is projected to be the backup behind Cousins.

It’s also why Wentz looks like the most vulnerable quarterback if Minnesota decides to move on from someone.

To be clear, that’s a projection about the depth chart, not a roster move the Vikings have announced.

Now, whoever takes that first snap, there’s a reason so much attention is on Justin Jefferson.

You could see his frustration in the Week 8 loss to Detroit, and it carried into the Week 9 loss to Buffalo.

Against the Bills, Jefferson was targeted 11 times. He came away with three catches for 58 yards.

Eleven targets. Three catches.

That’s a lot of throws his way for an offense that still couldn’t get him going. So the problem isn’t just volume anymore. Remember when fans were demanding 10 targets a game? He got 11. It still didn’t work, because the throws weren’t there.

So when Cousins was asked about reconnecting with Jefferson on November 11th, his answer was telling.

He said he’s going to take it play by play and go where his reads take him.

He trusts O’Connell to design opportunities for Jefferson the way he did when they worked together before. But as Cousins put it, you don’t go chasing things.

That’s a veteran quarterback talking. Don’t force it. Don’t throw into triple coverage just to get your star the ball. Read the defense, and the ball will find him.

And Cousins said he knows exactly what kind of receiver he has. He said Jefferson tends to make the quarterback right when the ball goes his way.

That’s the trust that made 2022 so special.

And the concern goes back further than these last two losses.

According to StatMuse, over his last two seasons, Jefferson has 115 catches for 1,441 yards and six touchdowns in 24 games.

Over two seasons. Compare that to 2022 alone, when he had 128 catches for 1,809 yards.

Inconsistent quarterback play has been a big part of the conversation around that drop.

On the November 11th edition of First Take, ESPN’s Stephen A. Smith said he expects Jefferson to start looking more like the receiver people know, at least to some degree, now that Cousins is back.

But he was much harsher about what came before.

Smith called Minnesota’s handling of Jefferson an utter disgrace. He said he stood by his criticism of O’Connell, and argued that Jefferson hasn’t looked like himself over the last year and a half because of what he’s been getting at quarterback.

Cousins took a much narrower view of what he can do right away.

He said he’s grateful to join a talented, well-coached team with promise in the second half of the season.

The Vikings are 6-2 after back-to-back losses. But the goal Cousins talked about was simple: go 1-0 this week, and put in the work to make it happen.

For Jefferson, the first test is pretty simple too. When the offense gives him a chance to make a play, can the quarterback and receiver turn it into a catch?

And if the reunion doesn’t get the offense moving, the conversation gets a lot harder for O’Connell.

Minnesota is still 6-2 and in the NFC North race. But this team started 6-0, and it spent draft capital at the deadline because the offense wasn’t keeping up with a defense good enough to carry a contender.

Murray hadn’t gotten the offense on track. And the Wentz start against Buffalo made the need impossible to ignore.

You can see the logic behind Cousins. He knows the system, and he already has a connection with playmakers like Jefferson.

Fox Sports Radio’s Jason McIntyre said on November 11th that he liked the trade for both sides.

His concern was what happens if Minnesota can’t turn it into a playoff run.

He sees a defense good enough to be special. And he raised the possibility that Brian Flores could move on after the season.

Remember, Flores has been in the Assistant Coach of the Year conversation and drawing head coaching buzz.

If Flores leaves and the Vikings still don’t have an answer at quarterback, McIntyre argued that things could unravel quickly.

He put the chances of O’Connell being fired at the end of the season at 20 to 25 percent.

To be clear, that’s McIntyre’s estimate, not a report that the Vikings are considering a coaching change.

And his criticism went beyond the Buffalo game.

He questioned why O’Connell took a chance on Murray after Arizona benched him. He pointed to the injuries and uneven play. And he wondered whether the Buffalo switch was a soft benching, which Fowler’s reporting now suggests it partly was.

He also brought up Sam Darnold, even if you put Darnold’s departure on the previous front office.

McIntyre’s real question was why the offense still looks like this after O’Connell backed Murray.

And there was an even bigger hypothetical from Phil Mackey of Purple Daily.

In a November 10th video for SKOR North, Mackey said Minnesota should make the Cousins deal as a last big swing.

He expected it could cost a second-round pick, and argued that would be an overpay, because Minnesota could have had Cousins in March without giving up any draft capital, before it signed Murray.

The actual trade cost a third-round pick.

But Mackey’s bigger point was about what he’d do if the move fails.

He said he would consider trading Justin Jefferson in the offseason, to Kansas City, San Francisco, or another contender that needs a weapon and would give up two first-round picks plus more.

Mackey still sees Jefferson as a Hall of Fame-caliber receiver. His argument is that Jefferson is in the wrong system.

To be clear, that’s one commentator’s proposal, not a sign that Jefferson wants out or that Minnesota is shopping him.

But it shows what’s at stake over these final eight games. This isn’t just about the playoffs. It’s about the future of the head coach, the defensive coordinator, and maybe even the franchise’s best player.

So let’s put it all together.

Kirk Cousins is officially a Viking again, for a 2027 third-round pick. But O’Connell says it’s too early to commit to him as the starter at Lambeau.

If Cousins isn’t ready, Kyler Murray is the likely option if he’s healthy. ESPN’s Jeremy Fowler reported that the Buffalo decision was partly a soft benching. And Carson Wentz looks like the most vulnerable quarterback on the roster.

Blake Cashman is in concussion protocol, and his availability for Green Bay is uncertain.

Justin Jefferson had 11 targets and just three catches against Buffalo. Cousins says he won’t go chasing, he’ll trust his reads.

Analysts are already putting O’Connell’s job security on the table if this doesn’t work.

And out there in free agency, Tyreek Hill is getting closer to medical clearance, a childhood Vikings fan who could give this offense a whole new dimension.

Now the Vikings have to show that Cousins’s familiarity with this offense can turn into wins, while this defense is still playing at this level.
