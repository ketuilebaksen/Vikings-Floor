Most of the attention has been on quarterback and running back. But there’s another spot on this offense where one injury could force a very tough decision. The Jonathan Taylor sweepstakes took another turn over the weekend. And Kirk Cousins just answered a question about his future in Las Vegas.

Three targets. One quarterback reunion debate. And just hours to decide.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. The clock is ticking, so let’s break it all down.

Let’s start with the position nobody is talking about, but everybody should be: tight end.

Adam Patrick of The Viking Age floated New Orleans Saints tight end Noah Fant as a trade option Monday morning.

And the reason starts with Josh Oliver.

Oliver tore his biceps in Week 3 and is unlikely to return this season. That took away Minnesota’s best run-blocking tight end.

Now, T.J. Hockenson is still a major part of the offense. He’s second on the team in receiving, behind only Justin Jefferson. But here’s the problem.

If Hockenson has to come off the field, for an injury, a breather, anything, Gavin Bartholomew or Ben Yurosek could suddenly be Minnesota’s top tight end.

And both of them are getting their first NFL snaps this season.

Think about that. One Hockenson injury, and the Vikings would be asking a player with almost no NFL experience to take over a key role in an offense that’s already struggling.

That’s why Patrick thinks the Vikings should add a veteran before the deadline.

Hockenson can still be the main target at the position. But Minnesota needs insurance.

Patrick’s favorite option is Eagles veteran Dallas Goedert, the tight end with 13 touchdowns in his last 21 games that we’ve talked about here on Vikings Floor.

But Fant is another way to add experience if the Vikings can’t land a bigger deal.

So who is Noah Fant?

He’s 28 and in his eighth NFL season. And he has a real track record of producing when he gets opportunities.

He averaged 43.3 receiving yards per game with Denver across 2020 and 2021.

He averaged 35.7 with Seattle in 2024.

And last season in Cincinnati, he caught 34 passes for 288 yards and three touchdowns in 15 games.

This year in New Orleans, he’s playing more snaps, but his targets have come closer to the line of scrimmage. That helps explain why he has 12 catches for 96 yards, even though he’s already matched last year’s three touchdowns.

Three touchdowns on just 12 catches. That’s a red-zone weapon.

With enough opportunities, Fant could give Minnesota something like 30 to 35 receiving yards a game, plus another target in the red zone, where this offense has struggled all season.

Denver drafted him 20th overall in 2019. Spotrac puts his career earnings at $39.1 million.

But for Minnesota, the number that matters now is the two-year, $8.5 million deal he signed with the Saints this offseason. And there’s no guaranteed money in 2027.

That’s important. Remember Dan Graziano’s report that the Vikings may not be in a position to absorb significant salary. A player with no guaranteed money next year is a low-risk addition.

And Fant isn’t the only Saints tight end in the conversation.

Daniel Popper of The Athletic named Minnesota as a fit for both Fant and fellow Saints tight end Juwan Johnson.

Popper thinks Johnson is the better player. But Fant would cost less in draft capital, and he could help as a complementary receiving tight end.

But here’s the catch.

A receiving tight end is a different job from what Josh Oliver did. Oliver was a run blocker.

So if the Vikings need somebody to open running lanes more than they need another pass catcher, Colts veteran Mo Alie-Cox might be the better fit.

And remember ESPN’s Ben Solak proposal from yesterday: bringing back Johnny Mundt, a blocking tight end who played three seasons under Kevin O’Connell, for basically a sixth-round pick.

So the tight end market has options. Goedert and Fant as receiving threats. Juwan Johnson in between. Alie-Cox and Mundt as blockers.

The front office could make a smaller move here and still get meaningful help. It just has to decide which job it needs that player to do.

And that choice tells you a lot about what the coaches think is really holding this offense back.

Now, if Minnesota wants to help the run game by adding the guy carrying the ball, that brings us right back to Jonathan Taylor.

And the Colts’ situation just got even more desperate.

Indianapolis fell to 2-7 after losing to Dallas in Week 9.

Two and seven. That puts the Colts in a very difficult spot.

They can keep their best runner. But if they don’t expect to compete during his prime, draft picks could do more for their rebuild.

And they’ve already shown they’re willing to sell. Quenton Nelson to Seattle. DeForest Buckner to San Francisco. The question is whether Taylor is next.

Now let’s look at the Giants side, because that’s Minnesota’s main competition.

The reporting and the recommendations need to be separated here.

Josina Anderson reported Sunday morning that New York had cooled on Taylor because of the price.

That’s a big development. Remember, The Athletic’s Jonathan Jones said the Vikings and Giants were the favorites. If the Giants are backing off, that could change the whole picture.

But after the Giants beat Philadelphia that same day, SNY’s Connor Hughes said nothing was imminent.

He said the Colts wanted a first-round pick, and described New York’s choice as whether to offer its 2027 first, or put together a package of other picks.

And Hughes thinks the Giants should offer the first.

But to be clear, that’s what Hughes thinks the Giants should do. It doesn’t mean they changed course or sent Indianapolis that offer.

You can see why he’s pushing for it, though.

Against the Eagles, the Giants ran 31 times for just 80 yards. And J.J. McCarthy accounted for 22 of those yards himself.

That leaves 58 rushing yards from everyone else, on about 30 carries.

Hughes singled out Devin Singletary’s lack of production. But his point went beyond one running back having a quiet game.

He sees a physical Giants team that executes well in big moments and keeps finding ways to win. He sees potential in the passing game with McCarthy. But he doesn’t think the Giants are getting enough from the ground game to support their young quarterback.

Sound familiar?

That’s almost exactly Minnesota’s situation. A team winning games, with a run game that isn’t producing enough.

And even if Taylor is closer to the back half of his career, Hughes thinks he can still be an elite runner in 2027 and a productive one in 2028.

Then there’s the update from Mike Florio.

Writing for Pro Football Talk on Sunday after the Colts’ loss, Florio said the chatter had been going on for about two weeks.

And the belief around the league, he said, is that Indianapolis will take the best offer on the table as the deadline approaches.

He listed the Giants, Vikings, Seahawks, Broncos, and Packers. And he named San Francisco as an example of a team that could come out of nowhere.

So Florio expects a trade. But he didn’t say Minnesota is getting Taylor.

Here’s what a buyer would be getting.

Four hundred twenty-two snaps this season. An overall Pro Football Focus grade of 62.8. And 610 rushing yards with eight touchdowns on 148 carries.

He’s averaging 4.1 yards per carry and has forced 19 missed tackles as a runner.

He’s also caught 26 passes on 34 targets for 220 yards.

So Minnesota has to decide how much it values a back who can contribute as both a runner and a receiver, with no guarantee its offer beats the rest of the field.

And remember the Vikings’ running game problem. Twenty-second in yards per carry. Twenty-seventh in expected points added per rush. Dead last in rushing touchdowns.

Taylor’s eight rushing touchdowns would immediately change that.

But the price is a first-round pick. And the front office, according to Alec Lewis, wants to protect its ability to build through the draft.

The deadline is today, Tuesday, November 10th, at 3 p.m. Central, 4 p.m. Eastern.

And the clock gets even tighter at quarterback.

Kyler Murray, the former first overall pick, hasn’t gotten the offense going the way Minnesota hoped. And his hamstring injury is being evaluated week to week.

Carson Wentz started last night against Buffalo.

Going into the game, the thinking was simple. If Wentz played the way he did against Green Bay in Week 1, when he came in and threw three touchdown passes, he could make a real case to keep the job.

If the offense stalled again, Minnesota would have only a short window to decide whether another quarterback is worth pursuing.

And that’s where Kirk Cousins comes in.

After the Raiders lost 38-7 to San Francisco, Cousins was asked about the trade speculation.

As shared by Vegas Sports Today, he told reporters that it feels a little bit out of his hands, and that he’ll let the decision-makers make decisions and react accordingly.

He also said he loves being there, and he loves his teammates.

That sounds like a player who knows the decision belongs to the front office. It doesn’t sound like someone asking to leave.

And as of Sunday, there was a pretty clear obstacle to any reunion.

Before the loss, ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported on X that the Raiders hadn’t received any trade inquiries about Cousins and weren’t interested in moving him.

According to Schefter, the plan was to keep starting Cousins until the Raiders believe first overall pick Fernando Mendoza is ready.

What we don’t know is whether the San Francisco game changes that timeline.

Because it was a rough one.

The loss dropped Las Vegas to 4-5. Cousins completed 22 of 32 passes for 216 yards, with one touchdown and three interceptions. His passer rating was 58.9.

Three interceptions. A 58.9 rating. In a 38-7 blowout.

That’s a big drop from the player Tom Pelissero described last week as a top-10 quarterback by every metric.

So if the Raiders decide Mendoza is ready now, they could reconsider what to do with Cousins.

But that’s just a scenario. There’s no indication they’ve made the switch.

Former Vikings linebacker Anthony Barr has a different view.

On the Bent Out of Shape podcast on November 5th, Barr said Minnesota should make a trade to bring Cousins back.

His argument? Cousins already knows Kevin O’Connell’s system. He knows how to get Justin Jefferson the ball. And he was part of a team that Barr remembers playing at a high level on both sides of the ball.

And the history is real.

According to StatMuse, Cousins threw for 23,265 yards, 171 touchdowns, and 55 interceptions across 88 games in six seasons in Minnesota.

His passer rating over that stretch was 101.2.

A 101.2 passer rating over six seasons. And remember 2022, his first year with O’Connell: 13-4, a division title, and Jefferson’s 1,809-yard Offensive Player of the Year season.

That history is exactly why Barr thinks a reunion could help this offense on short notice. Midseason quarterback trades usually fail because of the learning curve. Cousins wouldn’t have one.

But Barr is making a case for the move. Schefter is reporting where the Raiders stood before Sunday’s loss.

For a deal to happen, two things would have to be true. Wentz’s start would have to leave Minnesota wanting a change. And Las Vegas would have to be willing to make one, before this afternoon.

That’s a lot to come together in a few hours.

Now let’s go back to the challenge that came before all of this: Josh Allen.

Before Monday night, the Vikings were trying to bounce back from the Detroit loss. And Dallas Turner explained to Darren Wolfson on Sunday, November 8th, why this matchup changes how he rushes the passer.

Turner said Buffalo’s offensive line protects Allen well, so even getting into the backfield is a challenge. And once you get there, you still have to bring down a quarterback with Allen’s size, awareness, and athleticism.

So Turner wanted to be more deliberate about the path he takes to the quarterback.

Think about what that means.

If you rush too far upfield and leave a lane underneath you, you might beat your blocker. But if Allen sees that opening, he can step right through it and turn your pressure into a big run.

Keep him in the pocket, and you still have to deal with that arm.

The goal is to make Allen beat you, without giving him a clear path to escape.

That’s a different mindset for a young pass rusher tied for third in the NFL in sacks. Turner’s instinct is to attack. Against Allen, he had to be disciplined.

And Brian Flores knows this problem better than almost anyone.

He faced Allen during his years in the AFC East, with New England and Miami.

Speaking to reporters on November 3rd, Flores called Allen one of the most dynamic players in the league.

He pointed to the missed tackles, the strength, and the arm. And he gave Allen credit for the work behind all of it.

Flores also laid out the risk and reward for Minnesota.

Pressure is part of how this defense plays every week. Remember, this is a unit that blitzes around 70 percent of the time, and pressured Daniel Jones on 85 percent of his dropbacks. Flores said he didn’t expect to move too far away from that.

But the Vikings couldn’t be reckless.

They could draw up a perfect pressure that sends a defender through untouched, and Allen could still make him miss.

That’s when it becomes a job for the whole defense. The next rusher has to stay in the play. And the coverage has to hold up while Allen buys time to throw.

And Buffalo knew there was a test on the other side too.

On November 5th, Allen said Minnesota is the No. 1 defense for a reason, and that it has given plenty of good offenses trouble.

He said he welcomed that kind of test in the middle of the season, when teams have a better sense of who they are. And he wanted to see how the Bills respond when something goes wrong and they have to adjust during the game.

That was the matchup. The best defense in football against one of the most dangerous quarterbacks in the league.

So here’s where everything stands, with just hours left.

The tight end question comes down to what Minnesota needs most from Josh Oliver’s replacement. A receiver like Noah Fant, Juwan Johnson, or Dallas Goedert? Or a blocker like Mo Alie-Cox or Johnny Mundt?

Jonathan Taylor could move today. The Colts are 2-7 and expected to take the best offer. But the price is a first-round pick, the Giants may have cooled, and nobody knows who will pay it.

And with Kirk Cousins, a reunion is still just an idea. He says it’s out of his hands. Schefter says the Raiders weren’t interested in moving him. But after a three-interception game in a 38-7 loss, things can change fast.

Three targets. One quarterback debate. And one afternoon to decide.
