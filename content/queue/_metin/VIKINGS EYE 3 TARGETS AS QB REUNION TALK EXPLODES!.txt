KIRK COUSINS IS BACK! Vikings SHOCK The NFL With Deadline Day Trade After Bills Disaster!

Vikings fans, it happened.

Kirk Cousins is coming home.

Less than 15 hours after one of the ugliest offensive performances of the season, Nolan Teasley pulled the trigger. On deadline day, the Minnesota Vikings traded for Kirk Cousins and brought the franchise’s all-time passer rating leader back to Minnesota.

From a Monday night disaster against Buffalo, to a deadline-day reunion nobody fully believed would happen. This is one of the wildest 24 hours in recent Vikings history.

So what happened Monday night? What did Minnesota give up? What does it mean for Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz? And can a 38-year-old Kirk Cousins save this season?

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Buckle up, because we have a lot to cover.

Let’s start with the game that made all of this happen.

The Buffalo Bills beat the Vikings 23-6 on Monday Night Football at U.S. Bank Stadium.

Twenty-three to six. No touchdowns for Minnesota. Just two field goals from Will Reichard.

And the quarterback situation completely fell apart.

With Kyler Murray sidelined by his hamstring, Carson Wentz got the start, and it turned into one of the worst performances of his 11-year career.

Wentz went 8 for 25 for 81 yards.

Eight completions. Twenty-five attempts. Eighty-one yards.

And three turnovers: two interceptions, plus a lost fumble on a backward pass in the first quarter that was thrown so high not even Justin Jefferson could get to it.

When you can’t complete a pass to Justin Jefferson because the ball is too high for him, something has gone very wrong.

Late in the fourth quarter, Wentz was replaced by rookie Max Brosmer, who went 6 of 7 for 58 yards in mop-up duty.

And the numbers around that performance were brutal.

Minnesota finished with just 178 total yards. Fifty-one rushing yards. No touchdowns.

Buffalo held the ball for 42 minutes and 32 seconds. Minnesota had it for 17 minutes and 28 seconds.

Remember when this offense was holding the ball for 40 minutes a game? Remember when time of possession was Minnesota’s identity? Two weeks in a row now, opponents have done that to the Vikings. Detroit held it for nearly 36 minutes. Buffalo held it for over 42.

And only one of Wentz’s 11 drives lasted more than five plays.

One.

Remember everything we talked about before the game? The hope that Wentz could provide a spark. That he’d find number 18, trust his arm, and give Jefferson a chance. Jason McCourty said there was an opportunity for him to take over the job. Mike Florio said a strong game could take Minnesota out of the quarterback market.

It went the opposite way.

And here’s the part that makes it even more painful. The defense showed up again.

James Cook carried the ball 31 times for 107 yards and a late touchdown. Buffalo ground it out. But for most of the night, Brian Flores’s defense kept the Vikings in the game.

Josh Allen threw his only touchdown with 44 seconds left before halftime. He rolled right, got hit hard by Andrew Van Ginkel, and still found tight end Keleki Latu wide open in the back of the end zone for a 25-yard score.

That’s exactly what Flores warned about before the game. You can draw up the perfect pressure, get a defender home, and Allen can still make the play.

Allen finished 21 of 31 for 234 yards with a touchdown and an interception, against what was reported as a career-high blitz rate.

And this was still a one-score game until about six minutes left in the fourth quarter.

Let that sink in. Despite eight completions. Despite three turnovers. Despite 178 total yards. Minnesota’s defense kept this game within reach until the final minutes.

That’s an all-world defense being wasted.

The Vikings fell to 6-2. The Bills improved to 7-1.

After the game, Kevin O’Connell was asked whether he’d thought about acquiring a quarterback before the deadline.

His answer? He’d defer to Nolan.

That said everything. The decision was now in the general manager’s hands. And the clock was ticking.

So let’s go to deadline day.

Tuesday morning, the entire NFL knew the Vikings needed a quarterback.

Reports came in fast. Minnesota was calling around on quarterbacks all day.

And that created a problem. When everyone knows you’re desperate, you lose leverage. Every team on the other end of the phone knows exactly what you need.

Mike Florio was told by a source that the Vikings were going to get a quarterback now.

And just after noon, ESPN’s Adam Schefter reported that the Vikings had identified Raiders quarterback Kirk Cousins as a prime trade candidate.

Then, about an hour and a half later, it was done.

The Vikings traded for Kirk Cousins.

So what did Minnesota give up?

The Vikings sent their own 2027 third-round pick to Las Vegas.

That’s it. One third-round pick.

And Minnesota keeps the other third-round pick it got from Philadelphia in the Jonathan Greenard trade back in April.

Think about that price. Remember all the talk about whether the Vikings should spend a first-round pick on Jonathan Taylor. Whether three firsts for Justin Herbert made sense. Whether Matthew Berry was right that Minnesota should give the Raiders a first for Cousins.

Instead, Teasley got the quarterback for a third.

And the Raiders’ side makes sense too. They didn’t need to trade Cousins. But at 4-5, in a crowded AFC playoff picture, they’re unlikely to contend this season. So they took the pick and handed the keys to No. 1 overall pick Fernando Mendoza.

Remember what Cousins said after the Raiders’ 38-7 loss to San Francisco. He said it felt a little bit out of his hands, and he’d let the decision-makers make their decisions.

Well, the decision-makers decided. And now he’s back in purple.

Now let’s talk about why this could actually work.

Because Kirk Cousins hasn’t just been surviving in Las Vegas. He’s been producing.

At 38 years old, Cousins is fifth in the NFL this season with 2,340 passing yards. He has 20 touchdown passes, second-most in the league. And eight interceptions.

Twenty touchdown passes. Second in the entire NFL.

Now compare that to what Minnesota has had. Kyler Murray had five touchdown passes in six starts. The Vikings rank 31st in passing yards and 24th in passing touchdowns.

Cousins, by himself, has thrown four times as many touchdowns as Murray.

And look at who he was throwing to in Las Vegas. Star tight end Brock Bowers, and then a receiver group of Tre Tucker, Jalen Nailor, and Cody White. Not exactly a loaded room.

Plus, the Raiders’ running game ranks 31st in the NFL, at just 71.3 yards per game. So Cousins has been putting up these numbers with almost no help on the ground.

Here’s the stat that might matter most.

Cousins has thrown 315 passes in nine games, the fourth-most in the league. And he’s only been sacked 11 times.

Eleven sacks on 315 attempts, with no running game.

Now compare that to Minnesota. Vikings quarterbacks have attempted 225 passes and been sacked 23 times.

Twenty-three sacks on 90 fewer attempts.

That tells you something huge. Part of Minnesota’s sack problem hasn’t just been the offensive line. It’s been how the quarterbacks handle the pocket. Holding the ball. Scrambling into trouble. Not getting it out on time.

Cousins does the opposite. He gets the ball out. He reads defenses before the snap. He throws on rhythm.

Remember what Jason McCourty said before the Bills game? There were plays designed for Justin Jefferson, play-action, the window was there, he was open, and the ball wasn’t being thrown.

Kirk Cousins throws that ball.

And remember the history. In 2022, Cousins’s first season with Kevin O’Connell, Justin Jefferson caught 128 passes for 1,809 yards and won Offensive Player of the Year. The Vikings went 13-4 and won the division.

That’s the connection this offense has been missing for two years.

And the familiarity is everything.

All season, the warning about a midseason quarterback trade has been the same: a new quarterback would have to learn the system on the fly, and those trades rarely work.

Cousins doesn’t have to learn anything. He ran this offense. He knows the terminology. He knows the protections. He knows O’Connell’s play-calling rhythm. He knows exactly where Jefferson is going to be.

That’s why Anthony Barr made the case for this reunion on the Bent Out of Shape podcast last week. Cousins knows the system, he knows how to get Jefferson the ball, and he was part of a team that played at a high level on both sides of the ball.

And look at his Vikings résumé.

Cousins started 88 regular-season games for Minnesota over six seasons, going 50-37-1. He ranks third in franchise history in passing yards with 23,265, second in passing touchdowns with 171, and first in passer rating at 101.2 among quarterbacks with at least 20 starts.

The highest passer rating in Vikings history. And now he’s back.

Now let’s talk money, because remember all the reports about Minnesota not being able to absorb much salary.

Here’s how this works.

Cousins comes with a very low prorated base salary for the rest of this season. That fits a team with limited cap space.

But the Vikings also inherit the $10 million guarantee in his contract for 2027.

And there’s a detail you should know about. Cousins’s deal has what’s being described as a poison pill: a 2028 salary north of $78 million that becomes fully guaranteed on the fifth day of the next league year.

Seventy-eight million dollars.

Obviously, Minnesota is going to either release him or negotiate a new deal before that kicks in. Nobody is paying a 39-year-old quarterback $78 million. But it means this is very much a one-season move unless both sides agree to something new.

So what happens to everyone else?

This is where it gets uncomfortable.

The trade means Kyler Murray has been demoted to No. 2 when he’s healthy.

And that may not be soon anyway. Tom Pelissero reported that Murray is dealing with a Grade 2 hamstring strain that would keep him out of Monday’s game and potentially beyond.

Carson Wentz is likely QB3 now.

And rookie Max Brosmer may be waived, with the hope that he clears waivers and comes back on the practice squad.

Think about how fast that happened. Two weeks ago, Murray was the starter and the coach said he’d stay the starter when healthy. Then a hamstring opened the door for Wentz. Then Wentz had one of the worst games of his career. And now Cousins has the job.

Remember the panel quote? There’s no time for fairness. The Vikings proved it.

It also means Minnesota’s 2027 quarterback situation is unsettled.

The Vikings could bring Cousins back on a new deal. Or they could try a blockbuster trade after the season. Or they could draft a quarterback in the first round next year.

That’s a question for the offseason. Right now, the question is the next eight games.

And here’s the full-circle moment.

Cousins has less than a week to get ready for his first game back. And that game is against the Green Bay Packers, at Lambeau Field.

Lambeau Field. The exact place where Cousins played his last snap as a Vikings quarterback in 2023, when he tore his Achilles.

He walked off that field injured, never knowing if he’d wear purple again.

Now he walks back onto it as Minnesota’s starting quarterback, in the middle of a division race where every team is at least 5-3.

You can’t write a better script than that.

Now let’s talk about what didn’t happen at the deadline, because it matters too.

Jonathan Taylor is staying in Indianapolis.

The Colts decided not to trade him. They turned down multiple offers of second-round picks and insisted on at least a first-round pick.

The Giants reportedly offered a second-round pick plus a little more, and the Colts said no immediately. And Daniel Jones is staying in Indianapolis too.

The Colts kept selling, though. On deadline day they also traded Keenan Allen to San Francisco and Josh Downs to Kansas City.

So the 49ers, who face the Vikings in a few weeks, added both DeForest Buckner and Keenan Allen from Indianapolis.

And Minnesota’s running game? It stays as it is. Aaron Jones and Jordan Mason will have to carry it, with Kirk Cousins hopefully opening things up through the air.

And here’s why that might be enough.

Remember the logic everyone kept repeating. Defenses were using two-high safety looks against Minnesota more than half the time and doubling Jefferson, because they didn’t respect the passing game.

If Cousins can make defenses pay through the air, those safeties have to stay deep. That opens up the run game for Jones and Mason. Instead of adding a star running back to open the passing game, the Vikings added a quarterback who might open the running game.

So let’s put it all together.

Monday night, Minnesota lost 23-6 to Buffalo. Carson Wentz went 8 for 25 for 81 yards with three turnovers. The offense managed 178 yards and no touchdowns. And the defense, once again, kept the game close until the final minutes.

Tuesday, with the whole league knowing they needed a quarterback, the Vikings traded a 2027 third-round pick to Las Vegas for Kirk Cousins.

Cousins brings 20 touchdown passes, the second-most in the NFL. Only 11 sacks on 315 attempts. Six seasons of history in this exact offense. And the highest passer rating in franchise history.

Kyler Murray drops to No. 2 when healthy. Carson Wentz likely drops to No. 3.

Jonathan Taylor stayed in Indianapolis.

And the first test is at Lambeau Field, where Cousins’s last Vikings chapter ended.

The defense is still elite. The weapons are still elite. Justin Jefferson is still in his prime. And now, for the first time in a long time, the quarterback knows exactly how to get him the ball.
