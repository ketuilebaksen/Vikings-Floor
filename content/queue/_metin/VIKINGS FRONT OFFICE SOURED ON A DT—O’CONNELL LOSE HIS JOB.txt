VIKINGS FRONT OFFICE SOURED ON A DT — COULD O’CONNELL LOSE HIS JOB?

Vikings fans, Caleb Banks just got some bad news, and a new report raises a much bigger question than you might think.

Meanwhile, Matt LaFleur didn’t hold back about Brian Flores’s defense. One national analyst thinks Kevin O’Connell could be fired, and he already has a replacement in mind. And we’ve got the latest on Kirk Cousins heading into the Packers game.

It’s the day before Lambeau, and there’s a lot going on inside this building.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s break it all down.

Let’s start with Caleb Banks.

While the Kirk Cousins trade took over the headlines this week, something else happened that a lot of people missed.

The 18th overall pick in this year’s draft was a healthy scratch against Buffalo on Monday Night Football.

Healthy. Not injured. Just not dressed.

For a first-round rookie in the middle of his first season, that’s a big deal.

And to understand why this matters, you have to go back to how Banks ended up in Minnesota.

The Vikings had already fired Kwesi Adofo-Mensah. But they hadn’t hired his replacement by draft weekend.

Nolan Teasley didn’t arrive until about a month later. This is his first year in Minnesota and his first season as an NFL general manager.

So it was interim general manager Rob Brzezinski who signed off on taking Banks out of Florida.

That’s an important detail. Banks wasn’t Teasley’s pick. He inherited him.

Now, Banks came in with real promise.

Some longtime draft analysts saw him as potentially the best interior pass rusher in the entire 2026 class.

But there was a catch. A foot injury had limited him to just three games in his final college season at Florida.

And the combination of his size and that injury history led a lot of those same evaluators to think Minnesota took him too early.

The injury put him behind in the offseason, through training camp, and into the preseason.

Conditioning and technique were both concerns going into Week 1. Banks himself acknowledged that back in August.

And the production hasn’t come.

Through seven games, he’s played 107 defensive snaps, with 12 combined tackles, one tackle for loss, and no sacks.

No sacks. No quarterback hits to speak of. On a defense that leads the league in pressure and blitzes around 70 percent of the time.

And remember, Banks left the Colts game shortly before halftime with a limp, which raised more concerns given his foot history.

Then came the report that really turned heads.

On Friday, November 13th, NFL reporter Tony Pauline said on X that the feeling in the front office had gone from questioning the selection to simply not liking Banks at all.

From questioning the pick, to not liking the player.

That’s a big shift in how the organization reportedly views a first-round rookie less than a year after drafting him.

Now, let’s be careful about what we actually know.

Making Banks inactive was a coaching decision, likely involving Brian Flores. And there’s still a path back into the rotation, on a defense that’s very hard to break into.

Think about who he’s competing with. Jalen Redmond is ranked among Pro Football Focus’s top 10 defensive tackles. Levi Drake Rodriguez has played well in his first season as a starter. Rookie Dominique Orange has shown flashes. Flores doesn’t hand out snaps; you have to earn them.

But the bigger concern is what happens if Minnesota gives up on Banks this early.

And that brings us to a pattern some people are worried about.

Remember, the Vikings took J.J. McCarthy 10th overall in 2024. Then they traded him to the Giants for a fifth-round pick.

McCarthy is 23. He’s now 2-1 as New York’s starter. And he’s played well enough to raise a real question: did Minnesota move on too soon, and get too little in return?

Teasley didn’t draft McCarthy, but he made that trade.

And then Minnesota turned around and spent a third-round pick on Kirk Cousins, a 38-year-old on a short-term deal. A quarterback the Vikings could have pursued in free agency a few months earlier, without giving up a pick at all.

Think about that sequence. A 10th overall pick traded for a fifth. Then a third spent on a quarterback who was available for free in the spring.

Banks and McCarthy play different positions. But in both cases, the question is the same.

How much time do you give a first-round pick to develop?

Banks is in the first year of a $21 million rookie contract. And with so little production on tape, his trade value is probably as low as it’s been.

That’s the danger. If the front office sours on him now, and he gets moved, Minnesota could end up selling at the absolute bottom.

Making the frustration public doesn’t help the team or the player.

The better path is to handle it in-house. Give Banks a chance to earn meaningful snaps, so the staff can see what it really has before deciding whether to move him down the road.

Defensive tackles, especially big ones coming off foot injuries, often take time. A rookie season interrupted by conditioning issues isn’t the same as a bust.

Now let’s get to what this defense has already done to Green Bay. Because it’s a big part of why Sunday is so interesting.

In Minnesota’s Week 1 win, the Vikings sacked Jordan Love four times, picked him off once, and held him to an 88.1 passer rating.

Four sacks. An interception. And a 39-22 win.

And Love still threw for nearly 400 yards in that game. So it was a game of big plays and big mistakes. And Minnesota’s defense created the mistakes when it mattered.

On Thursday, November 12th, Matt LaFleur talked about Flores’s defense, and he didn’t hold back.

He said what stands out is how consistently it makes offenses play from behind the sticks.

In his view, it’s hard enough just to score against Minnesota, let alone move the ball consistently.

And he said he’s rarely seen so many third-and-long situations this far into a season.

Think about that coming from an opposing head coach. He’s basically saying the Vikings’ defense wins first and second down so consistently that offenses are stuck in third-and-long all game.

That’s where the pressure forces mistakes. Third-and-8. Third-and-11. That’s when Flores dials up the blitz, disguises the coverage, and quarterbacks start making bad decisions.

And that’s exactly why LaFleur is stressing a clean game heading into Sunday’s rematch at Lambeau on November 15th.

Both teams need this one in a tight NFC North race. Remember the numbers: a Vikings win pushes their playoff chances to about 90 percent. A loss drops them to 68.

But LaFleur doesn’t think the opener tells you everything about the rematch.

He said teams change over the course of a season, and Week 1 can be especially unpredictable.

The atmosphere in Minnesota made communication difficult in the opener, and Green Bay’s offense didn’t always have everyone on the same page.

That’s a credit to U.S. Bank Stadium, one of the loudest buildings in football.

But this time, LaFleur said the communication pressure is on his defense too.

All 11 defenders have to know what they’re doing. And with a different quarterback running Minnesota’s offense, Green Bay could see some wrinkles it hasn’t prepared for.

That’s an interesting point. Green Bay has film on Kyler Murray. It has film on Carson Wentz. But Kirk Cousins in this offense, in 2026? That’s something new. The Packers will have to rely on old tape from Cousins’s Vikings days and his Las Vegas film.

Jordan Love explained the other side of that problem on Wednesday, November 11th.

In the opener, he had to study film from last season, because he didn’t know what Brian Flores would bring.

Now he has more film to help him recognize what he’s seeing. But there are also more looks to prepare for, and no guarantee which ones Flores will use on Sunday.

That’s the genius of a Flores defense. The more film you have, the more possibilities you have to prepare for. More information doesn’t necessarily mean more clarity.

And remember, Love has been red-hot: five wins in his last six games, about 1,700 passing yards, 28 completions of 20-plus yards, and 15 total touchdowns. This is a chess match between one of the hottest quarterbacks in football and one of the most creative defensive minds in the game.

And that brings us to the coaching debate. Because ESPN’s Dominique Foxworth took the Flores conversation somewhere else entirely.

First, the context.

Minnesota traded a 2027 third-round pick to the Raiders for Cousins before the 4 p.m. Eastern deadline on November 10th.

Kyler Murray and Carson Wentz hadn’t gotten the offense playing consistently. And the hope is that Cousins can get it going fast enough to reach the playoffs.

Now, on the November 12th edition of This Is Football, Foxworth made a bold argument.

He said that if the Vikings miss the playoffs, or go out in disappointing fashion, they should fire Kevin O’Connell and promote Brian Flores.

Fire O’Connell. Promote Flores.

His case starts with O’Connell’s reputation for getting the best out of quarterbacks.

Foxworth pointed to Sam Darnold as the success story. O’Connell turned Darnold’s career around.

But then he said McCarthy playing well elsewhere doesn’t reflect well on Minnesota.

And he argued that the Vikings knew what they were getting in Murray, but didn’t get the most out of him.

So in Foxworth’s view, the very thing O’Connell is supposed to be great at, developing quarterbacks, hasn’t worked lately.

Meanwhile, he believes the defense is what has kept this team competitive.

But Foxworth also made an important distinction. He doesn’t think O’Connell is a bad coach.

His argument is more about what’s working and what isn’t.

He used Houston as an example of a talented defense that can still take a step back. And he argued that Minnesota under Flores has held up without looking like it would collapse if any one player went down.

That’s a big compliment to Flores. Think about it. Harrison Smith is 37. Blake Cashman is in concussion protocol. Caleb Banks is a healthy scratch. Players come and go, and this defense keeps performing. That’s coaching. That’s a system.

And Foxworth isn’t alone.

Jason McIntyre took a different approach on Fox Sports Radio on November 11th.

He likes the Cousins trade for both sides. But he put the chances of O’Connell being fired at the end of the season at 20 to 25 percent.

His concern is that Minnesota could waste what he called a potentially generational defense, lose Flores after the season, and still be searching for a long-term answer at quarterback.

That’s the nightmare scenario for Vikings fans. A generational defense, and nothing to show for it.

Now, to be very clear, those are two outside opinions. They are not reports that the Vikings are considering a coaching change.

But both of them put the pressure in the same place.

Can the offense finally do enough to help the defense carry this team into January?

And here’s the twist in all of this. Flores’s potential head coaching future works both ways.

If he gets head coaching interest from other teams after the season, Minnesota could lose him. That’s McIntyre’s fear.

But Foxworth’s idea is that Minnesota could keep him, by making him the head coach.

Either way, the next eight games could decide the future of both coaches.

If the offense can turn things around, Justin Jefferson is probably the first place we’ll see it.

His frustration was noticeable in the Week 8 loss to Detroit, and it carried into the Week 9 loss to Buffalo.

Against the Bills, the Vikings targeted him 11 times, and he came away with just three catches for 58 yards.

Over his last two seasons, according to StatMuse, Jefferson has 115 catches for 1,441 yards and six touchdowns in 24 games. Compare that to his 2022 season alone, with Cousins at quarterback: 128 catches for 1,809 yards.

That’s why this reunion matters so much. Jefferson averaged roughly 99 receiving yards per game with Cousins.

And Cousins already set the tone this week. He said he’ll take it play by play, go where his reads take him, and trust O’Connell to design opportunities for Jefferson. He said you don’t go chasing things, and that Jefferson tends to make the quarterback right when the ball comes his way.

And when Cousins was introduced, O’Connell said something that’s going to be tested right away on Sunday: when there’s a completion to be had, Cousins will find it.

If that’s true, Jefferson should benefit first.

And if it isn’t, the coaching conversation is only going to get louder.

So let’s put it all together.

Caleb Banks, the 18th overall pick, was a healthy scratch against Buffalo. Tony Pauline reports that the front office has gone from questioning the pick to not liking him at all. And with McCarthy now 2-1 in New York, there are real questions about how patient this organization is with its first-round picks.

And it all comes back to Sunday. Kirk Cousins. Justin Jefferson. Lambeau Field.
