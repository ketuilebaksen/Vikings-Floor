VIKINGS GET DOUBLE DOSE OF HUGE NEWS

Eight sacks. Four takeaways. A 30-10 massacre at Lambeau Field.

After Kirk Cousins saved Minnesota’s offense in just five days, Brian Flores’s defense is ripping hearts out of chests.

And now the national media is starting to say what Vikings fans have been feeling all along. This team might be the real deal.

But can the 7-2 Vikings survive the 8-1 San Francisco 49ers in Mexico City on Sunday night?

Today, we’ve got a double dose of huge news. One historic stat that connects this defense to two Super Bowl champions. And one path to the NFC North crown that nobody else in the division has.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s get into it.

Let’s start with how the national media is reacting, because the tone has completely changed in one week.

On one national show, the hosts were asked to name their top two teams in football.

And one of them put the Minnesota Vikings at No. 2.

His reasoning? The defense. He called it scary. Scary as heck. He said Brian Flores is going to blitz all day, every day. He’s coming at you.

And he said something that sums up this team perfectly. Defense wins championships. Points win games.

With Kirk Cousins, he said, Minnesota can now put up a decent amount of points. But if there’s any defense in the NFL this year that can shut you down and rip your heart right out of your chest, it’s the Minnesota Vikings.

That’s why he had them at No. 2.

His co-host made a point that might be even more important.

He said what Kirk Cousins did in five or six days, the other two quarterbacks couldn’t do in six months.

That doesn’t mean Minnesota is guaranteed a Super Bowl because of Cousins. But it tells you the Vikings have a real chance now.

And then came the line that got everyone laughing.

We’ve talked about the layups. The throws the quarterbacks kept missing. But he said the previous quarterbacks weren’t just missing layups. They were missing dunks, right underneath the rim. The ball was going in the hoop and still coming out.

Steve Smith Sr. made a similar point on Get Up. While Kevin O’Connell was scheming up wide-open throws, Minnesota’s quarterbacks spent half a year missing them.

And here’s the part people keep forgetting. Cousins did all of this without two of his best playmakers.

No Jordan Addison, who’s now on injured reserve after a freak injury in a Friday throwing session before the Green Bay game.

No T.J. Hockenson.

Some of Minnesota’s best weapons will be back. Addison is out for a little while on IR, but Hockenson’s return could come sooner.

As one analyst put it, what you want if you’re Minnesota is an offense that can support your elite defense. And Kirk Cousins lets them believe they have one.

And once Cousins gave Minnesota that offensive spark, Brian Flores unleashed something at Lambeau Field that hadn’t happened there in 37 years.

On another show, the “stunner of the week” was the Vikings going into Lambeau Field as three-point underdogs, and blowing out the Packers by 20.

Eight sacks. Four takeaways.

And here’s the historic part. That’s the first time an opposing team has done that at Lambeau Field since 1989.

Since 1989. Thirty-seven years.

The analyst said Kirk Cousins got the offense in the right place, and that this team is going to have something to say about who comes out of the NFC.

Another national ranking had the Vikings at No. 7 and said simply: they’re back. In a big way.

And that brings us to huge news number one: the historic stat that connects this defense to two Super Bowl champions.

Danny Parkins dropped some numbers on First Things First that should terrify every contender in the league.

First: over the last three seasons, whenever the Vikings get just league-average quarterback play, their record is 19-2.

Nineteen and two.

Remember, before the Green Bay game, that number was 18-2. Cousins delivered average-or-better quarterback play at Lambeau, and it became 19-2.

This team doesn’t need a superstar quarterback. It needs average. And now it has better than average.

But the second stat is the one that really jumps off the page.

Through 10 weeks of the 2026 season, Minnesota is holding opponents to 14 or fewer points per game, while allowing fewer than 175 passing yards per game.

Fourteen or fewer points. Fewer than 175 passing yards.

And since the turn of the century, only two other defenses in NFL history have posted those numbers over a season.

The 2002 Tampa Bay Buccaneers.

And the 2008 Pittsburgh Steelers.

And what did both of those teams do?

They won the Super Bowl.

Let that sink in.

The 2002 Bucs, with Warren Sapp, Derrick Brooks, John Lynch, and Ronde Barber. One of the greatest defenses ever assembled. They won Super Bowl 37.

The 2008 Steelers, with Troy Polamalu, James Harrison, and James Farrior. They won Super Bowl 43.

And now, the 2026 Minnesota Vikings are in that same company.

That’s huge news number one.

And it’s not just the numbers. Former players see it on film too.

Chris Canty and Jeff Saturday were asked which elite defense from NFL history this Vikings defense reminds them of.

The answer? The 2000s Pittsburgh Steelers. The team that went to three Super Bowls in a span of about half a decade.

And they explained why.

This defense is multiple. It can mix and match between zone pressures and man blitzes. It has a lot of different things it can do, and a lot of different players filling very specific roles.

Canty brought up Troy Polamalu playing in the back of that Steelers defense. And while he was careful not to directly compare the two, he pointed to Harrison Smith’s ability to read and decipher what offenses are doing.

And Saturday made a great point. A lot of people think those Steelers defenses were just physical. They weren’t. They were intelligent. Everyone played a role. They didn’t have a bunch of players you were afraid of individually. But put them together, and they were a nightmare.

Saturday said he played against them a number of times. He knows.

That’s exactly what this Vikings defense is. Smart. Disciplined. Every player knowing his job. And Brian Flores orchestrating it all.

Jeff Saturday was also impressed by Kirk Cousins.

He said he doesn’t care how familiar Cousins was with the system before. Cousins has been in two other offenses since leaving Minnesota.

To come back with five days to learn all the terminology, all the expectations, all the reads, and get them right? Saturday said that’s hard to do.

He said people are going to try to minimize what Cousins did. But it wasn’t easy. Green Bay had been beating teams. And Cousins went in and showed up the way he did.

Saturday’s verdict? He appreciates that Cousins had been in the offense before. But it was still special.

Now let’s look at exactly how this defense dismantled Green Bay, because the details are incredible.

Knowing his offense could finally keep up on the scoreboard, Flores cranked his season-long blitz rate, around 60 percent, up to 67 percent at Lambeau.

He sent extra rushers on 25 of Jordan Love’s 37 dropbacks.

Twenty-five of 37.

And here’s the most impressive part. Seven different Vikings defenders recorded a sack.

Seven.

Levi Drake Rodriguez led the way with two, collapsing the pocket from the inside.

And then Dallas Turner. Andrew Van Ginkel. Kyle Van Noy. Eric Wilson. Jalen Redmond. And Elijah Williams. All of them took turns putting Jordan Love on the ground.

That’s exactly what Jeff Saturday was talking about. It’s not one superstar pass rusher doing everything. It’s a whole system. Everybody contributing. Everybody playing their role.

And on the back end, Harrison Smith disguised coverages and picked off a pass. At 37 years old, the Hitman is still making plays.

Remember Kirk Cousins’s point after the game. He said there was going to be a lot of talk about the offense and his addition, but it’s the defense. They played lights out and kept giving the offense the ball back.

And remember why the defense could play that way. One analyst put it perfectly: once Cousins gave Minnesota a functioning offense, he took the handcuffs off Brian Flores.

For weeks, this defense felt like it had to be perfect, because the offense couldn’t score. Now it could take risks. It could blitz more. It could attack. Because if something went wrong, the offense could answer.

That’s huge news number one. A historic defense, finally unleashed.

Now let’s get to huge news number two: the cleanest path to the NFC North crown.

And this is where it gets terrifying for the rest of the NFC.

The NFC North has been the toughest division in football. According to the analysis, it’s the best division since 2002, with a combined record of 27-10.

Twenty-seven and 10. Four good teams. Nobody’s an easy out.

But when you look at the remaining schedule, the Vikings have the clearest path to the division title.

Nick Wright explained why.

First, Minnesota already beat the Bears. Remember, back on September 20th, the Vikings won an ugly 9-3 rock fight against Chicago, with Carson Wentz at quarterback. Ugly, but a win. And that win gives Minnesota the tiebreaker over Chicago.

Second, the Vikings only have two division games left.

Third, the final game against the Bears in Week 18 might not even matter by then, depending on how the standings shake out.

And fourth, there’s a break on the schedule. Minnesota gets the Patriots in Week 14, and New England will be without its injured quarterback, Drake Maye.

Wright was honest. He said he thinks the Bears are slightly better than Minnesota. But he said the Vikings have by far the cleanest path to winning the NFC North, because they beat the Bears and only have two division games left.

His conclusion? The Vikings are the correct favorite to win the division.

He also made a great point about the Bears. Their schedule won’t be a track meet against a Brian Flores defense.

Now let’s look at the rest of the division, because the picture has changed a lot.

The Detroit Lions have been hit by devastating season-ending injuries to Aidan Hutchinson and Jack Campbell.

Remember, Campbell was the linebacker who was taken off the field on a stretcher during the Vikings-Lions game earlier this month. Our thoughts are still with him. But losing both Hutchinson and Campbell for the season is a massive blow for Detroit’s defense.

And remember, Detroit holds a tiebreaker over Minnesota, because the Lions beat the Vikings 19-15 back in Week 8. But those injuries could change the course of their season.

The Green Bay Packers are 6-4 and buried at 1-3 in the division after back-to-back losses to Minnesota.

And the Chicago Bears are 7-2, but they face the hardest remaining schedule in the league.

Meanwhile, Minnesota sits at 7-2, with a 3-1 division record. Wins over the Packers twice, and the Bears. The only division loss came in Detroit.

That’s huge news number two. The Vikings control their own destiny, with the easiest path of any team in the division.

But before Minnesota can start thinking about division banners, it has to survive a massive test on Sunday Night Football.

This Sunday, November 22nd, in Mexico City.

Brian Flores’s second-ranked defense against Kyle Shanahan, Brock Purdy, Christian McCaffrey, and the 8-1 San Francisco 49ers.

And this 49ers team leads the NFL in offense. It just put up 46 points and 584 yards against Dallas.

Five hundred eighty-four yards in a single game.

That’s the ultimate strength-on-strength matchup. The league’s most dangerous offense against one of the most dominant defenses of this century.

Nick Wright summed up what’s at stake. If the Vikings go to Mexico City and it looks anything like what we just saw at Lambeau Field, the rest of the NFC, and the rest of the NFL, is going to be on notice.

And remember, Lambeau was without Jordan Addison and without T.J. Hockenson. The Vikings still scored 30 for only the second time all season.

If they can go to Mexico City and put up 30-plus against San Francisco, while the defense keeps playing at this level, Wright said a lot of people, him included, would consider them the best team in the league.

So here are the big questions for Sunday.

Can Christian McCaffrey and Deebo Samuel find creases in Minnesota’s blitz-heavy front? Flores is sending pressure on two-thirds of dropbacks. If McCaffrey breaks one big run against an overcommitted front, that’s how you punish an aggressive defense.

Can Brock Purdy handle the pressure? He’s playing as well as anyone in the league, according to O’Connell. But he hasn’t seen a pass rush like this.

Can Kirk Cousins beat the team that intercepted him three times when he was a Raider? He’s had time to study what went wrong in that 38-7 loss.

And can the Vikings handle the altitude in Mexico City? Remember, the 49ers trained in Colorado to adjust. The Vikings stayed home.

So let’s put it all together.

Huge news number one: Minnesota’s defense is allowing 14 or fewer points and fewer than 175 passing yards per game. Since 2000, only the 2002 Buccaneers and the 2008 Steelers have done that, and both won the Super Bowl. And with even league-average quarterback play over the last three seasons, the Vikings are 19-2.

Huge news number two: Minnesota has the cleanest path to the NFC North title. A 3-1 division record, a tiebreaker over Chicago, only two division games left, and a Week 14 game against a Patriots team without Drake Maye.

The national media has noticed. Ranked No. 2 by one analyst. Compared to the 2000s Steelers by Chris Canty and Jeff Saturday. The first team in 37 years to record eight sacks and four takeaways at Lambeau.

And now, the 8-1 49ers.
