But are we really doing enough to take it all the way? Because what happens over the next two weeks could change everything.

And this conversation is about to get a lot more interesting.

Welcome back to Vikings Floor. Let’s get into it.

Something is happening across the NFC that should have every Vikings fan paying very close attention.

While two of the conference’s biggest contenders make aggressive moves to strengthen their rosters, Minnesota is standing at a crossroads that could define its whole season.

And here’s what makes it even more intriguing. The Vikings may still have a chance to land a player who could transform their offense. But that chance could disappear before Nolan Teasley decides to pull the trigger.

We’re talking about Jonathan Taylor, the Indianapolis Colts’ star running back.

After what just happened with Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner, the pressure on Minnesota’s front office is on a completely different level.

Think about what just happened.

The Colts traded Quenton Nelson to the Seattle Seahawks.

Shortly after that, DeForest Buckner was sent back to the San Francisco 49ers.

Two established players. Two major NFC competitors getting stronger. And two opportunities that went by without Minnesota making a deal.

Remember, both of those names came up in the Vikings’ trade conversation earlier this month. Nelson was the guard who could’ve filled Donovan Jackson’s spot next to Christian Darrisaw. Buckner was the interior disruptor who could’ve made the Flores defense even scarier.

Now they’re both suiting up for NFC rivals.

And look at the prices.

Seattle got Nelson for a 2027 third-round pick and a 2028 fifth-rounder.

San Francisco brought Buckner back for a 2027 fourth-round pick.

Those weren’t impossible packages. A third and a fifth. A fourth. That’s not mortgaging the future. That’s the kind of deal a contender makes without thinking twice.

That’s exactly why this deserves attention. The Vikings have draft assets of their own. Remember, Minnesota has two third-round picks and multiple fifth-rounders in 2027.

So the question around this organization is getting harder to ignore. If Minnesota truly believes it can compete for a Super Bowl this season, how aggressive is it willing to be?

There’s another interesting detail involving Seattle.

Nolan Teasley came to Minnesota after spending years with the Seahawks. And because of his departure, Seattle is set to receive a third-round compensatory pick under the NFL’s minority hiring development program.

So now the Seahawks have a third-round pick tied to Teasley’s hiring, and they’ve added Quenton Nelson to an already competitive roster.

That doesn’t mean Minnesota made a mistake hiring Teasley. Not at all. But it adds another layer to a story that’s getting uncomfortable for Vikings fans watching their conference rivals get better.

And it brings us right back to Jonathan Taylor, because his situation could become the most important Vikings storyline before the November 10th trade deadline.

So what are the insiders saying?

ESPN insider Adam Schefter explained on The Pat McAfee Show that the Colts don’t want to trade Taylor.

Schefter’s understanding was that Indianapolis would prefer to keep its star running back, and he said he didn’t expect a trade based on what he knew at that point.

That lines up with what we heard earlier this week from Jonathan Jones of The Athletic: an executive told him that ownership loves Taylor.

But NFL Network insider Tom Pelissero had reported something else. He said no Colts player was expected to be completely off limits if Indianapolis decided to listen to offers.

So you have two very different perspectives.

On one side, Indianapolis has real reasons to keep a foundational offensive player. He’s their best player, ownership loves him, and he’s signed long term.

On the other side, the organization has already shown it’s willing to move major names to collect future draft capital. Quenton Nelson is gone. DeForest Buckner is gone. Those were cornerstone players too.

That doesn’t mean Taylor is available, and there’s no confirmed agreement involving Minnesota. But it explains why this possibility is getting so much attention.

And here’s where the conversation gets serious for the Vikings.

This team isn’t struggling because the whole roster needs rebuilding. It’s the opposite.

Minnesota has built an identity around outstanding defense, exceptional special teams, and a coaching staff that keeps finding ways to win even though the offense hasn’t matched the other two units.

Look at the efficiency numbers.

Through Week 7, the figures discussed in the report put the Vikings’ defense second in DVOA. Special teams ranked first.

The offense? Thirtieth.

Let that sink in. A team playing at an elite level in two phases of football, and getting some of the league’s weakest efficiency from the third.

And yet, Minnesota is still unbeaten.

That tells you two things. One, the defense and special teams are incredible. And two, imagine what this team could be if the offense were even average.

Kevin O’Connell has clearly committed to running the football.

In the numbers presented, Minnesota ranked second in rushing play percentage and third in rushing attempts per game.

So the commitment is there. O’Connell wants to run the ball, control the clock, and protect his defense.

But the results haven’t matched the commitment.

The Vikings ranked 22nd in yards per carry. Twelfth in rushing yards per game. And 27th in rushing touchdowns per game, averaging roughly half a rushing touchdown per game.

Half a touchdown per game, on one of the most run-heavy offenses in football.

In other words, Minnesota is investing heavily in the ground game without getting elite production back.

Jordan Mason has contributed since his return. Aaron Jones is still an important option, though he’s dealing with that knee and the MRI we talked about earlier this week.

But the real question is whether that combination gives Minnesota the explosive, game-changing rushing threat it may need once the competition gets a lot tougher in January.

Picture a playoff game in Seattle. Or San Francisco.

Two teams that just got stronger by adding Nelson and Buckner.

The defense is keeping Minnesota within striking distance. The special teams unit is doing its job. And the offense needs one player who can turn a tough situation into a huge gain.

That’s the scenario driving the argument for Jonathan Taylor.

A running back who can put up 100 or 125 yards in a high-pressure postseason game could change how Minnesota approaches those games.

And remember, Taylor ran for 95 yards and a touchdown against this exact Vikings defense last Sunday, including a 38-yard touchdown run. If he can do that against the best defense in football, imagine what he could do for Minnesota.

Taylor’s presence wouldn’t automatically fix the offensive line’s problems. And getting him would require a serious investment. But that potential impact is exactly why his name deserves consideration.

And the biggest question might not be whether Taylor fits this offense. It might be whether Nolan Teasley is willing to give up valuable future draft picks for a running back in his late 20s when the Vikings are already undefeated.

That’s the decision hanging over Minnesota right now.

There’s an important financial detail too, because Taylor’s contract might make a trade more manageable than a lot of fans expect.

Now here’s where the Taylor situation gets even more interesting. The financial numbers suggest Minnesota might have a path to making this move without blowing up its roster.

According to the figures in the report, the team acquiring Taylor would take on about $5.5 million for the current season, including $4.8 million guaranteed.

His projected contract numbers then rise to $16 million in 2027 and $17.9 million in 2028.

So for this season, the hit is manageable. It’s the future years where the real commitment shows up.

But there’s a catch. Minnesota reportedly has about $4.6 million in available cap space, which ranks 26th in the NFL.

So $5.5 million coming in, and $4.6 million available. The math doesn’t quite work as it stands.

That means adjustments would be needed. But restructuring an existing contract could create extra flexibility. NFL front offices do it all the time.

The bigger obstacle might be the trade compensation Indianapolis demands.

And here’s something Vikings fans should think about.

After watching Quenton Nelson and DeForest Buckner get moved for fairly modest draft packages, could Jonathan Taylor end up being available for less than people expected?

Nobody knows what Indianapolis would accept. But the possibilities being discussed include combinations of second-, third-, and fourth-round picks.

If the Colts are in sell mode, and if their asking prices for Nelson and Buckner are any guide, the price for Taylor might not be as crazy as some of the bigger proposals suggested.

Still, Taylor isn’t Minnesota’s only option. Not by a long shot.

Tony Pollard of the Tennessee Titans is an intriguing alternative.

He’s coming off a performance with 130 rushing yards and three touchdowns. That’s a back who’s playing well right now.

Pollard could strengthen Minnesota’s backfield without needing the same investment as Taylor. A fifth- or sixth-round pick was suggested as a possible offer, though there’s no confirmation Tennessee would accept that.

A fifth or a sixth for a back coming off 130 yards and three scores? That’s the kind of value move that could make a real difference without touching the premium picks.

Then there’s Khalil Mack.

Minnesota’s pass rush has already gotten strong contributions from Dallas Turner and Kyle Van Noy. But adding another established veteran could make an already dangerous defensive front even scarier.

And think about the bonus. Mack could be a valuable mentor for Dallas Turner, a young edge rusher still developing into a star. Learning from one of the best pass rushers of his generation, in the same meeting room, every single day.

And there’s a strategic angle. Getting Mack could also keep another NFC North rival, like Chicago, from getting him first.

Then there’s Budda Baker.

We covered him earlier this month in the safety discussion, and his name is still around. His versatility in the secondary could give Minnesota more flexibility, especially with Harrison Smith at 37.

But giving up a third-round pick for a safety would raise questions about whether Minnesota should be prioritizing its offense instead. When your offense ranks 30th in DVOA and your defense ranks second, where should your resources go?

And maybe the most surprising name mentioned is Jalen Carter.

With Philadelphia struggling in the scenario being described, the idea of landing a young defensive tackle with elite potential is fascinating.

Picture Jalen Carter alongside Jalen Redmond and Levi Drake Rodriguez. That could give Minnesota an intimidating defensive interior for years.

But this is still speculation, not a confirmed negotiation.

There’s also an important development involving Jauan Jennings.

According to the report attributed to Adam Schefter, other teams have contacted Minnesota about the wide receiver, but the Vikings haven’t actively been shopping him.

Tom Pelissero had shared a similar message earlier.

A fifth-round pick was suggested as compensation that might convince Minnesota to consider moving Jennings. But nothing indicates any offer has been accepted.

So for now, his future is still uncertain. Remember, he has just two catches for nine yards this season. Teams calling about him tells you the league still sees value in him, even if it hasn’t shown up in Minnesota yet.

And while all these trade possibilities keep generating discussion, a new controversy has popped up.

Colin Cowherd once again left the undefeated Vikings out of his top 10 NFL teams heading into Week 8.

Out of the top 10. The only undefeated team in the league.

Meanwhile, Seattle was No. 1 on his list, despite a 4-3 record. San Francisco was No. 2.

A 4-3 team at No. 1, and a 6-0 team outside the top 10.

That raises a real question. Why does an undefeated team with one of the league’s best defenses keep struggling to get recognition?

Maybe Minnesota’s offensive limitations are shaping those rankings. The 30th-ranked offense. The field goals. The lack of explosive plays. Some analysts look at that and see a team waiting to be exposed.

But wins matter. And the Vikings keep finding ways to get them.

At the end of the season, nobody asks how pretty your wins were. They ask how many you had.

And now comes a potentially defining NFC North matchup against the Detroit Lions.

It’s another chance for Minnesota to show exactly where it belongs among the NFL’s best. Another chance to prove the doubters wrong.

And remember, Detroit came into this week ranked among the worst total defenses in the league. If there was ever a game for this offense to break out, this is it.

A big offensive performance against the Lions wouldn’t just be a win. It would be a statement to Colin Cowherd and every other critic who keeps leaving this team out.

The stakes couldn’t be clearer.

The Vikings have the defense. They have the special teams. And they have the opportunity.

What’s still uncertain is whether the front office will make the aggressive move that could lift these championship hopes to another level.

Because while Minnesota waits, Seattle got Quenton Nelson. San Francisco got DeForest Buckner. The NFC is loading up.

And at some point, standing still becomes its own decision.
